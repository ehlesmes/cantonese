In this chapter, we will learn about **Describing Symptoms & Illnesses**.

In daily life, being able to accurately describe how you feel when you are sick
is essential. Spoken Cantonese uses direct and highly descriptive terms for
physical ailments.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Describing Symptoms & Illnesses

Spoken Cantonese uses direct terms for physical ailments:

- `病[bing6|to be sick / illness / disease]`: To be sick or have an illness.
- `唔舒服[m4syu1fuk6|uncomfortable / unwell / sick]`: Unwell, sick, or feeling
  physically uncomfortable.
- `發燒[faat3siu1|to have a fever]`: To run a fever.
- `感冒[gam2mou6|common cold / to catch a cold]`: A common cold.
- `咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]`:
  Completed aspect marker.
- `中[zung3|to hit / catch (an illness)]` `咗[zo2|completed]`: To catch an
  illness or get infected (e.g. `中[zung3|catch]` `咗[zo2|completed]`
  `感冒[gam2mou6|cold]`).
- `咳[kat1|to cough / cough]`: To cough, or a cough itself.
- `流鼻水[lau4bei6seoi2|to have a runny nose]`: Literally "flowing nose water,"
  meaning to have a runny nose.
- `喉嚨痛[hau4lung4tung3|sore throat]`: A sore throat.
- `頭痛[tau4tung3|headache]`: A headache.
- `肚痛[tou5tung3|stomach ache / abdominal pain]`: A stomach ache.
- `頭暈[tau4wan4|dizzy / lightheaded]`: Feeling dizzy or lightheaded.
- `痾[o1|to have diarrhea / excrete]`: Colloquial term meaning to have diarrhea.
- `嘔[au2|to vomit / throw up]`: To vomit or throw up.
- `腸胃炎[coeng4wai6jim4|gastroenteritis / stomach flu]`: Gastroenteritis or
  stomach flu.
- `熱氣[jit6hei3|internal heat / inflammation]`: "Yeet hay," a traditional
  concept of internal heat or inflammation.
- `嚴重[jim4cung5|serious / severe / grave]`: Serious or severe.
- `傳染[cyun4jim5|to infect / infectious / contagious]`: To infect, or to be
  contagious.
- `係咁[hai6gam2|continuously / constantly]`: Continuously, constantly, keep
  doing something.
  - _Example_: `我[ngo5|I / me]` `今日[gam1jat6|today]`
    `係咁[hai6gam2|continuously]` `咳[kat1|to cough]`
    `呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]`！:
    I keep coughing continuously today!

## 2. Situational Dialogue: Feeling Unwell

A friend (A) asks another friend (B) about their health.

```dialog
A: 你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 點呀[dim2aa3|how is it? / how are things? / what's up?]？係咪[hai6mai6|is or not (A-not-A question form)] 唔舒服[m4syu1fuk6|uncomfortable / unwell / sick] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === How are you doing today? Are you feeling unwell?
B: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]，我[ngo5|I / me] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 係咁[hai6gam2|continuously / constantly] 咳[kat1|to cough / cough] 同埋[tung4maai4|and / with / together with] 流鼻水[lau4bei6seoi2|to have a runny nose] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，好似[hou2ci5|looks like / seems] 中[zung3|to catch] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 感冒[gam2mou6|common cold / to catch a cold] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === Yes, I keep coughing and having a runny nose continuously today, looks like I caught a cold.
A: 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 你[nei5|you] 有冇[jau5mou5|have or not (A-not-A question form)] 發燒[faat3siu1|to run a fever] 同埋[tung4maai4|and / with / together with] 喉嚨痛[hau4lung4tung3|sore throat] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Then do you have a fever and sore throat?
B: 有[jau5|to have] 少少[siu2siu2|a little bit / slightly] 頭痛[tau4tung3|headache]，但係[daan6hai6|but] 冇[mou5|to not have / there is not] 發燒[faat3siu1|to have a fever]。
   === I have a slight headache, but no fever.
```

## 3. Exercises

Test your understanding of Cantonese symptom descriptions and illness terms.

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following refers to gastroenteritis / stomach flu in Cantonese?
  A) 感冒[gam2mou6|common cold]
  B) 腸胃炎[coeng4wai6jim4|gastroenteritis]
  C) 喉嚨痛[hau4lung4tung3|sore throat]
  D) 熱氣[jit6hei3|internal heat]
answer: B
explanation: |
  `腸胃炎[coeng4wai6jim4|gastroenteritis / stomach flu]` is the standard medical and colloquial term for gastroenteritis.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank to say "I keep coughing continuously today" in Cantonese:
  我[ngo5|I / me] 今日[gam1jat6|today] ________ 咳[kat1|to cough] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
answer: 係咁[hai6gam2|continuously / constantly]
explanation: |
  `係咁[hai6gam2|continuously / constantly]` is a high-frequency colloquial adverb meaning "constantly" or "keep doing something".
```

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into Cantonese with correct semantic annotations:
  "I have a fever and sore throat today."
answer: 我[ngo5|I / me] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 發燒[faat3siu1|to have a fever] 同埋[tung4maai4|and / with / together with] 喉嚨痛[hau4lung4tung3|sore throat] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
explanation: |
  `我[ngo5|I / me]` is "I", `今日[gam1jat6|today]` is "today", `發燒[faat3siu1|to have a fever]` is "have a fever", `同埋[tung4maai4|and]` is "and", and `喉嚨痛[hau4lung4tung3|sore throat]` is "sore throat".
```
