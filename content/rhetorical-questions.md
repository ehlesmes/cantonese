In this lesson, we will dive into a cornerstone of conversational fluency:
**Rhetorical Questions & Spoken Doubts**. Native Cantonese speakers rely heavily
on rhetorical patterns to express sarcasm, correct misunderstandings, show
surprise, or declare firm disbelief.

To converse like a local, you must learn to structure doubts naturally rather
than relying on dry, literal translations. We will master four key grammar
patterns:

1. **Rhetorical Sarcasm & Disbelief**: Using the
   `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]` ... `咩[me1|particle]` frame.
2. **Counter-Expectation & Surprise**: Using the `乜[mat1|how / why]` ...
   `咩[me1|particle]` frame to express shock or question an assumption.
3. **Rhetorical Defiance**: Using `點會[dim2wui5|how could / how can]` to state
   that something is highly improbable or impossible.
4. **Alternative Doubt**: Expressing options of truth or falsehood using
   `真[zan1|real / true]` and `假[gaa2|fake / false]` coordinated by
   `定[ding6|or]`.

Let's explore these patterns to elevate your spoken Cantonese.

---

## 1. Rhetorical Sarcasm & Disbelief: `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]` ... `咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]`

When you want to convey a rhetorical "Don't tell me that..." or "Could it be
that...?", Cantonese uses the paired structure: `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]`
at the beginning of the clause and the doubt particle
`咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]` at the very end. This
structure expects a negative answer and often carries a tone of disbelief or
mild sarcasm.

- **Structure**: `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]` + `[Statement]` +
  `咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]` + `？`

```cantonese
唔通[m4tung1|could it be that] 你[nei5|you] 信[seon3|believe] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 咩[me1|particle]？
===
Don't tell me you believe him? / Could it be that you believe him?
```

```cantonese
唔通[m4tung1|could it be that] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 請[ceng2|treat] 我哋[ngo5dei6|us] 飲[jam2|drink] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee] 咩[me1|particle]？
===
Could it be that the boss is treating us to coffee? (Implies: Absolutely not, he is too stingy!)
```

## 2. Counter-Expectation & Surprise: `乜[mat1|how / why / rhetorical prefix]` ... `咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]`

To express shock, surprise, or to correct a false assumption (similar to saying
"Wait, is it not the case that...?" or "What? I thought you..."), Cantonese uses
`乜[mat1|how / why / rhetorical prefix]` at the start of a sentence paired with
`咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]` at the end.

- **Structure**: `乜[mat1|how / why / rhetorical prefix]` + `[Subject]` +
  `[Verb/Adjective]` + `咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]` +
  `？`

```cantonese
乜[mat1|how] 你[nei5|you] 唔[m4|not] 知[zi1|know] 咩[me1|particle]？
===
What? You didn't know?
```

```cantonese
乜[mat1|how] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔[m4|not] 去[heoi3|go] 咩[me1|particle]？
===
Wait, is he not going? / What, he is not going?
```

> [!NOTE] While `咩[me1|particle]` is used in both
> `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]` ...
> `咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]` and
> `乜[mat1|how / why / rhetorical prefix]` ...
> `咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]` structures,
> `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]` highlights disbelief/sarcasm, whereas
> `乜[mat1|how / why / rhetorical prefix]` highlights surprise and a correction
> of assumptions.
>
> Also note that in fast, colloquial speech, the sentence-final particle
> `咩[me1|particle]` is occasionally dropped since `唔通[m4tung1|could it be]`
> and `乜[mat1|what]` already set the rhetorical tone. However, it is highly
> recommended to keep `咩[me1|interrogative particle]` as a beginner.

## 3. Rhetorical Defiance: `點會[dim2wui5|how could / how can]`

To firmly declare that an action or state is impossible ("How could...?"), use
the rhetorical pronoun `點會[dim2wui5|how could / how can]` directly before the
verb or adjective.

- **Structure**: `[Subject]` + `點會[dim2wui5|how could / how can]` +
  `[Verb/Adjective]` + `㗎[gaa3|particle]` / `呀[aa3|particle]` + `？`

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 點會[dim2wui5|how could] 錯[co3|wrong] 呀[aa3|particle]？
===
How could he be wrong?
```

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 點會[dim2wui5|how could] 唔[m4|not] 信[seon3|believe] 我[ngo5|me] 呀[aa3|particle]？
===
How could you not believe me?
```

A very common standalone phrase is combining `點會[dim2wui5|how could]` with
`咁[gam3|so / like this]`:

```cantonese
點會[dim2wui5|how could] 咁[gam3|so / like this] 㗎[gaa3|particle]？
===
How could it be like this?
```

## 4. Alternative Doubt: `真[zan1|real / true]` vs `假[gaa2|fake / false]` with `定[ding6|or]`

To express skepticism about the validity of a statement or piece of news, use
the question conjunction `定[ding6|or]` to coordinate `真[zan1|real / true]` and
`假[gaa2|fake / false]`.

- **Structure**: `[Statement/Noun]` + `係[hai6|to be]` +
  `真[zan1|real / true]` + `定[ding6|or]` + `假[gaa2|fake / false]` +
  `㗎[gaa3|particle]` + `？`

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 講[gong2|speak] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 嘢[je5|thing / stuff] 係[hai6|to be] 真[zan1|real / true] 定[ding6|or] 假[gaa2|fake / false] 㗎[gaa3|particle]？
===
Is what he says true or fake?
```

```cantonese
呢[ni1|this] 件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items] 衫[saam1|clothes] 係[hai6|to be] 真[zan1|real / true] 定[ding6|or] 假[gaa2|fake / false] 㗎[gaa3|particle]？
===
Is this piece of clothing real or fake? (E.g. brand authenticity)
```

This is fully registered and highly authentic!

## 5. Situational Dialogue: Coffee Rumors at the Office

Wing-sum (A) and Wing-kit (B) discuss a rumor around the office regarding the
boss (`老細[lou5sai3|boss]`) treating the team to coffee. Wing-kit is highly
skeptical.

```dialog
A: 我[ngo5|I] 聽講[ting1gong2|to hear of / be told] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 返工[faan1gung1|at work] 飲[jam2|to drink] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee] 唔[m4|not] 使[sai2|need] 俾[bei2|to give] 錢[cin2|money] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === I heard that tomorrow we don't need to pay money to drink coffee at work!
B: 點會[dim2wui5|how could] 呀[aa3|particle]？ 邊個[bin1go3|who] 講[gong2|speak] 㗎[gaa3|particle]？
   === How could that be? Who said that?
A: 係[hai6|to be] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 講[gong2|speak] 嘅[ge3|particle]！
   === The boss said it!
B: 唔通[m4tung1|could it be that] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 請[ceng2|treat] 我哋[ngo5dei6|us] 飲[jam2|drink] 咩[me1|particle]？
   === Could it be that the boss is treating us to drinks?
A: 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 話[waa6|say] 係[hai6|to be] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 可能[ho2nang4|maybe] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 開心[hoi1sam1|happy] 啦[laa1|particle]。
   === The boss said yes! Maybe he is happy today.
B: 乜[mat1|how] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to] 開心[hoi1sam1|happy] 嘅[ge3|particle] 咩[me1|particle]？ 我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 信[seon3|believe] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === What, he will be happy? I don't believe it!
A: 你[nei5|you] 去[heoi3|go] 問[man6|ask] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 啦[laa1|particle]！ 我[ngo5|I] 冇[mou5|have not] 講[gong2|speak] 錯[co3|wrong] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === You go ask him! I didn't say it wrong!
B: 好[hou2|good] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 如果[jyu4gwo2|if] 係[hai6|to be] 假[gaa2|fake / false] 嘅[ge3|particle]， 你[nei5|you] 請[ceng2|treat] 我[ngo5|me] 食[sik6|to eat] 菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun] 啦[laa1|particle]！
   === Great! If it is fake, you treat me to eat pineapple buns!
A: 冇[mou5|have not] 問題[man6tai4|problem]！ 真[zan1|real / true] 嘅[ge3|particle] 就[zau6|then] 得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree] 啦[laa1|particle]！
   === No problem! If it's true, then it's fine!
```

  ________ 你[nei5|you] 唔[m4|not] 去[heoi3|go] ________？
answer: 乜[mat1|how / why / rhetorical prefix], 咩[me1|rhetorical final particle indicating doubt]
explanation: |
  The counter-expectation surprise frame is `乜[mat1|how / why] + [Statement] + 咩[me1|particle]？`.

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following correctly describes the difference between 點會[dim2wui5|how could] and 唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]?
  A) 點會[dim2wui5|how could] is placed before a verb to denote impossibility, whereas 唔通[m4tung1|could it be that] initiates the sentence and pairs with 咩[me1|particle].
  B) 點會[dim2wui5|how could] is only used for positive questions, while 唔通[m4tung1|could it be that] is only used for negative questions.
  C) Both occupy the exact same syntactic slot and are interchangeable without changing the particles.
answer: A
explanation: |
  `點會[dim2wui5|how could]` acts as an adverb directly modifying verbs/adjectives within a clause (e.g. `點會[dim2wui5|how could]` `錯[co3|wrong]`), while `唔通[m4tung1|could it be that]` is a sentence-initial conjunction that pairs with the sentence-final particle `咩[me1|particle]`.
```
