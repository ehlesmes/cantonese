In this chapter, we will explore some of the most widely used and colorful
Cantonese **Native Idioms**
(`俗語[zuk1jyu5|colloquial expression / slang / idiom]`).

Many of these idioms are structurally split-verbs, which means they follow the
exact same grammatical rules of separation we learned in the previous chapter.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Spoken Cantonese Native Idioms

Many of the most popular colloquial expressions used by Hong Kongers in daily
life are structurally split-verbs. Let's study five of the most expressive
idioms:

### 1. Chatting & Gossiping: `吹水[ceoi1seoi2|to chat / shoot the breeze / gossip]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Blowing water"
- **Colloquial usage**: Chattering, hanging out, or gossiping casually.
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 茶餐廳[caa4caan1teng1|Hong Kong style café / diner] 吹[ceoi1|to blow / boast] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 水[seoi2|water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
We are shooting the breeze at the Hong Kong style café.
```

### 2. Pretending or Showing Off: `扮嘢[baan6je5|to pretend / show off / act]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Dressing up things"
- **Colloquial usage**: Pretending, showing off, playing dumb, or acting like
  someone you're not.
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 扮[baan6|to dress up / act / pretend] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 嘢[je5|thing / stuff / matter] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
===
He is really just pretending / showing off, obviously!
```

### 3. Standing Someone Up: `放飛機[fong3fei1gei1|to stand someone up / break a promise]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Releasing an airplane"
- **Colloquial usage**: Standing someone up, failing to show up to an agreed
  meeting or appointment.
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 又[jau6|again] 放[fong3|to release / let go / put] 我[ngo5|I / me] 飛機[fei1gei1|airplane] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
===
Hey, you stood me up again today! (Friendly or direct warning!)
```

### 4. Sitting Back and Watching Drama: `食花生[sik6faa1sang1|to sit back and watch drama / spectate]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Eating peanuts"
- **Colloquial usage**: Staying out of a conflict or dramatic situation, just
  sitting back to enjoy the drama unfold (like eating popcorn at a movie).
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 隔籬[gaak3lei4|next to / beside / neighbor] 食[sik6|to eat] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 花生[faa1sang1|peanut] 啫[ze1|sentence-final particle meaning 'only', 'just', or downplaying something]。
===
I am just sitting back eating peanuts (watching the drama unfold next to me).
```

### 5. Getting Fired: `炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired / lay off / fire someone]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Stir-frying squid" (historically, when employees were
  fired from shops in Hong Kong, they had to pack up their sleeping mats, which
  rolled up like a curled, stir-fried squid).
- **Colloquial usage**: Getting fired or firing someone.
- **Splitting example**: When you want to say "the boss fired him," you split
  the verb `炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired / lay off / fire someone]` and
  insert the pronoun object (`佢[keoi5|he / she / it]`) in the middle:

```cantonese
老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
===
Whoa, the boss fired him already!
```

### 6. Four-Character Idioms & Color Terms

Cantonese also uses `四[sei3|four]` `字[zi6|characters]`
`俗語[zuk1jyu5|idiomatic expressions]` and color-based idioms to express vivid
imagery:

- `人山人海[jan4saan1jan4hoi2|crowded / huge crowds of people]` (Mountains of
  people, seas of people): Used to describe a very crowded place.
- `黑面[hak1min6|to have a dark expression / angry face]` (Black face): To look
  visibly angry or annoyed.

```cantonese
旺角[wong6gok3|Mong Kok (place in Hong Kong)] 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 人山人海[jan4saan1jan4hoi2|crowded / huge crowds of people] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
Mong Kok is truly packed with people!
```

```cantonese
老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 黑[hak1|black / dark / bad luck] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 面[min6|face / surface] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
===
The boss is showing a dark/angry face right now! (Note how "black face" can split like a verb!)
```

## 2. Situational Dialogue: A Weekend Standup & Office Gossip

Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B) are talking on Saturday, gossiping about their
colleague David.

```dialog
A: Wing-kit，你[nei5|you] 知[zi1|to know / be aware of] 唔[m4|not / no (negative prefix)] 知道[zi1dou3|to know / be aware of] 今日[gam1jat6|today] David 放[fong3|to release / let go / put] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 飛機[fei1gei1|airplane] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Wing-kit, do you know that David stood us up today?
B: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]！ 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 話[waa6|to say / speak / talk] 一定[jat1ding6|certainly / definitely] 嚟[lei4|to come]，今日[gam1jat6|today] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 又[jau6|again / also] 放飛機[fong3fei1gei1|to stand someone up / break a promise] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
   === Yes! Yesterday he said he would definitely come, and today he stood us up again, obviously!
A: 我[ngo5|I / me] 話[waa6|to say / speak / talk] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive marker) / let] 你[nei5|you] 聽[teng1|to listen / hear] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 啱啱[aam1aam1|just now / recently] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] David 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
   === Let me tell you: the boss just fired David!
B: 哇[waa1|wow / whoa]！ 真係[zan1hai6|really] 㗎[gaa4|particle]？ 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 平時[ping4si4|normally / usually] 好[hou2|very / quite / extremely] 鍾意[zung1ji3|to like / love / prefer] 扮嘢[baan6je5|to pretend / show off / act] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！
   === Wow! Really? He normally likes to pretend/show off so much, obviously!
A: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]，今日[gam1jat6|today] David 又[jau6|again / also] 遲到[ci4dou3|to be late / arrive late]，老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 黑[hak1|black / dark / bad luck] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 面[min6|face / surface] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
   === Yes, David was late again today, the boss was showing a really dark/angry face!
B: 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 隔籬[gaak3lei4|next to / beside / neighbor] 食[sik6|to eat] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 花生[faa1sang1|peanut] 啫[ze1|sentence-final particle meaning 'only', 'just', or downplaying something]。
   === In that case, we are just sitting back next to him eating peanuts (watching the drama unfold).
```

## 3. Exercises

Test your understanding of Cantonese native idioms, color terms, and grammatical
splits.

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following is the correct Cantonese way to say "The boss fired him"?
  (Hint: Split-verb caau2jau4jyu2 with a pronoun object)
  A) 老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired / lay off / fire someone] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it]。
  B) 老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid]。
  C) 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid]。
answer: B
explanation: |
  `炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired / lay off / fire someone]` is an idiomatic split-verb. To say "the boss fired him," you split the verb and insert the pronoun object `佢[keoi5|he / she / it]` immediately after the verb part `炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire]` along with the completed aspect marker, giving `老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor]` `炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire]` `咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]` `佢[keoi5|he / she / it]` `魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid]`. Option C means "He fired the boss".
```

```exercise
answer: 吹[ceoi1|to blow / boast] / 水[seoi2|water]
explanation: |
  The idiom `吹水[ceoi1seoi2|to chat / shoot the breeze / gossip]` (literally "blowing water") is structurally a split-verb. To express continuous action, the marker `緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)]` splits the compound, yielding `吹[ceoi1|to blow / boast]` `緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)]` `水[seoi2|water]`.
```
