Now that you can handle convenience store transactions and count currency, it is
time to experience the culinary heart of Hong Kong: the iconic
`茶餐廳[caa4caan1ting1|Hong Kong style café]`.

A `茶餐廳[caa4caan1ting1|Hong Kong style café]` is a fast-paced, high-efficiency
neighborhood eatery serving local comfort foods, sweet pastries, and unique
fusion drinks. In this lesson, you will learn to order food like a local,
specify options like hot or cold using `定[ding6|or]`, count dishes using
classifiers like `杯[bui1|cup]` and `碟[dip6|plate (classifier)]`, and use
polite request softeners.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. The Diner Lexicon: Core Drinks & Snacks

To get started at a `茶餐廳[caa4caan1ting1|Hong Kong style café]`, you must know
the core staples. These items are loved by locals and represent the fusion food
culture of Hong Kong:

- `奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea]`: Creamy, strong black tea filtered through a sack
  and sweetened with evaporated milk.
- `咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]`: Strong local-style coffee.
- `鴛鴦[jyun1joeng1|yuanyang]`: An iconic blend of seven parts milk tea and
  three parts coffee.
- `檸茶[ning4caa4|lemon tea]`: Black tea served with fresh slices of lemon.
- `菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun]`: A sweet, fluffy bun topped with a
  crunchy, sugary crust resembling a pineapple skin.
- `西多士[sai1do1si2|French toast (loanword)]`: Thick-sliced bread filled with
  peanut butter, dipped in egg, deep-fried, and served with butter and syrup.
- `蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart]`: A sweet custard pastry encased in a flaky puff
  pastry or crumbly shortcrust shell.

Let's look at how to express simple cravings using our verbs
`想[soeng2|to want to]` and `要[jiu3|to want / need]`:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 飲[jam2|drink] 奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I would like to drink milk tea.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
I want a pineapple bun!
```

## 2. Cold or Hot? Expressing Choices with `定[ding6|or]`

When you order drinks in a Cantonese café, the server will almost always ask if
you want it hot or cold.

### Core Adjectives

- `熱[jit6|hot]`: Hot temperature (for drinks or food).
- `凍[dung3|cold]`: Cold or iced temperature.

### Alternative Questions with `定[ding6|or]`

To ask a question offering two alternatives, Cantonese uses the conjunction
`定[ding6|or]`. Unlike the Mandarin word for "or" (_huòzhě_), `定[ding6|or]` is
reserved **exclusively** for questions where the listener must make a choice
between option A and option B:

```cantonese
熱[jit6|hot] 定[ding6|or] 凍[dung3|cold] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Hot or cold?
```

You can use `定[ding6|or]` to connect complete noun phrases or full actions:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 想[soeng2|to want to] 飲[jam2|drink] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee] 定[ding6|or] 檸茶[ning4caa4|lemon tea] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Do you want to drink coffee or lemon tea?
```

```cantonese
呢個[ni1go3|this one] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun] 定[ding6|or] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Is this a pineapple bun or an egg tart?
```

## 3. Counting Foods & Drinks: Classifiers

In Cantonese, you cannot simply place a number directly before a noun. You must
insert a **classifier** (also known as a measure word) between the number and
the noun. Choosing the right classifier depends on the shape or nature of the
item:

### Key Classifiers

- `個[go3|general classifier]`: The general, round-item classifier. Used for
  buns, tarts, and general round foods (e.g.,
  `菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun]`, `蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart]`).
- `杯[bui1|cup]`: The container classifier for liquids. Used for any glass, cup,
  or mug of drink (e.g., `奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea]`, `咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]`).
- `碟[dip6|plate (classifier)]`: The flat-dish classifier. Used for plates of
  food, such as stir-fries or barbecued meats (e.g., `叉燒[caa1siu1|char siu]`).

### The Two Rules: `二[ji6|two]` vs `兩[loeng5|two]`

Recall that when counting items using a classifier, the number "two" must be
spoken as `兩[loeng5|two]`, never as `二[ji6|two]`:

- **Correct**: `兩[loeng5|two]` `杯[bui1|cup]` (two cups)
- **Incorrect**: `二[ji6|two]` `杯[bui1|cup]`

Let's look at full ordering examples combining quantities, temperatures, and
foods:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 一[jat1|one] 杯[bui1|cup] 凍[dung3|cold] 檸茶[ning4caa4|lemon tea] 同埋[tung4maai4|and] 兩[loeng5|two] 個[go3|general classifier] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I would like to have a glass of iced lemon tea and two egg tarts.
```

```cantonese
唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]，我[ngo5|I] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 一[jat1|one] 碟[dip6|plate (classifier)] 叉燒[caa1siu1|char siu] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
Please, I need a plate of barbecued pork.
```

## 4. Polite Expressions & Request Softeners: `唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]` vs `多謝[do1ze6|thank you]` and the particle `啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]`

Being polite is essential when dining out. Cantonese has two distinct ways to
say "thank you," and their usage depends entirely on the situation.

### 1. The Gratitude Divide: `唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]` vs `多謝[do1ze6|thank you]`

- **`唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]`**: Used to thank someone for a **service,
  assistance, or favor** (e.g., when a waiter brings you water, clears your
  table, or gives you the bill). It also means "please" or "excuse me" when
  starting a request.
- **`多謝[do1ze6|thank you]`**: Used to thank someone for a **gift, compliment,
  money, or hospitality** (e.g., when a friend buys you lunch, gives you a
  present, or compliments your Cantonese). In retail, clerks will say
  `多謝[do1ze6|thank you]` to thank you for your payment or business.

Observe how they are used at the table:

```cantonese
唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]，我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 埋單[maai4daan1|to pay the bill] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
Excuse me, I would like to pay the bill.
```

```cantonese
多謝[do1ze6|thank you] 你[nei5|you] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
Thank you for your egg tart!
```

### 2. Softening Suggestions and Requests: Particle `啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]`

The sentence-final particle
`啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]`
is used to soften commands, make suggestions, or encourage action. Without it, a
request can sound blunt or demanding. Adding
`啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]`
turns it into a polite request:

```cantonese
唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 一[jat1|one] 杯[bui1|cup] 凍[dung3|cold] 奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea] 我[ngo5|I] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
Please give me a glass of iced milk tea.
```

## 5. Conversational Dialogue

Below is a simple ordering interaction between a customer (A) and a waiter (B)
in a café.

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]！ 我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 一[jat1|one] 個[go3|general classifier] 菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun] 同埋[tung4maai4|and] 一[jat1|one] 杯[bui1|cup] 奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === Excuse me! I would like one pineapple bun and one cup of milk tea.
B: 好[hou2|good / very] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。 奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 熱[jit6|hot] 定[ding6|or] 凍[dung3|cold] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Sure. Do you want the milk tea hot or cold?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 凍[dung3|cold] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle]。 唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]！
   === I want the cold one. Thank you!
B: 好[hou2|good / very]，一[jat1|one] 個[go3|general classifier] 菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun]，一[jat1|one] 杯[bui1|cup] 凍[dung3|cold] 奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea]。
   === Okay, one pineapple bun, one cup of cold milk tea.
A: 唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]，我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 埋單[maai4daan1|to pay the bill] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。 幾多[gei2do1|how many / how much] 錢[cin2|money] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Excuse me, I want to pay the bill. How much is it?
B: 三十[saam1sap6|thirty] 蚊[man1|dollar] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]，唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]。
   === It is thirty dollars, thank you.
A: 好[hou2|good / very] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，我[ngo5|I] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 現金[jin6gam1|cash] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。 多謝[do1ze6|thank you]！
   === Great, I will pay with cash. Thank you!
B: 多謝[do1ze6|thank you]！ 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Thank you! Bye-bye!
A: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
```

## 6. Exercises

Review the terms and grammar introduced in this chapter:

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following represents the correct way to order "two cups of hot coffee" in Cantonese?
  A) 二[ji6|two] 杯[bui1|cup] 熱[jit6|hot] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]
  B) 兩[loeng5|two] 杯[bui1|cup] 凍[dung3|cold] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]
  C) 兩[loeng5|two] 杯[bui1|cup] 熱[jit6|hot] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]
  D) 兩[loeng5|two] 個[go3|general classifier] 熱[jit6|hot] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]
answer: C
explanation: When counting quantities, the number two must be 兩[loeng5|two] rather than 二[ji6|two]. The correct classifier for coffee is 杯[bui1|cup], and the adjective for hot is 熱[jit6|hot].
```
