## 1. Modern Romance & Student Relationship Codes

Navigating the world of romance and dating in Hong Kong involves a unique blend
of traditional colloquial Cantonese and highly specific modern student slang.

### Spoken Romance Basics

To talk about relationships, spoken Cantonese uses several high-frequency verbs:

- `拍拖[paak3to1|to date / go steady]`: The standard spoken verb for dating
  someone.
- `單身[daan1san1|single]`: To be single.
- `分手[fan1sau2|to break up]`: To break up with a partner.
- `表白[biu2baak6|to confess one's love]`: To make a romantic confession to
  someone.

### Split-Verb Relationship Structures

Many romance verbs like `拍拖[paak3to1|to date]` and
`分手[fan1sau2|to break up]` are separable verbs (Verb-Object pairs). When
adding an aspect marker like `咗[zo2|completed aspect]`, it must go between the
two characters, not at the end:

- `拍拖[paak3to1|to date]` -> `拍咗拖[paak3 zo2 to1|dated / have been dating]`
- `分手[fan1sau2|to break up]` ->
  `分咗手[fan1 zo2 sau2|broke up / have broken up]`

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 已經[ji5ging1|already] 分咗手[fan1 zo2 sau2|broke up] 啦[laa1|particle]。
===
We have already broken up.
```

### The University Relationship Status Codes

In Hong Kong university culture, students have developed a famous alphanumeric
coding system to describe their relationship statuses. The status is represented
by a letter indicating their status, followed by a number indicating the number
of romantic relationships they have experienced:

- **A (Available)**: Single.
- **O (Occupied)**: Currently in a relationship.
- **C (Complicated)**: In a complicated relationship status.

Combined with a number, you get unique slang terms:

- `A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated]`: Single and has never been in a
  relationship (a very common student self-descriptor).
- `O1[ou1jat1|in a relationship for the first time]`: In a relationship for the
  first time in their life.

When someone successfully transitions from being single to being in a
relationship, they use a highly popular student slang expression:

- `出[ceot1|to exit / go out / leave]` `Pool[pu1|singles pool (student slang)]`:
  Literally meaning "leaving the singles pool" (getting into a relationship).

## 2. The Dynamics of Flirting & "Soldiering"

Cantonese pop culture and student dynamics have created distinct humorous terms
for crushes and unrequited admiration.

### Crushes & Ideals

- `暗戀[am3 lyun2|to crush on / love secretly]`: To have a secret crush on
  someone.
- `女神[neoi5san4|goddess / ideal woman]`: An ideal woman or female crush.
- `男神[naam4san4|male god / ideal man]`: An ideal man or male crush.

### The Concept of "Soldiering" (`收兵[sau1bing1|to collect platonic admirers]`)

One of the most unique local slang metaphors is:

- `收兵[sau1bing1|to collect platonic admirers (literally 'collect soldiers')]`:
  The act of keeping platonic admirers around to do favors, buy gifts, or drive
  them around, without ever intending to date them.
- `兵[bing1|platonic admirer who is led on (literally 'soldier')]`: A platonic
  admirer who is led on and does favors for their crush (the "goddess") in the
  hope of eventually dating them.

## 3. Social Media & Messaging Culture

When texting or browsing social media, Cantonese speakers seamlessly integrate
English loanwords and spoken contractions.

### Social Media Actions

- `Po[pou1|to post (social media loanword)]` `相[soeng2|photo / picture]`: To
  post photos.
- `呃[aak1|to cheat / trick / fish]` `like[laaik1|likes (social media)]`:
  Literally meaning "cheating/fishing for likes" (posting attention-seeking
  photos or updates to harvest likes).
- `些牙[se1aa4|to share (loanword)]`: To share a post or link (a direct phonetic
  loanword from English "share").
- `科勞[fo1lou4|to follow (loanword)]`: To follow someone on social media
  (phonetic loanword from "follow").
- `船[syun4|boat / ship]` `P[pi1|party (loanword)]`: A yacht party or boat
  party, highly popular for social media posting in the summer.

### Messaging Etiquette

- `覆[fuk1|to reply / write back]`: To reply to a message.
- `已讀不回[ji5duk6bat1wui4|read but not reply / ghost]`: To read a message and
  not reply (ghosting someone).
- `潛水[cim4seoi2|to lurk / go offline / go silent]`: Literally "diving
  underwater" (going completely silent or offline in group chats).

## 4. Public Displays of Affection & Infidelity

- `放閃[fong3sim2|to show public displays of affection / show PDA]`: Literally
  "releasing a flash" (showing public displays of affection or bragging about
  relationships on social media).
- `閃[sim2|flashy / showing PDA]`: Flashy, sweet, or showing PDA.
- `閃盲[sim2maang4|to be blinded by PDA]`: Literally "blinded by the flash"
  (being blinded by other people's sweet relationship posts).
- `甜[tim4|sweet / romantic]`: Sweet or romantic.
- `心碎[sam1seoi3|heartbroken]`: Heartbroken.
- `出軌[ceot1gwai2|to cheat / have an affair / infidelity]`: To cheat or have an
  affair.

## 5. Situational Dialogues

### Dialogue 1: Gossiping About an "Out of Pool" Classmate

Two university friends, `A` and `B`, talk about their classmate who has finally
found a partner.

```dialog
A: 喂[wai2|hey!]，你[nei5|you] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 去唔去[heoi3 m4 heoi3|go or not] 船[syun4|boat / ship] P[pi1|party (loanword)] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Hey, are you going to the yacht party tomorrow?
B: 去[heoi3|to go] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 聽講[ting1gong2|hear say] 佢哋[keoi5dei6|they] 會[wui5|will] 喺[hai2|at] 嗰度[go2dou6|there] 放閃[fong3sim2|to show PDA] 囉[lo1|particle]！
   === Yes, I am! I heard they will show off their relationship over there!
A: 邊個[bin1go3|who] 呀[aa3|particle]？ 唔通[m4tung1|could it be that] 係[hai6|is] 嗰個[go2go3|that] A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated] 同學[tung4hok6|classmate / student] 咩[me1|particle]？
   === Who? Could it be that classmate who was single and never dated?
B: 係[hai6|yes] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 佢[keoi5|he] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 表[biu2|to confess] 咗[zo2|completed action] 白[baak6|love]， 成功[sing1gung1|successfully] 出[ceot1|to leave] Pool[pu1|singles pool (student slang)] 啦[laa1|particle]！
   === Yes! He confessed his love yesterday and successfully left the singles pool!
A: 真係[zan1hai6|really] 甜[tim4|sweet] 爆[baau3|extremely] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 我[ngo5|I] 真係[zan1hai6|really] 戥[dang6|for] 佢[keoi5|him] 開心[hoi1sam1|happy] 囉[lo1|particle]！
   === That is extremely sweet! I am really happy for him!
```

### Dialogue 2: Texting Etiquette & Social Media

A girlfriend (`A`) complains to her boyfriend (`B`) about his texting habits.

```dialog
A: 點解[dim2gaai2|why] 你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 已讀不回[ji5duk6bat1wui4|read but not reply / ghost] 我[ngo5|me] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Why did you read and not reply to me today?
B: 唔好意思[m4 hou2 ji3si1|sorry] 呀[aa3|particle]， 我[ngo5|I] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 好[hou2|very] 忙[mong4|busy] 呀[aa3|particle]。
   === I am so sorry, I was very busy today.
A: 但係[daan6hai6|but] 你[nei5|you] 又[jau6|again] 喺[hai2|at] IG[ai1zi1|Instagram] Po[pou1|post] 相[soeng2|photo] 呃[aak1|to fish] like[laaik1|likes]？
   === But then you posted a photo on Instagram to fish for likes?
B: 嗰個[go2go3|that one] 係[hai6|is] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 影[jing2|photograph] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 靚[leng3|pretty] 相[soeng2|photo] 啫[ze1|particle]。
   === That was just a nice photo taken yesterday.
A: 算啦[syun3 laa1|forget it]， 你[nei5|you] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 同[tung4|with] 我[ngo5|me] 出街[ceot1gaai1|go out / go on a date] 放閃[fong3sim2|to show PDA] 補數[bou2sou3|to make up for it] 啦[laa1|particle]！
   === Forget it, you must go on a date with me tomorrow and show some PDA to make up for it!
```

## 6. Exercises

Test your understanding of romance terminology, student slang, and social media
expressions in Cantonese.

```exercise
question: |
  Which status code represents a student who has never been in a romantic relationship before?
  A) O1[ou1jat1|in a relationship for the first time]
  B) A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated]
  C) C1[si1jat1|complicated first relationship]
  D) A2[ei1ji6|single after two relationships]
answer: B
explanation: A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated] represents a student who is Available (single) and has experienced zero (0) relationships. O1[ou1jat1|in a relationship for the first time] represents Occupied (in a relationship) for the first time.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct social media term meaning "to post photos":
  佢[keoi5|she] 鍾意[zung1ji3|to like] 喺[hai2|at] IG[ai1zi1|Instagram] ______ 呃[aak1|to fish] like[laaik1|likes]。
answer: Po[pou1|to post] 相[soeng2|photo]
explanation: Po[pou1|to post (social media loanword)] followed by 相[soeng2|photo / picture] represents the common colloquial verb phrase "to post photos".
```

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into colloquial Cantonese:
  "Why did you ghost me (read and not reply) yesterday?"
answer: 點解[dim2gaai2|why] 你[nei5|you] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 已讀不回[ji5duk6bat1wui4|read but not reply / ghost] 我[ngo5|me] 呀[aa3|particle]？
explanation: 點解[dim2gaai2|why] is "why", 你[nei5|you] is "you", 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] is "yesterday", 已讀不回[ji5duk6bat1wui4|read but not reply / ghost] is the verb meaning "to ghost / read but not reply", 我[ngo5|me] is "me", and 呀[aa3|particle] is the friendly sentence-final particle.
```
