In this chapter, we will master some of the most critical colloquial
**Sentence-Final Particles (SFPs)** in Cantonese.

In Cantonese, sentence-final particles shape emotion and meaning. They express
the speaker's emotional state, convey subtle attitudes, and establish the
relationship between speaker and listener.

We will learn how to use:

1. `啫[ze1|only / just]` (downplaying and minimizing)
2. `嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]`
   (stating the obvious)
3. `吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]` (asking for confirmation)

---

## 1. Downplaying & Minimizing: `啫[ze1|only / just]`

When you want to show that something is "only," "just," or "nothing more than"
what you've stated, you attach `啫[ze1|only / just]` at the very end of your
sentence.

This particle is highly practical because it downplays the significance of an
action or price, preventing you from sounding boasting, dramatic, or overly
serious.

### 1. Minimizing Numbers & Prices

If someone thinks a price is expensive, or if you want to emphasize that an
amount is small, use `啫[ze1|only / just]`:

```cantonese
十[sap6|ten] 蚊[man1|dollar] 啫[ze1|only / just]！
===
It is only ten dollars!
```

### 2. Downplaying Identity or Responsibility

You can use it to reassure someone that you are "just" a student or "only" doing
a basic job:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 學生[hok6saang1|student] 啫[ze1|only / just]。
===
I am only a student (nothing more).
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 問[man6|to ask] 吓[haa5|brief action marker] 啫[ze1|only / just]。
===
I am just asking (don't take it seriously).
```

## 2. Stating the Obvious: `嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]`

In Mandarin, the question particle _ma_ is a simple question marker. In
Cantonese, however,
`嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]`
is **assertive**. It indicates that what you are saying is an obvious truth, a
reminder, or a fact that the listener already knows or ought to accept.

Think of it as the English "you know," "obviously," or "of course."

### 1. Pointing Out Obvious Facts

Use it when a reason or fact should be completely clear to both parties:

```cantonese
今日[gam1jat6|today] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a day off]，公司[gung1si1|company] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！
===
Today is a holiday, so the office is obviously closed!
```

### 2. Reminding of Social Roles or Duties

It is often used to assert what is natural or expected based on relationships:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 朋友[pang4jau5|friend]，你[nei5|you] 應該[jing1goi1|should] 知[zi1|to know] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！
===
You are their friend, so you should obviously know!
```

## 3. Asking for Confirmation: `吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]`

The particle `吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]` is used to express
doubt, surprise, or to ask for confirmation of what you just heard. It can be
used as a standalone interjection (like "Huh?") or added to the end of a
sentence.

### 1. Expressing Surprise or Doubt

```cantonese
吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]？真[zan1|real / true] 㗎[gaa3|sentence-final particle indicating assertion]？
===
Huh? Is that true?
```

### 2. Asking for Confirmation

When added to the end of a statement, it turns it into a rhetorical question or
asks for confirmation of an unexpected fact:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 唔[m4|not] 去[heoi3|go] 吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]？
===
You are not going, really? (I thought you were!)
```

## 4. Situational Dialogue: A Simple Query

Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B) discuss their schedule.

```dialog
A: 你[nei5|you] 去[heoi3|go] 邊度[bin1dou6|where] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？去[heoi3|go] 買嘢[maai5je5|to shop] 吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]？
   === Where are you going? Going shopping, really?
B: 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，我[ngo5|I] 去[heoi3|go] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 啫[ze1|only / just]。
   === No, I'm just going to work.
A: 今日[gam1jat6|today] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a day off]，公司[gung1si1|company] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！
   === Today is a holiday, the office is obviously closed!
B: 我[ngo5|I] 問[man6|to ask] 吓[haa5|brief action marker] 啫[ze1|only / just]。
   === I'm just asking.
```
