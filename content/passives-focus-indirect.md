In this lesson, we will cover three advanced, high-frequency grammatical pillars
that will elevate your conversational flow to native speed and structure:

1. **The Passive Voice**: Structuring passive statements using
   `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]`.
2. **Focus & Clarification**: Defining or emphasizing statements using
   `即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning]`.
3. **Indirect Speech & Mistaken Beliefs**: Reporting spoken messages using
   `話[waa6|to say]` and expressing mistaken assumptions using
   `以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think]`.

Mastering these tools will allow you to recount stories, clarify
misunderstandings, and describe unexpected workplace or daily life occurrences
with ease.

---

## 1. The Passive Voice: `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]`

Unlike formal written Chinese which relies on the passive marker (Mandarin:
_bèi_), colloquial spoken Cantonese uses `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]`
as its primary passive voice marker.

In Cantonese, the passive voice is highly active and structural: you must
explicitly state the "agent" (the person or thing performing the action). If the
agent is unknown, native speakers use `人[jan4|person]` or
`人哋[jan4dei6|other people]` as a placeholder.

### Passive Voice Structure

- **Formula**: `[Receiver/Object]` + `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]` +
  `[Agent/Placeholder]` + `[Verb]` + `[Aspect/Suffixes]`

Let's look at how this works in practice:

### 1. Known Agent (e.g. The Boss)

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 話[waa6|to say] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]。
===
I was scolded by the boss.
```

> [!NOTE] In Cantonese, the verb `話[waa6|to say]` carries a double meaning.
> While it literally means "to say", when used in passive contexts or when
> directed at someone (e.g., `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]`
> `人[jan4|person]` `話[waa6|to say]`), it colloquially means "to scold" or
> "criticize".

### 2. Unknown Agent / General People (using `人[jan4|person]` or `人哋[jan4dei6|other people]`)

If your wallet or phone is stolen, or something is broken, and you do not know
exactly who did it, you cannot omit the agent in Cantonese. You must use
`人[jan4|person]` or `人哋[jan4dei6|other people]` as the agent:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 個[go3|general classifier] 銀包[ngan4baau1|wallet] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 人[jan4|person] 偷[tau1|to steal] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]！
===
My wallet was stolen by someone! (Literally: "My wallet by someone stolen!")
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 部[bou6|classifier for machines] 電話[din6waa2|phone] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 人哋[jan4dei6|other people] 打爛[da2laan6|to break] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]。
===
My phone was smashed by others.
```

> [!NOTE] If the receiver (the object being acted upon) is already obvious from
> the context of the conversation, it can be omitted. For example, you can
> simply exclaim `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]` `人[jan4|person]`
> `偷[tau1|to steal]` `咗[zo2|completed action marker]`！(It got stolen by
> someone!) without explicitly stating "my wallet".

## 2. Focus, Defining & Emphasizing: `即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning]`

When speaking Cantonese, you will hear `即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning]` in
almost every conversation. It is a highly versatile focus marker used to:

- Define or clarify a statement ("namely", "that is to say", "which means").
- Summarize a point or draw a conclusion ("basically", "in other words").

### Clarifying Actions and Statuses

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]？ 即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning] 你[nei5|you] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a day off] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
You're not going to work? Basically, you have a day off!
```

We can also pair `即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning]` with `話[waa6|to say]` to
form `即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning]` `話[waa6|to say]`, which translates
precisely to "which is to say that..." or "which means that...":

```cantonese
即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning] 話[waa6|to say] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔[m4|not] 嚟[lei4|to come / sentence-final particle] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
Which is to say he is not coming.
```

## 3. Indirect Speech & Mistaken Assumptions: `話[waa6|to say]` & `以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think]`

Recounting conversations and describing assumptions that turned out to be false
are crucial elements of native communication.

### 1. Indirect Speech: `話[waa6|to say]` ... `知[zi1|to know]`

In Cantonese, the verb `話[waa6|to say]` acts both as "to say" and as a
conjunction "that" to introduce reported clauses.

To express "tell someone", spoken Cantonese uses the structure
`話[waa6|to say]` + [Person] + `知[zi1|to know]` (literally: "say to person to
let them know"):

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 話[waa6|to say] 我[ngo5|I] 知[zi1|to know] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 好[hou2|good / very] 攰[gui6|tired] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He told me that he is very tired.
```

You can also use this structure to report more complex situations or news:

```cantonese
同事[tung4si6|colleague] 話[waa6|to say] 我[ngo5|I] 知[zi1|to know] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]。
===
My colleague told me that the boss is not going to work tomorrow.
```

Alternatively, you can use `話俾[waa6bei2|to tell]` + [Person] +
`知[zi1|to know]` or `話俾[waa6bei2|to tell]` + [Person] +
`聽[teng1|to listen]`, which are extremely common variations with the exact same
meaning:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 話俾[waa6bei2|to tell] 我[ngo5|I] 聽[teng1|to listen] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 好[hou2|good / very] 攰[gui6|tired] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He told me that he is very tired.
```

And for a complex sentence:

```cantonese
阿媽[aa3maa1|mother] 話俾[waa6bei2|to tell] 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 聽[teng1|to listen] 今晚[gam1maan5|tonight] 出去[ceot1heoi3|go out] 食飯[sik6faan6|to eat a meal]。
===
Mom told us that tonight we are going out to eat a meal.
```

### 2. Mistaken Assumptions: `以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think]`

When you assumed something to be true but it turned out to be false (e.g., "I
thought you were at work, but you are not"), Cantonese uses the verb
`以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think]`.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think] 你[nei5|you] 返[faan1|to return] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 工[gung1|job]。
===
I mistakenly thought you were working.
```

## 4. Situational Dialogue: A Workplace Incident

Wing-sum (A) notices that Wing-kit (B) looks completely exhausted at the office.
B explains that his phone was stolen and he was scolded by the boss, but then
reveals a surprising workplace twist.

```dialog
A: 你[nei5|you] 點解[dim2gaai2|why] 咁[gam3|so / to this degree] 攰[gui6|tired] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Why are you so tired?
B: 好[hou2|good / very] 慘[caam2|miserable] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 我[ngo5|I] 部[bou6|classifier for machines] 電話[din6waa2|phone] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 人[jan4|person] 偷[tau1|to steal] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]！
   === So miserable! My phone was stolen by someone!
A: 真係[zan1hai6|really] 慘[caam2|miserable] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 之後[zi1hau6|after] 呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]？
   === That's really pitiful! And then?
B: 之後[zi1hau6|after] 我[ngo5|I] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 遲到[ci4dou3|to be late]， 又[jau6|also / again] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 話[waa6|to say] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]！
   === Afterward I arrived late to work, and I was scolded by the boss again!
A: 即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning] 話[waa6|to say] 你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 真係[zan1hai6|really] 好[hou2|good / very] 慘[caam2|miserable] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Which means you are really so miserable today!
B: 係[hai6|to be / yes] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。 啱啱[aam1aam1|just now] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 話[waa6|to say] 我[ngo5|I] 知[zi1|to know]， 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 會[wui5|will / can] 同[tung4|with / and] 我[ngo5|I] 一齊[jat1cai4|together] 食飯[sik6faan6|to eat a meal]。 我[ngo5|I] 以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 想[soeng2|to want to] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 我[ngo5|I] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]！
   === Yes indeed. Just now the boss told me that tomorrow he will eat a meal together with me. I mistakenly thought he wanted to fire me as well!
A: 即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔[m4|not] 會[wui5|will / can] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 你[nei5|you] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 咁[gam3|so / to this degree] 你[nei5|you] 放心[fong3sam1|to feel relieved] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Namely he won't fire you! So please set your mind at ease!
```

Test your understanding of passives, focus markers, and reported speech
structures.

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into Cantonese using correct semantic annotations:
  "My wallet was stolen by someone."
  (Hint: Use ngo5 for "I", ngan4baau1 for "wallet", bei2 for passive marker, and tau1 for "to steal")
answer: 我[ngo5|I] 個[go3|general classifier] 銀包[ngan4baau1|wallet] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 人[jan4|person] 偷[tau1|to steal] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]。
explanation: |
  The passive construction is `[Object] + 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] + [Agent] + [Verb] + 咗[zo2|completed action marker]`. Since the thief is unknown, we use `人[jan4|person]` as a placeholder agent.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct passive marker to say "I was scolded by the boss":
  我[ngo5|I] ________ 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 話[waa6|to say] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]。
answer: 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]
explanation: |
  `俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let]` is the spoken Cantonese passive marker, corresponding to the formal written Chinese marker (Mandarin: *bèi*). `話[waa6|to say]` colloquially means "to scold" when used in a passive sentence.
```

  C) 以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think]
answer: B
explanation: |
  `即係[zik1hai6|that is / meaning]` is a very high-frequency focus marker in spoken Cantonese used to clarify, define, or emphasize a statement.
