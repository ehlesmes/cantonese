Welcome to Chapter 15! In this lesson, we will cover three advanced,
high-frequency grammatical pillars that will elevate your conversational flow to
native speed and structure:

1. **The Passive Voice**: Structuring passive statements using
   `俾[bei2|by / to give]`.
2. **Focus & Clarification**: Defining or emphasizing statements using
   `即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning]`.
3. **Indirect Speech & Mistaken Beliefs**: Reporting spoken messages using
   `話[waa6|to say / tell]` and expressing mistaken assumptions using
   `以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think / assume / believe]`.

Mastering these tools will allow you to recount stories, clarify
misunderstandings, and describe unexpected workplace or daily life occurrences
with ease.

## 1. The Passive Voice: `俾[bei2|by / to give]`

Unlike formal written Chinese which relies on the passive marker (Mandarin:
_bèi_), colloquial spoken Cantonese uses `俾[bei2|by / to give]` as its primary
passive voice marker.

In Cantonese, the passive voice is highly active and structural: you must
explicitly state the "agent" (the person or thing performing the action). If the
agent is unknown, native speakers use `人[jan4|person / people / human]` or
`人哋[jan4dei6|others / other people]` as a placeholder.

### Passive Voice Structure

- **Formula**: `[Receiver/Object]` + `俾[bei2|by / to give]` +
  `[Agent/Placeholder]` + `[Verb]` + `[Aspect/Suffixes]`

Let's look at how this works in practice:

### 1. Known Agent (e.g. The Boss)

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 俾[bei2|by / to give] 老細[lou5sai3|boss / employer (colloquial)] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]。
===
I was scolded by the boss.
```

> [!NOTE] In Cantonese, the verb `話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech]`
> carries a double meaning. While it literally means "to say", when used in
> passive contexts or when directed at someone (e.g., `俾[bei2|by]`
> `人[jan4|people / others]` `話[waa6|to scold]`), it colloquially means "to
> scold" or "criticize".

### 2. Unknown Agent / General People (using `人[jan4|person / people / human]` or `人哋[jan4dei6|others / other people]`)

If your wallet or phone is stolen, or something is broken, and you do not know
exactly who did it, you cannot omit the agent in Cantonese. You must use
`人[jan4|person / people / human]` or `人哋[jan4dei6|others / other people]` as
the agent:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 個[go3|measure word for general items] 銀包[ngan4baau1|wallet / purse] 俾[bei2|by / to give] 人[jan4|person / people / human] 偷[tau1|to steal] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]！
===
My wallet was stolen by someone! (Literally: "My wallet by someone stolen!")
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 部[bou6|measure word for machines/phones] 電話[din6waa2|telephone / phone] 俾[bei2|by / to give] 人哋[jan4dei6|others / other people] 打爛[da2laan6|to break / smash / shatter] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]。
===
My phone was smashed by others.
```

> [!NOTE] If the receiver (the object being acted upon) is already obvious from
> the context of the conversation, it can be omitted. For example, you can
> simply exclaim `俾[bei2|by / to give]` `人[jan4|person / people / human]`
> `偷[tau1|to steal]`
> `咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]`！(It
> got stolen by someone!) without explicitly stating "my wallet".

## 2. Focus, Defining & Emphasizing: `即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning]`

When speaking Cantonese, you will hear
`即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning]` in almost every
conversation. It is a highly versatile focus marker used to:

- Define or clarify a statement ("namely", "that is to say", "which means").
- Summarize a point or draw a conclusion ("basically", "in other words").

### Clarifying Actions and Statuses

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work / be at work]？ 即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning] 你[nei5|you] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a holiday / day off] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
You're not going to work? Basically, you have a day off!
```

We can also pair `即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning]`
with `話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech]` to form
`即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely]` `話[waa6|to say / tell]`, which translates
precisely to "which is to say that..." or "which means that...":

```cantonese
即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔[m4|not] 嚟[lai4|to come] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
Which is to say he is not coming.
```

## 3. Indirect Speech & Mistaken Assumptions: `話[waa6|to say / tell]` & `以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think / assume / believe]`

Recounting conversations and describing assumptions that turned out to be false
are crucial elements of native communication.

### 1. Indirect Speech: `話[waa6|to say / tell]` ... `知[zi1|to know]`

In Cantonese, the verb `話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech]` acts both as
"to say" and as a conjunction "that" to introduce reported clauses.

To express "tell someone", spoken Cantonese uses the structure
`話[waa6|to say]` + [Person] + `知[zi1|to know]` (literally: "say to person to
let them know"):

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 我[ngo5|I / me] 知[zi1|to know] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 攰[gui6|tired / exhausted] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He told me that he is very tired.
```

You can also use this structure to report more complex situations or news:

```cantonese
同事[tung4si6|colleague] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 我[ngo5|I / me] 知[zi1|to know] 老細[lou5sai3|boss] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|go to work]。
===
My colleague told me that the boss is not going to work tomorrow.
```

Alternatively, you can use `話俾[waa6bei2|to tell / say to]` + [Person] +
`知[zi1|to know]` or `話俾[waa6bei2|to tell / say to]` + [Person] +
`聽[teng1|to listen / hear]`, which are extremely common variations with the
exact same meaning:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 話俾[waa6bei2|to tell / say to] 我[ngo5|I / me] 聽[teng1|to listen / hear] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 攰[gui6|tired / exhausted] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He told me that he is very tired.
```

And for a complex sentence:

```cantonese
阿媽[aa3maa1|mother] 話俾[waa6bei2|to tell / say to] 我哋[ngo5dei6|us] 聽[teng1|to listen / hear] 今晚[gam1maan5|tonight] 出去[ceot1heoi3|go out] 食飯[sik6faan6|eat a meal]。
===
Mom told us that tonight we are going out to eat a meal.
```

### 2. Mistaken Assumptions: `以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think / assume / believe]`

When you assumed something to be true but it turned out to be false (e.g., "I
thought you were at work, but you are not"), Cantonese uses the verb
`以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think / assume / believe]`.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think / assume / believe] 你[nei5|you] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work / be at work] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect particle indicating action in progress]。
===
I mistakenly thought you were working.
```

## 4. Situational Dialogue: A Workplace Incident

Wing-sum (A) notices that Wing-kit (B) looks completely exhausted at the office.
B explains that his phone was stolen and he was scolded by the boss, but then
reveals a surprising workplace twist.

```dialog
A: 你[nei5|you] 點解[dim2gaai2|why / how come] 咁[gam3|so / such / to this degree] 攰[gui6|tired / exhausted] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Why are you so tired?
B: 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 慘[caam2|miserable / pitiful / tragic] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 我[ngo5|I / me] 部[bou6|measure word for machines/phones] 電話[din6waa2|telephone / phone] 俾[bei2|by / to give] 人[jan4|person / people / human] 偷[tau1|to steal] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]！
   === So miserable! My phone was stolen by someone!
A: 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 慘[caam2|miserable / pitiful / tragic] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 之後[zi1hau6|afterwards / after] 呢[ni1|this]？
   === That's really pitiful! And then?
B: 之後[zi1hau6|afterwards / after] 我[ngo5|I / me] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work / be at work] 遲到[ci4dou3|to be late / arrive late]， 又[jau6|again / also] 俾[bei2|by / to give] 老細[lou5sai3|boss / employer (colloquial)] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]！
   === Afterward I arrived late to work, and I was scolded by the boss again!
A: 即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 慘[caam2|miserable / pitiful / tragic] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Which means you are really so miserable today!
B: 係[hai6|to be] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。 啱啱[aam1aam1|just now / exactly] 老細[lou5sai3|boss / employer (colloquial)] 話[waa6|to say / tell / words / speech] 我[ngo5|I / me] 知[zi1|to know]， 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 會[wui5|will / would / can / know how to / meet] 同[tung4|with / and] 我[ngo5|I / me] 一齊[jat1cai4|together] 食飯[sik6faan6|to eat a meal / have dinner]。 我[ngo5|I / me] 以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think / assume / believe] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 想[soeng2|to want / would like to] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 我[ngo5|I / me] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 添[tim1|particle indicating in addition / as well / furthermore]！
   === Yes indeed. Just now the boss told me that tomorrow he will eat a meal together with me. I mistakenly thought he wanted to fire me as well!
A: 即係[zik1hai6|that is / namely / in other words / meaning] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔[m4|not] 會[wui5|will / would / can / know how to / meet] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 你[nei5|you] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 咁[gam3|so / such / to this degree] 你[nei5|you] 放心[fong3sam1|to feel relieved / set one's mind at ease] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Namely he won't fire you! So please set your mind at ease!
```

  The passive construction is `[Object] + 俾[bei2|by / to give] + [Agent] + [Verb] + 咗[zo2|completed aspect]`. Since the thief is unknown, we use `人[jan4|person / people / human]` as a placeholder agent.
