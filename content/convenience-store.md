Now that you know how to introduce yourself and strike up a basic conversation,
it is time to venture out into the vibrant streets of Hong Kong.

In this lesson, we will focus on everyday transactions—specifically inside the
ubiquitous local convenience stores (like 7-Eleven and Circle K). You will learn
how to express wants and needs, ask how much things cost, and count spoken
dollars using Cantonese numerals.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Wanting & Needing: `想[soeng2|to want to]` vs `要[jiu3|to want / need]`

To request items or express desires, Cantonese uses two main auxiliary verbs.
Understanding the pragmatic difference between them helps you sound polite and
natural:

- `想[soeng2|to want to]`: Equivalent to "would like to" or "want to". It is
  soft, polite, and expresses an internal desire or intention.
- `要[jiu3|to want / need]`: Equivalent to "need" or a stronger, more assertive
  "want". It implies necessity or an immediate, direct request.

### Basic Sentence Structure

To ask for or request something, place either auxiliary verb directly before the
verb `買[maai5|buy]` or use `要[jiu3|to want / need]` directly with a noun:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 買[maai5|buy] 嘢飲[je5jam2|drinks] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I would like to buy some drinks.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 呢個[ni1go3|this one]。
===
I need this one.
```

> [!TIP] When you are at a counter ordering or buying something, using a
> combination like `我[ngo5|I]` `想[soeng2|to want to]`
> `要[jiu3|to want / need]` is an extremely common, polite, and natural way to
> make requests.

## 2. Pointing Things Out & Connecting Items: `呢個[ni1go3|this one]`, `嗰個[go2go3|that one]`, `咁樣[gam2joeng6|like this]`, and `同埋[tung4maai4|and]`

If you do not know the name of an object in a shop, you can simply point and use
these high-frequency demonstrative pronouns:

- `呢個[ni1go3|this one]`: Used for items close to you.
- `嗰個[go2go3|that one]`: Used for items further away.
- `咁樣[gam2joeng6|like this]`: Used when describing choices, indicating "like
  this" or "this way".

To connect two nouns together, like saying "and" in English, use the conjunction
`同埋[tung4maai4|and]`.

Observe how these are used to make simple requests:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 買[maai5|buy] 呢個[ni1go3|this one] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I want to buy this one.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 呢個[ni1go3|this one] 同埋[tung4maai4|and] 嗰個[go2go3|that one] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
I will take this one and that one!
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 咁樣[gam2joeng6|like this] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle] 嘢飲[je5jam2|drinks] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I would like to have a drink like this.
```

## 3. Asking for Prices & Counting Dollars

### Asking "How much?"

To ask how much an item costs, we use `幾多[gei2do1|how many / how much]` and
`錢[cin2|money]`. Together, `幾多[gei2do1|how many / how much]` `錢[cin2|money]`
is the standard phrase for "How much is it?":

```cantonese
呢個[ni1go3|this one] 幾多[gei2do1|how many / how much] 錢[cin2|money] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
How much is this one?
```

```cantonese
嗰個[go2go3|that one] 幾多[gei2do1|how many / how much] 錢[cin2|money] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
How much is that one?
```

### The Spoken Dollar: `蚊[man1|dollar]`

In formal written Chinese, currency is represented by words like _jyun_ or
_faai_. However, in spoken Cantonese, you **must** use the colloquial classifier
`蚊[man1|dollar]` to mean "dollars".

To state a price, use the pattern: **Number + `蚊[man1|dollar]`**

### Cantonese Numerals (1 to 10)

Here are the basic numerals you will use to count dollars and specify
quantities:

- `一[jat1|one]`: One
- `二[ji6|two]`: Two (used in compound numbers like twelve or twenty)
- `三[saam1|three]`: Three
- `四[sei3|four]`: Four
- `五[ng5|five]`: Five
- `六[luk6|six]`: Six
- `七[cat1|seven]`: Seven
- `八[baat3|eight]`: Eight
- `九[gau2|nine]`: Nine
- `十[sap6|ten]`: Ten

> [!IMPORTANT] **The `二[ji6|two]` vs `兩[loeng5|two]` Rule:** When counting
> items or stating prices with a classifier like `蚊[man1|dollar]`, you must use
> `兩[loeng5|two]` instead of `二[ji6|two]`.
>
> - **Correct**: `兩[loeng5|two]` `蚊[man1|dollar]`
> - **Incorrect**: `二[ji6|two]` `蚊[man1|dollar]`

### Combined Prices

You can easily combine tens and units just like in English:

- `十[sap6|ten]` `五[ng5|five]` `蚊[man1|dollar]` = fifteen dollars
- `二十[ji6sap6|twenty]` `三[saam1|three]` `蚊[man1|dollar]` = twenty-three
  dollars
- `三十[saam1sap6|thirty]` `二[ji6|two]` `蚊[man1|dollar]` = thirty-two dollars

Let's look at examples:

```cantonese
呢個[ni1go3|this one] 四[sei3|four] 蚊[man1|dollar]。
===
This one is four dollars.
```

```cantonese
嗰個[go2go3|that one] 二十[ji6sap6|twenty] 蚊[man1|dollar]。
===
That one is twenty dollars.
```

## 4. Conversational Dialogue

Below is a simple interaction between a customer (A) and a convenience store
clerk (B) in Hong Kong.

```dialog
A: 你好[nei5hou2|hello]呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]，我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 買[maai5|buy] 呢個[ni1go3|this one] 同埋[tung4maai4|and] 嗰個[go2go3|that one]。
   === Hello there! Excuse me, I want to buy this one and that one.
B: 好[hou2|good / very] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。 呢個[ni1go3|this one] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 可樂[ho2lok6|cola]，嗰個[go2go3|that one] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 薯片[syu4pin2|potato chips]。
   === Sure. This one is Coke, that one is potato chips.
A: 幾多[gei2do1|how many / how much] 錢[cin2|money] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === How much is it?
B: 三十[saam1sap6|thirty] 蚊[man1|dollar] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === It is thirty dollars.
A: 好[hou2|good / very] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。 唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please]！
   === Okay. Thank you!
B: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
```

## 5. Exercises

Review the terms and grammar introduced in this chapter:

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following is the correct spoken Cantonese phrase for "two dollars"?
  A) 二[ji6|two] 蚊[man1|dollar]
  B) 兩[loeng5|two] 蚊[man1|dollar]
  C) 二十[ji6sap6|twenty] 蚊[man1|dollar]
  D) 兩[loeng5|two] 個[go3|general classifier]
answer: B
explanation: When counting currency or items with a classifier like 蚊[man1|dollar], the number two must be pronounced as 兩[loeng5|two] rather than 二[ji6|two].
```

```exercise
question: Translate "Excuse me, I would like to buy Coke and potato chips." into Cantonese with correct annotations.

explanation: 唔該[m4goi1|thank you / please] is used for 'excuse me' or 'please'; 想[soeng2|to want to] is the softening auxiliary verb; 同埋[tung4maai4|and] is used to connect nouns.
```
