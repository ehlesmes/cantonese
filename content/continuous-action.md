Now that you have learned how to tell the time and express schedules, it is time
to talk about what is happening _right now_ in the present moment. In this
chapter, we will learn how to describe ongoing actions using the continuous
aspect suffix, navigate verb-object splitting rules, and pick up the iconic
local slang verb `Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off]` to talk about slacking off.

---

## 1. Ongoing Action: Suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]`

When you want to describe an action that is happening right now in Cantonese,
you attach the verbal aspect suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` directly
to the verb. This is equivalent to the English "-ing" or the Mandarin
_zhèngzài_.

### The Continuous Aspect Pattern

> **Formula:** [Subject] + [Verb] + `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` +
> [Object]

Observe this basic structure in action:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 食[sik6|to eat] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 飯[faan6|cooked rice / meal] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I am eating a meal.
```

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 睇[tai2|to look / watch] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 書[syu1|book] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He is reading a book.
```

## 2. Verb-Object Splitting with `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]`

Many high-frequency Cantonese verbs are actually **compound verbs** consisting
of a verb and a default noun object (Verb-Object pairs). Examples include:

- `食飯[sik6faan6|to eat a meal]` (literally "eat rice")
- `返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]` (literally "return to work")
- `落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]` (literally "fall rain")
- `打風[daa2fung1|to have a typhoon]` (literally "hit wind")

> [!IMPORTANT] **Splitting Rule**: When using the aspect marker
> `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]`, you must place it **between the verb and
> the object**, never at the end of the compound.

Let's look at how these are split in everyday speech:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 返[faan1|to return] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 工[gung1|job] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I am working (in the process of going to/doing work).
```

```cantonese
而家[ji4gaa1|now] 落[lok6|to descend] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 雨[jyu5|rain] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
It is raining right now.
```

```cantonese
出面[ceot1min6|outside] 打[daa2|to hit] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 風[fung1|wind] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
A typhoon is raging outside right now!
```

## 3. The Ultimate Slang: `Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off]`

One of the most famous and beloved colloquial words in modern Cantonese is the
verb `Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off]`. It has no standard Chinese character (the
English letters "Hea" are universally written by locals in text messages).

`Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off]` can mean:

1. **To chill or hang out** lazily without a specific purpose.
2. **To slack off** or procrastinate on the job or at school.
3. **Low-effort / easy** (when used as an adjective, e.g., "a hea job").

When combined with our continuous aspect marker, it describes the ultimate state
of chilling out:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 做[zou6|to do] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 咩嘢[me1je5|what kind of thing] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
What are you doing right now?
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！
===
I am chilling out, obviously!
```

## 4. Situational Dialogue: Checking In

Ka-ho (A) calls Wing-yan (B) to see what she is doing. B is slacking off in her
room while Ka-ho is busy working.

```dialog
A: 喂[wai2|hey! / hello]，Wing-yan！ 你[nei5|you] 做[zou6|to do] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 咩嘢[me1je5|what kind of thing] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Hey, Wing-yan! What are you doing right now?
B: 我[ngo5|I] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 房[fong2|room] 入面[jap6min6|inside] Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！ 你[nei5|you] 呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]？
   === I'm chilling out inside my room, obviously! How about you?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 返[faan1|to return] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 工[gung1|job] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，好[hou2|good / very] 忙[mong4|busy] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
   === I'm working right now, I'm very busy!
B: 吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]？ 依家[ji1gaa1|now] 幾點[gei2dim2|what time] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === What? What time is it now?
A: 依家[ji1gaa1|now] 下晝[haa6zau3|in the afternoon] 四[sei3|four] 點[dim2|o'clock] 半[bun3|half] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 我[ngo5|I] 仲[zung6|still / yet] 做[zou6|to do] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 嘢[je5|thing] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === It's 4:30 in the afternoon already! I'm still doing work.
B: 噉[gam2|like this / then] 你[nei5|you] 慢慢[maan6maan6|slowly] 做[zou6|to do] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]，我[ngo5|I] 繼續[gai3zuk6|to continue] Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]，拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Well, take your time working then. I'll continue chilling, bye-bye!
A: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
```

## 5. Exercises

Test your understanding of the continuous aspect suffix and slang `Hea`:

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank to say "He is reading a book right now":
  佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 睇[tai2|to look / watch] ________ 書[syu1|book] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
answer: 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]
explanation: The continuous aspect suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` attaches directly to the verb `睇[tai2|to look / watch]` to show the action is in progress.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into colloquial Cantonese: "We are slacking off/chilling out right now."
  A) 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
  B) 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
  C) 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 休息[jau1sik1|to rest] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
answer: B
explanation: |
  "Chilling out/slacking off right now" combines the slang verb `Hea[hea3|to chill / slack off]` with the continuous aspect suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]`. Option B is correct.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Correct the error in the sentence "He is working right now":
  佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
answer: 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 返[faan1|to return] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 工[gung1|job] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
explanation: |
  `返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]` is a verb-object compound verb. The aspect suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` must split the compound and be placed directly between the verb `返[faan1|to return]` and the object `工[gung1|job]`.
```
