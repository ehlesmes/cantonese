## 1. Currency & Spoken Prices in Cantonese

In daily Cantonese life, handling money and pricing is highly colloquial and
differs significantly from formal written Chinese. When asking for prices,
instead of the formal `幾多錢[gei2 do1 cin2|how much money]`, you will
frequently hear speakers use the shorter, snappier
`幾錢[gei2 cin2|how much money]` in casual street environments.

### The Units of Currency

To talk about currency, spoken Cantonese employs unique units:

- `蚊[man1|dollar]`: The standard spoken unit of currency (equivalent to the
  written Hong Kong dollar symbol).
- `毫[hou4|ten cents / dime]`: Used for ten-cent intervals.
- `毫子[hou4 zi2|ten-cent coin / dime]`: The physical coin of ten cents.
- `分[fan1|cent / minute]`: Used for single cents (though rarely seen in modern
  transactions).

### Spoken Pricing Shortcuts

Cantonese speakers love efficiency, which shows in pricing shortcuts:

- `個[go3|general measure word]` represents a dollar when followed by decimal
  values. The most common is `半[bun3|half]` ($1.5 is
  `個半[go3bun3|one and a half]`), but it applies to others like
  `個二[go3ji6|one point two]` ($1.2) or `個六[go3luk6|one point six]` ($1.6).
  Note that for values of $2 and above, the number must precede
  `個[go3|measure word]` (e.g., $2.5 is
  `兩個半[loeng5 go3 bun3|two and a half]`, $3.5 is
  `三個半[saam1 go3 bun3|three and a half]`).
- When counting two of a currency unit, always use `兩[loeng5|two]` instead of
  `二[ji6|two]`. E.g., `` `兩[loeng5|two]` `` `` `蚊[man1|dollar]` `` ($2).
- When prices are in the hundreds or thousands, the final unit is often dropped
  if there are no smaller trailing digits. E.g., `$380` is commonly said as
  `三百八[saam1 baak3 baat3|three hundred and eight (tens)]` instead of the full
  `三百八十蚊[saam1baak3 baat3sap6 man1|380 dollars]`.

## 2. Essential Clothing & Shopping Classifiers

Using the correct classifier (measure word) is essential to sounding natural
when referring to different items in a shop or market. Avoid using
`個[go3|general measure word]` for everything! Here are the core classifiers for
shopping:

- `件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items]`: Used for upper-body
  clothing (shirts, jackets, coats).
  - Example: `` `呢[ni1|this]` ``
    `` `件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items]` ``
    `` `衫[saam1|clothes / clothing / shirt]` `` (this shirt)
- `條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects]`: Used for long, thin items,
  including pants, skirts, ties, and necklaces.
  - Example: `` `呢[ni1|this]` ``
    `` `條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects]` ``
    `` `褲[fu3|pants / trousers]` `` (this pants)
- `頂[deng2|measure word for hats]`: Exclusively used for hats and caps.
  - Example: `` `呢[ni1|this]` `` `` `頂[deng2|measure word for hats]` ``
    `` `帽[mou2|hat / cap]` `` (this hat)
- `對[deoi3|pair / couple]`: Used for paired items like shoes, socks, or
  earrings.
  - Example: `` `呢[ni1|this]` `` `` `對[deoi3|pair / couple]` ``
    `` `鞋[haai4|shoes]` `` (these shoes)
- `隻[zek3|measure word for animals or objects in pairs]`: Used to refer to just
  one item of a pair.
  - Example: `` `呢[ni1|this]` ``
    `` `隻[zek3|measure word for animals or objects in pairs]` ``
    `` `鞋[haai4|shoes]` `` (this single shoe)

## 3. The Art of Bargaining (`講價[gong2 gaa3|to bargain]`)

If you are shopping in Hong Kong’s vibrant street markets (like the Ladies'
Market in Mong Kok or Temple Street in Yau Ma Tei), bargaining
(`講價[gong2 gaa3|to bargain]`) is part of the fun and local culture.

Here are the key verbs and expressions you need to bargain effectively with the
`老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor]`:

- `平啲啦[peng4 di1 laa1|cheaper please]`: The ultimate gold-standard bargaining
  phrase. It uses `平[peng4|cheap]` with the comparative suffix `啲[di1|a bit]`
  and the softening particle `啦[laa1|particle]`.
- `減價[gaam2 gaa3|to reduce price / go on sale]`: To ask the boss to mark down
  the price.
- `特價[dat6 gaa3|special price / bargain price]`: Refer to items on a special
  bargain sale.
- `有折[jau5 zit3|to have a discount]` / `打折[daa2 zit3|to give a discount]`:
  Referring to discounts. E.g., asking if there is a discount
  (`折[zit3|discount]`).
- `送[sung3|to give as a gift / include for free]`: Ask for free additions or
  gifts (e.g., buy this and get that for free).
- `算啦[syun3 laa1|forget it / let it be]`: The ultimate walk-away strategy! If
  they won't budge on price, say `算啦[syun3 laa1|forget it / let it be]` and
  walk. They will often call you back with a lower price!

## 4. Situational Dialogues

### Dialogue 1: At a Clothing Market Stall in Mong Kok

A customer (`A`) set eyes on a pair of pants and tries to bargain with the stall
owner (`B`).

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me]，老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor]，呢[ni1|this]條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects]褲[fu3|pants / trousers]幾多錢[gei2 do1 cin2|how much money]呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Excuse me, boss, how much are these pants?
B: 呢[ni1|this]條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects]褲[fu3|pants / trousers]好[hou2|very]靚[leng3|high quality]嘅[ge3|final assertive particle]，三[saam1|three]百[baak3|hundred]八[baat3|eight]。
   === These pants are of very high quality, three hundred and eighty.
A: 三[saam1|three]百[baak3|hundred]八[baat3|eight]？鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely]貴[gwai3|expensive]！兩[loeng5|two]百[baak3|hundred]蚊[man1|dollar]得唔得[dak1 m4 dak1|can or not]呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Three hundred and eighty? Extremely expensive! Is two hundred dollars okay?
B: 兩[loeng5|two]百[baak3|hundred]蚊[man1|dollar]太[taai3|too]平[peng4|cheap]啦[laa1|particle]，賣[maai6|sell]唔到[m4dou2|cannot]！不如[bat1jyu4|how about]咁[gam3|so]啦[laa1|particle]，你[nei5|you]買[maai5|buy]埋[maai4|also]呢[ni1|this]件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items]衫[saam1|clothes / clothing / shirt]，我[ngo5|I]俾[bei2|give]個[go3|general measure word]折[zit3|discount]你[nei5|you]。兩[loeng5|two]件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items]三[saam1|three]百[baak3|hundred]二[ji6|two]啦[laa1|particle]！
   === Two hundred dollars is too cheap, I can't sell it! How about this: if you also buy this shirt, I'll give you a discount. Two pieces for three hundred and twenty then!
A: 好啦[hou2 laa1|okay]！你[nei5|you]真係[zan1hai6|really]好[hou2|very]叻[lek1|smart]講價[gong2 gaa3|to bargain]！
   === Okay! You are really smart at bargaining!
```

### Dialogue 2: Checking Out at the Cashier

The customer (`A`) heads to the cash counter to settle the payment with the
cashier (`B`).

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me]，收銀[sau1 ngan2|cashier]喺[hai2|at]邊度[bin1dou6|where]呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Excuse me, where is the cashier?
B: 收銀[sau1 ngan2|cashier]喺[hai2|at]呢度[ni1dou6|here]。你[nei5|you]想[soeng2|want to]點樣[dim2joeng6|how]俾[bei2|give]錢[cin2|money]？
   === The cashier is over here. How would you like to pay?
A: 用[jung6|to use]八達通[baat3daat6tung1|Octopus card]得唔得[dak1 m4 dak1|can or not]呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Is it okay to use an Octopus card?
B: 可以[ho2ji5|can]呀[aa3|particle]。碌卡[luk1 ka1|swipe credit card]都[dou1|also]得[dak1|capability]嘅[ge3|final assertive particle]。
   === Yes, you can. Swiping a credit card is also fine.
A: 噉[gam2|then]我[ngo5|I]碌卡[luk1 ka1|swipe credit card]啦[laa1|particle]。
   === In that case, I will swipe a credit card.
B: 好[hou2|okay]，呢個[ni1go3|this one]係[hai6|is]你[nei5|your]嘅[ge3|possessive particle]單[daan1|bill / receipt / sheet]。多謝[do1ze6|thank you]晒[saai3|entirely]！
   === Okay, this is your receipt. Thank you very much!
```

## 5. Exercises

Use these exercises to test your understanding of currency, classifiers, and
bargaining in Cantonese.

```exercise
question: |
  Which classifier should you use to ask for the price of a pair of shoes?
  A) 件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items]
  B) 條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects]
  C) 對[deoi3|pair / couple]
  D) 頂[deng2|measure word for hats]
answer: C
explanation: 對[deoi3|pair / couple] is the standard classifier for paired items like shoes or socks. 件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items] is for shirts, 條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects] is for pants/long thin objects, and 頂[deng2|measure word for hats] is for hats.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Complete the dialogue with the correct bargaining phrase meaning "cheaper, please":
  A: 呢[ni1|this]頂[deng2|measure word for hats]帽[mou2|hat / cap]好[hou2|very]貴[gwai3|expensive]呀[aa3|particle]！______ 得唔得[dak1 m4 dak1|can or not]呀[aa3|particle]？
answer: 平啲[peng4 di1|cheaper / a bit cheaper]
explanation: 平啲[peng4 di1|cheaper / a bit cheaper] is combined with the potential question 得唔得[dak1 m4 dak1|can or not] to ask "Can it be a bit cheaper?".
```

explanation: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me] means "excuse me", 呢[ni1|this] is "this", 條[tiu4|measure word for long, thin objects] is the classifier for trousers (褲[fu3|pants / trousers]), 幾錢[gei2 cin2|how much money] is the short spoken form of "how much money", and 呀[aa3|particle] is the softening sentence-final particle.
