In this chapter, we will explore the vocabulary and cultural structures of
Cantonese dating and relationships.

Navigating the world of romance and dating in Hong Kong involves a unique blend
of traditional colloquial Cantonese and highly specific modern student slang.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Modern Romance & Student Relationship Codes

To talk about relationships, spoken Cantonese uses several high-frequency verbs:

- `拍拖[paak3to1|to date]`: The standard spoken verb for dating someone.
- `單身[daan1san1|single]`: To be single.
- `分手[fan1sau2|to break up]`: To break up with a partner.
- `表白[biu2baak6|to confess one's love]`: To make a romantic confession to
  someone.

### Split-Verb Relationship Structures

Many romance verbs like `拍拖[paak3to1|to date]` and
`分手[fan1sau2|to break up]` are separable verbs (Verb-Object pairs). When
adding an aspect marker like `咗[zo2|completed action marker]`, it must go
between the two characters, not at the end:

- `拍拖[paak3to1|to date]` -> `拍[paak3|to clap]`
  `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` `拖[to1|to pull]`
- `分手[fan1sau2|to break up]` -> `分[fan1|minute / to divide]`
  `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` `手[sau2|hand]`

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 已經[ji5ging1|already] 分[fan1|minute / to divide] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 手[sau2|hand] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
===
We have already broken up.
```

### The University Relationship Status Codes

In Hong Kong university culture, students have developed a famous alphanumeric
coding system to describe their relationship statuses. The status is represented
by a letter indicating their status, followed by a number indicating the number
of romantic relationships they have experienced:

- **A (Available)**: Single.
- **O (Occupied)**: Currently in a relationship.
- **C (Complicated)**: In a complicated relationship status.

Combined with a number, you get unique slang terms:

- `A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated]`: Single and has never been in a
  relationship (a very common student self-descriptor).
- `O1[ou1jat1|in a relationship for the first time]`: In a relationship for the
  first time in their life.

When someone successfully transitions from being single to being in a
relationship, they use a highly popular student slang expression:

- `出[ceot1|to exit]` `Pool[pu1|singles pool (student slang)]`: Literally
  meaning "leaving the singles pool" (getting into a relationship).

## 2. Situational Dialogue: A Classmate "Out of Pool"

Two university friends, Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B), talk about their
classmate.

```dialog
A: 喂[wai2|hey! / hello]，你[nei5|you] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 去唔去[heoi3m4heoi3|go or not] 街[gaai1|street] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Hey, are you going out tomorrow?
B: 去街[heoi3gaai1|to go out]？ 我[ngo5|I] 單身[daan1san1|single] 㗎[gaa3|sentence-final particle indicating assertion] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！ 同[tung4|with / and] 邊個[bin1go3|who / which] 去[heoi3|go] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Go out? Obviously I am single! Who am I going with?
A: 聽講[ting1gong2|to hear of] David 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 表[biu2|to confess / show] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 白[baak6|clear / to explain]，成功[sing1gung1|successfully] 出[ceot1|to exit] Pool[pu1|singles pool (student slang)] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！
   === I heard David confessed yesterday and successfully left the singles pool!
B: 哇[waa1|wow]！ 真係[zan1hai6|really] 甜[tim4|sweet / romantic] 爆[baau3|extremely] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 終於[zung1jyu1|finally] 拍[paak3|to clap] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 拖[to1|to pull] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！
   === Wow! That's extremely sweet! He's finally dating someone!
B: 噉[gam2|like this / then] 我[ngo5|I] 都[dou1|also / all] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 努力[nou5lik6|to try hard] 出[ceot1|to exit] Pool[pu1|singles pool (student slang)] 先[sin1|first] 得[dak1|okay / can / ready] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！
   === In that case, I also need to work hard to leave the singles pool, obviously!
```

## 3. Exercises

Test your understanding of romance terminology, student slang, and relationships
in Cantonese.

```exercise
question: |
  Which status code represents a student who has never been in a romantic relationship before?
  A) O1[ou1jat1|in a relationship for the first time]
  B) A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated]
  C) C1[si1jat1|complicated first relationship]
  D) A2[ei1ji6|single after two relationships]
answer: B
explanation: |
  A0[ei1ling4|single and never dated] represents a student who is Available (single) and has experienced zero (0) relationships.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blanks to say "We have already broken up" in Cantonese:
  我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 已經[ji5ging1|already] ________ 咗[zo2|completed action marker] ________ 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
answer: 分[fan1|minute / to divide] / 手[sau2|hand]
explanation: |
  The verb `分手[fan1sau2|to break up]` is a split-verb. To form the completed aspect, the marker `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` is placed between `分[fan1|minute / to divide]` and `手[sau2|hand]`.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into Cantonese with correct annotations:
  "He confessed yesterday."
answer: 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 表[biu2|to confess / show] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 白[baak6|clear / to explain]。
explanation: |
  The verb `表白[biu2baak6|to confess one's love]` is a split-verb. To say "confessed", the completion aspect marker `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` is placed between `表[biu2|to confess / show]` and `白[baak6|clear / to explain]`.
```
