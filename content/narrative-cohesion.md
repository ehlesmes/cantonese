Storytelling and conversation are not just series of isolated sentences. To
sound like a natural Cantonese speaker, you need to connect your thoughts
smoothly, express logical relationships, highlight contrasts, and build
arguments.

In this chapter, we will master **Narrative Cohesion** using six essential pairs
and groups of colloquial Cantonese connectives:

1. **Cause & Effect**: `因為[jan1wai6|because]` ... `所以[so2ji5|therefore]`
2. **Concession & Contrast**: `雖然[seoi1jin4|although]` ...
   `但係[daan6hai6|but]`
3. **Incremental Addition**: `唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only]` ...
   `仲[zung6|still / yet]`
4. **Extreme Emphasizers & Transitions**:
   - `甚至[sam6zi3|even]`
   - `既然[gei3jin4|since]`
   - `反而[faan2ji4|instead]`

We will also see how Sau-han handles getting
`炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire]` `咗[zo2|completed action marker]`
`魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid]`, and how she uses these connectives to tell a cohesive,
engaging story.

---

## 1. Cause & Effect: `因為[jan1wai6|because]` ... `所以[so2ji5|therefore]`

To explain why something happened and what the result was, Cantonese speakers
use the classic pair `因為[jan1wai6|because]` ... `所以[so2ji5|therefore]`.
While English speakers rarely use "because" and "so" in the same sentence, in
Cantonese it is extremely common and natural to use both together.

Structure:

- `因為[jan1wai6|because]` [Cause], `所以[so2ji5|therefore]` [Result]

Let's look at some daily examples:

```cantonese
因為[jan1wai6|because] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 琴日[kam4jat6|yesterday (variant)] 唔開心[m4hoi1sam1|sad]， 所以[so2ji5|therefore] 去[heoi3|go] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 玩[waan2|to play]。
===
Because she was sad yesterday, therefore she went to have fun.
```

Another example:

```cantonese
因為[jan1wai6|because] 我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 想[soeng2|to want to] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]， 所以[so2ji5|therefore] 打算[daa2syun3|to plan] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a day off]。
===
Because I don't want to go to work, I plan to take a holiday.
```

## 2. Concession & Contrast: `雖然[seoi1jin4|although]` ... `但係[daan6hai6|but]`

When you want to contrast two ideas ("although A, but B"), you use
`雖然[seoi1jin4|although]` ... `但係[daan6hai6|but]`. Similar to the
cause-and-effect pair, Cantonese speakers frequently use both parts in the same
sentence to frame the contrast clearly.

Structure:

- `雖然[seoi1jin4|although]` [Concession], `但係[daan6hai6|but]`
  [Contrast/Result]

Let's look at a daily example:

```cantonese
雖然[seoi1jin4|although] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 啲[di1|a bit / some] 嘢食[je5sik6|food] 好[hou2|good / very] 靚[leng3|beautiful]， 但係[daan6hai6|but] 真係[zan1hai6|really] 好[hou2|good / very] 貴[gwai3|expensive] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！
===
Although the food here is very beautiful, it is really expensive, obviously!
```

Another example:

```cantonese
雖然[seoi1jin4|although] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 畀人[bei2jan4|by someone (passive marker)] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid]， 但係[daan6hai6|but] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 都[dou1|also / all] 好[hou2|good / very] 開心[hoi1sam1|happy] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
Although she got fired, she is still very happy.
```

## 3. Incremental Addition: `唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only]` ... `仲[zung6|still / yet]`

To add emphasis by listing multiple facts ("not only A, but also B"), Cantonese
uses `唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only]` paired with `仲[zung6|still / yet]`. This
construction builds narrative momentum, making your description sound highly
engaging and expressive.

Structure:

- `唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only]` [Fact A], `仲[zung6|still / yet]` [Fact B]
  `添[tim1|particle indicating addition]`

Let's look at an example:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only] 有錢[jau5cin2|rich]， 仲[zung6|still / yet] 好[hou2|good / very] 識[sik1|to know how to] 玩[waan2|to play] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]！
===
Not only is he rich, he also really knows how to have fun!
```

Another example:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only] 想[soeng2|to want to] 去[heoi3|go] 旅行[leoi5hang4|to travel]， 仲[zung6|still / yet] 想[soeng2|to want to] 買[maai5|buy] 書[syu1|book] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]。
===
Not only do I want to travel, but I also want to buy books as well.
```

## 4. Extreme Emphasizers & Transitions: `甚至[sam6zi3|even]`, `既然[gei3jin4|since]`, `反而[faan2ji4|instead]`, `跟住[gan1zyu6|and then]`, `其實[kei4sat6|actually]`

To elevate your narrative fluency to an advanced level, you must master
transitional and logical words that highlight extreme circumstances, logical
consequences, sequential timelines, and unexpected outcomes:

- **Extreme Emphasizer**: `甚至[sam6zi3|even]` is used to introduce an extreme
  case or build up to a surprising point.
- **Logical Consequence**: `既然[gei3jin4|since]` establishes a premise that
  justifies a subsequent action ("since it is already the case that..."). It is
  almost always paired with `就[zau6|then]` or `噉[gam2|like this / then]`
  `就[zau6|then]` in the resulting clause.
- **Unexpected Contrast**: `反而[faan2ji4|instead]` highlights a result that is
  the opposite of what was expected.
- **Narrative Sequence**: `跟住[gan1zyu6|and then]` is a high-frequency spoken
  connective used to sequence chronological events (equivalent to "and then").
- **Clarification / Reality check**: `其實[kei4sat6|actually]` is used to
  introduce an underlying reality, a concession, or to clarify a point.

**Note on Placement**:

- `甚至[sam6zi3|even]`, `反而[faan2ji4|instead]`, and `其實[kei4sat6|actually]`
  usually come **after** the subject of the clause (though
  `其實[kei4sat6|actually]` can also start a sentence).
- `跟住[gan1zyu6|and then]` is placed at the beginning of the sequenced clause.
- `既然[gei3jin4|since]` is placed at the beginning of the premise clause,
  either before or immediately after the subject.

Let's see how each is used:

### 1. Extreme Emphasizer: `甚至[sam6zi3|even]`

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 好[hou2|good / very] 唔開心[m4hoi1sam1|sad]， 甚至[sam6zi3|even] 喊[haam3|to cry] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
She was very sad, she even cried!
```

```cantonese
天氣[tin1hei3|weather] 好[hou2|good / very] 熱[jit6|hot]， 甚至[sam6zi3|even] 有[jau5|to have / there is] 三十[saam1sap6|thirty] 度[dou6|degree / place] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
The weather is very hot, it is even 30 degrees!
```

### 2. Logical Consequence: `既然[gei3jin4|since]`

```cantonese
既然[gei3jin4|since] 你[nei5|you] 唔[m4|not] 想[soeng2|to want to] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]， 噉[gam2|like this / then] 就[zau6|then] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a day off] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
Since you don't want to go to work, then in that case just take a holiday!
```

```cantonese
既然[gei3jin4|since] 大家[daai6gaa1|everyone] 都[dou1|also / all] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 呢度[ni1dou6|here]， 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 就[zau6|then] 食飯[sik6faan6|to eat a meal] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
Since everyone is here, then let's eat!
```

### 3. Unexpected Contrast: `反而[faan2ji4|instead]`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 原本[jyun4bun2|originally] 諗住[nam2zyu6|to intend] 去[heoi3|go] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]， 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 反而[faan2ji4|instead] 叫[giu3|to be called / call] 我[ngo5|I] 去[heoi3|go] 玩[waan2|to play]。
===
I originally intended to go to work, but he instead told me to go have fun.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 以為[ji5wai4|to mistakenly think] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 會[wui5|will / can] 嬲[nau1|angry]， 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 反而[faan2ji4|instead] 幫[bong1|to help] 我[ngo5|I]。
===
I assumed he would be angry, but instead he helped me.
```

### 4. Narrative Sequence: `跟住[gan1zyu6|and then]`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 去[heoi3|go] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 買[maai5|buy] 衫[saam1|clothes]， 跟住[gan1zyu6|and then] 去[heoi3|go] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 飲茶[jam2caa4|to have dim sum]。
===
I went to buy clothes, and then went to drink tea.
```

### 5. Clarification & Fact Clarification: `其實[kei4sat6|actually]`

```cantonese
其實[kei4sat6|actually] 我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 唔開心[m4hoi1sam1|sad]， 我[ngo5|I] 只係[zi2hai6|only] 攰[gui6|tired] 啫[ze1|only / just]。
===
Actually, I am not sad, I am only tired.
```

## 5. Situational Dialogue: Sau-han's Big Exploits

Wing-kit (A) meets Sau-han (B) at a local cafe during working hours. He is
surprised to see her slacking off on a weekday.

```dialog
A: 雖然[seoi1jin4|although] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 星期六[sing1kei4luk6|Saturday]， 但係[daan6hai6|but] 你[nei5|you] 都[dou1|also / all] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 嘅[ge2|sentence-final particle expressing doubt, curiosity, or surprise]？ 點解[dim2gaai2|why] 你[nei5|you] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] Hea[hea1|to chill / slack off] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Although today is not Saturday, you are still not working? Why are you chilling here?
B: 因為[jan1wai6|because] 我[ngo5|I] 公司[gung1si1|company] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 我[ngo5|I] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！
   === Because my company fired me, obviously!
A: 吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]？！ 炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？ 你[nei5|you] 原本[jyun4bun2|originally] 唔開心[m4hoi1sam1|sad] 諗住[nam2zyu6|to intend] 喊[haam3|to cry] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === What?! Fired? Were you originally sad and planning to cry?
B: 原本[jyun4bun2|originally] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 有[jau5|to have / there is] 啲[di1|a bit / some] 唔開心[m4hoi1sam1|sad] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle]。 但係[daan6hai6|but] 我[ngo5|I] 唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only] 冇[mou5|to not have] 喊[haam3|to cry]， 反而[faan2ji4|instead] 好[hou2|good / very] 開心[hoi1sam1|happy] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]！
   === Originally I was indeed a bit sad. But not only did I not cry, on the contrary, I am very happy!
A: 點解[dim2gaai2|why] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Why?
B: 既然[gei3jin4|since] 我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 使[sai2|to need / use] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]， 噉[gam2|like this / then] 我[ngo5|I] 就[zau6|then] 可以[ho2ji5|can] 去[heoi3|go] 旅行[leoi5hang4|to travel] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！ 我[ngo5|I] 甚至[sam6zi3|even] 諗住[nam2zyu6|to intend] 去[heoi3|go] 香港[hoeng1gong2|Hong Kong] 玩[waan2|to play] 兩[loeng5|two] 個[go3|general classifier] 星期[sing1kei4|week] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]！
   === Since I don't have to work, then I can go travel, obviously! I even intend to go to Hong Kong to play for two weeks!
A: 哇[waa1|wow]！ 你[nei5|you] 唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only] 好似[hou2ci5|seems like] 有錢[jau5cin2|rich] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]， 仲[zung6|still / yet] 好[hou2|good / very] 識[sik1|to know how to] Hea[hea1|to chill / slack off] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]！
   === Wow! Not only do you seem to have become rich, but you also really know how to chill!
```

## 6. Exercises

Test your understanding of advanced Cantonese connectives and narrative flow.

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into Cantonese using correct semantic annotations:
  "Although it is raining, I still want to go swimming."
  (Hint: Use seoi1jin4 for "although", daan6hai6 for "but", dou1 for "still/also", and jau4seoi2 for "to swim")
answer: 雖然[seoi1jin4|although] 落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]， 但係[daan6hai6|but] 我[ngo5|I] 都[dou1|also / all] 想[soeng2|to want to] 去[heoi3|go] 游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]。
explanation: |
  The concession pattern is `雖然[seoi1jin4|although] ... 但係[daan6hai6|but]`. `落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]` is "to rain", `我[ngo5|I]` is "I", `都[dou1|also / all]` is "also / both / all", `想[soeng2|to want to]` is "want to", `去[heoi3|go]` is "go", and `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]` is "to swim".
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct connective meaning "not only" in Cantonese:
  佢[keoi5|he / she / it] ________ 有錢[jau5cin2|rich]， 仲[zung6|still / yet] 好[hou2|good / very] 識[sik1|to know how to] 玩[waan2|to play] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]！
answer: 唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only]
explanation: |
  The incremental addition pattern is `唔單止[m4daan1zi2|not only] ... 仲[zung6|still / yet] ... 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]`. It is used to express "not only... but also... as well".
```

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following connectives is the most appropriate to express a logical consequence based on a given premise (equivalent to "since / given that")?
  A) 雖然[seoi1jin4|although]
  B) 既然[gei3jin4|since]
  C) 反而[faan2ji4|instead]
answer: B
explanation: |
  `既然[gei3jin4|since]` is used to introduce a premise that justifies a logical consequence ("since... then..."). `雖然[seoi1jin4|although]` introduces concession ("although"), and `反而[faan2ji4|instead]` indicates an unexpected contrast ("instead").
```
