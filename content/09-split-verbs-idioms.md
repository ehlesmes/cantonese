Welcome to Chapter 9! In this chapter, we will explore one of the most
structurally unique and culturally vibrant areas of Colloquial Cantonese:
**Split-Verbs** and **Native Idioms**.

First, we will study **Split-Verbs** (linguistically known as _separable verbs_
or _lei-hap-ci_ in Cantonese). These words translate as simple single verbs in
English (e.g. "to swim" or "to sing"), but structurally they are compound
verb-object pairs. To express ongoing actions, past completions, or duration,
Cantonese speakers literally split these compounds in half and insert grammar
markers directly between them.

Second, we will learn some of the most widely used and colorful Cantonese
**Native Idioms** (`俗語[zuk1jyu5|colloquial expression / slang / idiom]`). Many
of these idioms are structurally split-verbs, which means they follow the exact
same grammatical rules of separation!

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Structuring Split-Verbs: Insert in the Middle

In Cantonese, split-verbs are composed of two parts:

1. A **Verb Part** (the action)
2. An **Object Part** (the target of the action)

For example, `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]` is made of `游[jau4|to swim]` and
`水[seoi2|water]`. Similarly, `唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing]` is made of
`唱[coeng3|to sing]` and `歌[go1|song]`.

When you want to attach **aspect markers**—such as completed
`咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)]`,
continuous
`緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)]`,
or experiential
`過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before']`—you
must **split the compound** and insert the marker between the two parts:

```
[Verb Part] + [Aspect Marker] + [Object Part]
```

Let's see how standard split-verbs behave when modified:

### 1. Swimming: `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]`

- Verb Part: `游[jau4|to swim]`
- Object Part: `水[seoi2|water]`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 水[seoi2|water] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle indicating a change of state or already]。
===
I swam yesterday already.
```

```cantonese
佢哋[keoi5dei6|they / them] 游[jau4|to swim] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 水[seoi2|water] 喎[wo3|sentence-final particle indicating reported speech, surprise, or warning]！
===
Apparently, they are swimming right now!
```

### 2. Singing: `唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing]`

- Verb Part: `唱[coeng3|to sing]`
- Object Part: `歌[go1|song]`

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 聽[teng1|to listen / hear] 吓[haa2|what / sentence-final particle expressing surprise, disbelief, or seeking quick confirmation]！ 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唱[coeng3|to sing] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 歌[go1|song] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
Take a listen! She is singing a song!
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 唔[m4|not / no (negative prefix)] 識[sik1|to know / be familiar with / speak (a language)] 唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！
===
Obviously, I don't know how to sing! (No split is needed for base dictionary infinitive forms).
```

### 3. Dancing & Jogging: `跳舞[tiu3mou5|to dance]` and `跑步[paau2bou6|to run / jog]`

- `跳舞[tiu3mou5|to dance]` splits into `跳[tiu3|to jump / leap / dance]` and
  `舞[mou5|dance]`.
- `跑步[paau2bou6|to run / jog]` splits into `跑[paau2|to run]` and
  `步[bou6|step / pace]`.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 去[heoi3|to go] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 跑步[paau2bou6|to run / jog] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I went jogging. (Note: Here, "jogging" is a noun phrase following "went," so no split is needed inside the compound).
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 跑[paau2|to run] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 步[bou6|step / pace] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！ 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 先[sin1|first / only then] 傾[king1|to talk / chat] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
I am jogging right now! Let's chat tomorrow instead!
```

### 4. Asking A-not-A Questions with Split-Verbs

When forming **A-not-A questions** (Verb-not-Verb) with split-verbs, you must
only duplicate the **verb part**, not the entire compound. It is a very common
learner mistake to say `游水唔游水[jau4seoi2 m4 jau4seoi2|swim or not swim]`
(swim-water-not-swim-water)—this is incorrect!

The correct structure is to repeat the verb part, and leave the object part at
the end:

```
[Verb Part] + 唔 + [Verb Part] + [Object Part]
```

However, if you are asking about past completion or past experience involving an
aspect marker (like `咗[zo2|completed]` or `過[gwo3|past experience]`), you
generally cannot use the A-not-A verb duplication. Instead, use
`有冇[jau5 mou5|have or have not]`. For example, `游過水[jau4gwo3seoi2|swum]`
becomes `有冇游過水[jau5mou5jau4gwo3seoi2|have you swum]`, not
`游唔游過水[jau4m4jau4gwo3seoi2|have you swum]`.

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 游唔游[jau4 m4 jau4|do you swim (A-not-A question form)] 水[seoi2|water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Are you swimming today?
```

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 今晚[gam1maan5|tonight] 唱唔唱[coeng3 m4 coeng3|do you sing (A-not-A question form)] 歌[go1|song] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Are you singing tonight?
```

## 2. Modifying Split-Verbs: Time, Duration & Frequency

When you want to describe **how long** (duration) or **how many times**
(frequency) you performed a split-verb action, the description of time or
frequency must also split the verb and be placed between the two halves:

```
[Verb Part] + [Aspect Marker] + [Duration / Frequency] + [Object Part]
```

### 1. Specifying Duration

To say you performed an action for "one hour" (`一[jat1|one]`
`個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` `鐘頭[zung1tau4|hour]`),
place the duration immediately after the verb part:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 鐘頭[zung1tau4|hour] 水[seoi2|water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He swam for two hours.
```

### 2. Specifying Frequency

To say you performed an action "three times" (`三[saam1|three]`
`次[ci3|time / occurrence (classifier)]`), insert the frequency after the verb
part:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 唱[coeng3|to sing] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 三[saam1|three] 次[ci3|time / occurrence (classifier)] 歌[go1|song] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle indicating a change of state or already]！
===
I have sung three times already!
```

## 3. Spoken Cantonese Native Idioms

Many of the most popular colloquial expressions
(`俗語[zuk1jyu5|colloquial expression / slang / idiom]`) used by Hong Kongers in
daily life are structurally split-verbs. Let's study five of the most expressive
idioms:

### 1. Chatting & Gossiping: `吹水[ceoi1seoi2|to chat / shoot the breeze / gossip]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Blowing water"
- **Colloquial usage**: Chattering, hanging out, or gossiping casually.
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 喺[hai2|at / in / on (locative preposition)] 茶餐廳[caa4caan1teng1|Hong Kong style café / diner] 吹[ceoi1|to blow / boast] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 水[seoi2|water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
We are shooting the breeze at the Hong Kong style café.
```

### 2. Pretending or Showing Off: `扮嘢[baan6je5|to pretend / show off / act]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Dressing up things"
- **Colloquial usage**: Pretending, showing off, playing dumb, or acting like
  someone you're not.
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 扮[baan6|to dress up / act / pretend] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 嘢[je5|thing / stuff / matter] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
===
He is really just pretending / showing off, obviously!
```

### 3. Standing Someone Up: `放飛機[fong3fei1gei1|to stand someone up / break a promise]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Releasing an airplane"
- **Colloquial usage**: Standing someone up, failing to show up to an agreed
  meeting or appointment.
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 又[jau6|again] 放[fong3|to release / let go / put] 我[ngo5|I / me] 飛機[fei1gei1|airplane] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
===
Hey, you stood me up again today! (Friendly or direct warning!)
```

### 4. Sitting Back and Watching Drama: `食花生[sik6faa1sang1|to sit back and watch drama / spectate]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Eating peanuts"
- **Colloquial usage**: Staying out of a conflict or dramatic situation, just
  sitting back to enjoy the drama unfold (like eating popcorn at a movie).
- **Splitting example**:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 喺[hai2|at / in / on (locative preposition)] 隔離[gaak3lei4|next to / beside / neighbor] 食[sik6|to eat] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 花生[faa1sang1|peanut] 啫[ze1|sentence-final particle meaning 'only', 'just', or downplaying something]。
===
I am just sitting back eating peanuts (watching the drama unfold next to me).
```

### 5. Getting Fired: `炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired / lay off / fire someone]`

- **Literal meaning**: "Stir-frying squid" (historically, when employees were
  fired from shops in Hong Kong, they had to pack up their sleeping mats, which
  rolled up like a curled, stir-fried squid).
- **Colloquial usage**: Getting fired or firing someone.
- **Splitting example**: When you want to say "the boss fired him," you split
  the verb `炒魷魚[caau2jau4jyu2|to get fired / lay off / fire someone]` and
  insert the pronoun object (`佢[keoi5|he / she / it]`) in the middle:

```cantonese
老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
===
Whoa, the boss fired him already!
```

### 6. Four-Character Idioms & Color Terms

Cantonese also uses `四字俗語[sei3zi6zuk1jyu5|four-character idioms]` and
color-based idioms to express vivid imagery:

- `人山人海[jan4saan1jan4hoi2|crowded / huge crowds of people]` (Mountains of
  people, seas of people): Used to describe a very crowded place.
- `黑面[hak1min6|to have a dark expression / angry face]` (Black face): To look
  visibly angry or annoyed.

```cantonese
旺角[wong6gok3|Mong Kok (place in Hong Kong)] 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 人山人海[jan4saan1jan4hoi2|crowded / huge crowds of people] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
Mong Kok is truly packed with people!
```

```cantonese
老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 黑[hak1|black / dark / bad luck] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 面[min6|face / surface] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
===
The boss is showing a dark/angry face right now! (Note how "black face" can split like a verb!)
```

## 4. Situational Dialogue: A Weekend Standup & Office Gossip

Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B) are talking on Saturday about their weekend plans
and gossiping about what happened to their colleague.

```dialog
A: Wing-kit，你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 唔[m4|not / no (negative prefix)] 係[hai6|to be (am/is/are) / yes] 去[heoi3|to go] 游水[jau4seoi2|to swim] 咩[me1|sentence-final particle expressing rhetorical doubt, surprise, or sarcasm]？
   === Wing-kit, aren't you going swimming today?
B: 我[ngo5|I / me] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 已經[ji5ging1|already] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 水[seoi2|water] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 今日[gam1jat6|today] 我[ngo5|I / me] 想[soeng2|to want to / would like to / think] 同[tung4|with / same as / and] 佢哋[keoi5dei6|they / them] 一齊[jat1cai4|together] 吹水[ceoi1seoi2|to chat / shoot the breeze / gossip] 啫[ze1|sentence-final particle meaning 'only', 'just', or downplaying something]。
   === I already swam yesterday! Today I just want to shoot the breeze together with them.
A: 但係[daan6hai6|but / however] 佢哋[keoi5dei6|they / them] 放[fong3|to release / let go / put] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 你[nei5|you] 飛機[fei1gei1|airplane] 喎[wo3|sentence-final particle indicating reported speech, surprise, or warning]！ 佢哋[keoi5dei6|they / them] 唔[m4|not / no (negative prefix)] 喺[hai2|at / in / on (locative preposition)] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 喎[wo3|sentence-final particle indicating reported speech, surprise, or warning]！
   === But they stood you up! Apparently, they are not here!
B: 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 咩[me1|sentence-final particle expressing rhetorical doubt, surprise, or sarcasm]？ 佢哋[keoi5dei6|they / them] 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 衰[seoi1|bad / mean / wicked] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！ 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 我[ngo5|I / me] 去[heoi3|to go] 睇書[tai2syu1|to read books] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle indicating a change of state or already]。
   === Really? They are so mean! In that case, I am going to read books now.
A: 你[nei5|you] 知[zi1|to know / be aware of] 唔[m4|not / no (negative prefix)] 知道[zi1dou3|to know / be aware of] 老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor] 炒[caau2|to stir-fry / speculate / fire] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] David 魷魚[jau4jyu2|squid] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Do you know that the boss fired David already?
B: 哇[waa1|wow / whoa]！ 真係[zan1hai6|really / truly] 咩[me1|sentence-final particle expressing rhetorical doubt, surprise, or sarcasm]？ 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 遲到[ci4dou3|to be late / arrive late] 咗[zo2|verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)] 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 多[do1|many / much / more] 次[ci3|time / occurrence (classifier)] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！ 我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 食[sik6|to eat] 緊[gan2|verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect)] 花生[faa1sang1|peanut] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！
   === Whoa! Really? Well, he was late so many times, obviously! Looks like we are sitting back watching the drama unfold now!
```
