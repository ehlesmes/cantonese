In this chapter, we will learn about **Gathering Plans & Social Slang**.

Making plans and managing social gatherings comes with its own local slang in
Hong Kong.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Gathering Plans & Shorthand

Here is the essential vocabulary for organizing and describing social events:

- `局[guk6|gathering]`: A gathering, social plan, or get-together (e.g., a
  drinking or dinner plan).
- `直落[zik6lok6|to continue onto another venue]`: To continue to another venue
  or the next round of a night out without stopping.
- `下場[haa6coeng4|afterparty]`: The next venue, round, or afterparty of the
  night (literally "next scene").
- `放飛機[fong3fei1gei1|to stand someone up]`: To stand someone up, cancel plans
  last minute, or fail to show up.
- `飛機王[fei1gei1wong4|chronic flake]`: A chronic flake; someone who habitually
  stands people up.
- `派對[paai3deoi3|party]`: A party or festive social gathering.
- `吹水[ceoi1seoi2|to chat / gossip]`: To chat, shoot the breeze, or gossip with
  friends.
- `潛水[cim4seoi2|to go silent / lurk]`: To go offline, disappear from chat
  groups, or go silent (literally "to dive/snorkel").
- `得閒[dak1haan4|free (spare time)]`: Free, having spare time.
- `AA制[ei1ei1zai3|splitting the bill]`: Going Dutch or splitting the bill
  evenly among the group.

## 2. Situational Dialogue: Planning Saturday Night

Two friends, Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B), discuss their plans for Saturday.

```dialog
A: 星期六[sing1kei4luk6|Saturday] 你[nei5|you] 得閒[dak1haan4|free (spare time)] 唔[m4|not] 得閒[dak1haan4|free (spare time)] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 有[jau5|to have / there is] 個[go3|general classifier] 唱[coeng3|to sing]K 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle] 局[guk6|gathering] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === Are you free this Saturday? We have a karaoke gathering plan.
B: 唱[coeng3|to sing]K 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？好呀[hou2aa3|sure]！有[jau5|to have / there is] 邊個[bin1go3|who / which] 去[heoi3|go] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Karaoke? Sure! Who is going?
A: 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 差唔多[caa1m4do1|almost] 十[sap6|ten] 個[go3|general classifier] 人[jan4|person] 去[heoi3|go] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。不過[bat1gwo3|but] David 成日[seng4jat6|always] 放飛機[fong3fei1gei1|to stand someone up]，真係[zan1hai6|really] 飛機王[fei1gei1wong4|chronic flake] 嚟[lei4|to come / sentence-final particle] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle]！
   === Almost ten of us are going. But David always stands people up, he is really a chronic flake!
B: 哈哈[haa1haa1|haha]！噉[gam2|like this / then] 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 唱[coeng3|to sing] 完[jyun4|finished] 之後[zi1hau6|after] 會[wui5|will / can] 唔[m4|not] 會[wui5|will / can] 直落[zik6lok6|to continue onto another venue] 去[heoi3|go] 下場[haa6coeng4|afterparty] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Haha! Then after we finish singing, will we keep going straight to the next venue?
A: 唱[coeng3|to sing] 完[jyun4|finished] 都[dou1|also / all] 幾[gei2|quite] 晏[aan3|late]，或者[waak6ze2|perhaps / or] 去[heoi3|go] 食[sik6|to eat] 宵夜[siu1je2|late night snack] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]。
   === It will be quite late after singing, maybe we can go for a late night snack.
```

## 3. Exercises

Test your understanding of gathering terms and flaking slang in Cantonese.

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following refers to a person who habitually stands people up or cancels plans last minute?
  A) 飛機王[fei1gei1wong4|chronic flake]
  B) 派對[paai3deoi3|party]
  C) 吹水[ceoi1seoi2|to chat / gossip]
answer: A
explanation: |
  `飛機王[fei1gei1wong4|chronic flake]` comes from the phrase `放飛機[fong3fei1gei1|to stand someone up]` and describes a chronic flake.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Which term is used when friends decide to go Dutch or split the bill evenly?
  A) 直落[zik6lok6|to continue onto another venue]
  B) AA制[ei1ei1zai3|splitting the bill]
  C) 潛水[cim4seoi2|to go silent / lurk]
answer: B
explanation: |
  `AA制[ei1ei1zai3|splitting the bill]` is the standard colloquial term for splitting a bill.
```
