Now that you know how to greet people and make basic introductions, we will
learn how to negate statements, ask yes-no questions using the classic A-not-A
structure, and inquire about people's names and language skills.

---

## 1. Negation & A-not-A Questions

### Negating with `唔[m4|not]`

To negate `係[hai6|to be / yes]`, place the negative adverb `唔[m4|not]`
directly before it, forming `唔[m4|not]` `係[hai6|to be / yes]` ("is not / are
not"):

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 美國[mei5gwok3|United States] 人[jan4|person]。
===
I am not American.
```

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 靚仔[leng3zai2|handsome boy / plain white rice]。
===
He is not handsome.
```

> [!TIP] The particle
> `呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]`
> is frequently added to negative statements and declarations to soften the
> tone. For example, stating `我[ngo5|I]` `唔[m4|not]` `識[sik1|to know how to]`
> `講[gong2|to speak]` `廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language]`
> `呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]`
> sounds much friendlier and less abrupt than stating it without the particle.

### Asking Questions with `係唔係[hai6 m4 hai6|is it?]`

In Cantonese, the most natural way to ask yes-no questions is the **A-not-A
construction**, where you present the verb, the negative marker `唔[m4|not]`,
and the verb again. For the verb `係[hai6|to be / yes]`, this forms the compound
expression `係唔係[hai6 m4 hai6|is it?]`.

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 係唔係[hai6 m4 hai6|is it?] 英國[jing1gwok3|United Kingdom] 人[jan4|person] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Are you a British person?
```

> [!TIP] Notice that we add the friendly softening particle
> `呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]`
> to the end of the question. This softens the inquiry so it sounds like a warm,
> polite question rather than a cold interrogation.

## 2. Asking for Names & Languages

### Asking "What is your name?"

To ask someone's name, we use the verb `叫[giu3|to be called / call]`, the
pronoun `咩嘢[me1je5|what kind of thing]`, and the noun `名[meng2|name]`:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 叫[giu3|to be called / call] 咩嘢[me1je5|what kind of thing] 名[meng2|name] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
What is your name?
```

To answer, simply state: **`我[ngo5|I]` `叫[giu3|to be called / call]` [Name]**.
For example:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 叫[giu3|to be called / call] David。
===
My name is David.
```

### Speaking Languages with `識[sik1|to know how to]`

To express that you know how to speak a language, use the verb
`識[sik1|to know how to]` (which indicates a learned ability) paired with
`講[gong2|to speak]`:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 識[sik1|to know how to] 講[gong2|to speak] 廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
I know how to speak Cantonese!
```

To ask someone if they speak a language, apply the A-not-A pattern to the verb
`識[sik1|to know how to]`, creating `識唔識[sik1 m4 sik1|do you know how to?]`:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 識唔識[sik1 m4 sik1|do you know how to?] 講[gong2|to speak] 英文[jing1man4|English language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Do you know how to speak English?
```

> [!TIP] **Bounce-back Questions with
> `呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]`** To casually bounce a question
> back to someone (like saying "How about you?"), attach the query particle
> `呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]` to a pronoun. For example:
> `你[nei5|you]` `呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]`？means "How about
> you?".

## 3. Conversational Dialogue

Below is a typical conversation between two people meeting at a social event for
the first time.

```dialog
A: 你好[nei5hou2|hello]呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 我[ngo5|I] 叫[giu3|to be called / call] David。 你[nei5|you] 叫[giu3|to be called / call] 咩嘢[me1je5|what kind of thing] 名[meng2|name] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Hello there! My name is David. What is your name?
B: 你好[nei5hou2|hello]呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 我[ngo5|I] 叫[giu3|to be called / call] Mary。 呢個[ni1go3|this one] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 我[ngo5|I] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle] 細佬[sai3lou2|younger brother]，Tom。
   === Hello there! My name is Mary. This is my younger brother, Tom.
A: 你好[nei5hou2|hello]呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly] Tom！ Mary，你哋[nei5dei6|you (plural)] 係唔係[hai6 m4 hai6|is it?] 美國[mei5gwok3|United States] 人[jan4|person] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Hello there Tom! Mary, are you all American?
B: 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 美國[mei5gwok3|United States] 人[jan4|person] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 加拿大[gaa1naa4daai6|Canada] 人[jan4|person]。 你[nei5|you] 呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]？
   === We are not American, we are Canadian. How about you?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 美國[mei5gwok3|United States] 人[jan4|person]。 你哋[nei5dei6|you (plural)] 識唔識[sik1 m4 sik1|do you know how to?] 講[gong2|to speak] 廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === I am American. Do you all know how to speak Cantonese?
B: 我[ngo5|I] 識[sik1|to know how to] 講[gong2|to speak] 廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 我[ngo5|I] 嘅[ge3|possessive / linking particle] 細佬[sai3lou2|younger brother] 唔[m4|not] 識[sik1|to know how to]。你[nei5|you] 呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]？
   === I know how to speak Cantonese! My younger brother does not know. How about you?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 識[sik1|to know how to] 講[gong2|to speak] 廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，我[ngo5|I] 識[sik1|to know how to] 講[gong2|to speak] 英文[jing1man4|English language]。
   === I don't know how to speak Cantonese, I know how to speak English.
B: 唔緊要[m4gan2jiu3|it doesn't matter]！ 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 朋友[pang4jau5|friend] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Never mind! We are friends now!
A: 好[hou2|good / very] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Great! Bye-bye!
B: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
```

## 4. Exercises

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank to complete the question "Are you Canadian?":
  你[nei5|you] ________ 加拿大[gaa1naa4daai6|Canada] 人[jan4|person] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
answer: 係唔係[hai6 m4 hai6|is it?]
explanation: The A-not-A question form 係唔係[hai6 m4 hai6|is it?] is the standard and most natural way to ask yes-no questions in spoken Cantonese.
```

```exercise
question: Translate "We are not British." into colloquial Cantonese using proper annotations.
answer: 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 英國[jing1gwok3|United Kingdom] 人[jan4|person]。
explanation: 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] is the plural pronoun for "we/us", 唔[m4|not] negates the equative verb 係[hai6|to be / yes], and 英國[jing1gwok3|United Kingdom] paired with 人[jan4|person] means British person/people.
```
