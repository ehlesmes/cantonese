Welcome to Chapter 6! Now that you have learned how to tell the time and
describe ongoing actions, let's explore how to express what you _can_, _may_, or
_have experienced_ doing in Cantonese.

In Cantonese, the word "can" or "able to" is divided into several highly
specific verbs depending on whether you are talking about a **learned skill**,
**situational permission**, or a **future probability**. We will also learn how
to discuss your life experiences using the experiential aspect marker
`過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before']`,
and use the powerful verbal suffixes
`得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`
and `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]` to describe if an
action is physically doable or if you are unable to achieve it.

Let's dive in!

## 1. The Three Faces of "Can": `識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill]`, `可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]`, and `會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to]`

In English, we use the word "can" to cover many meanings. In Cantonese, you must
choose the correct modal verb based on the _type_ of capability.

### 1. Learned Skills and Abilities: `識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill]`

Use `識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill]` when "can" means you
**know how to do** something because you learned it. This is used for languages,
sports, driving, cooking, and academic subjects.

- `我[ngo5|I / me]` `識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill]`
  `講[gong2|to speak / say / talk]` `廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language]`
  — I can (know how to) speak Cantonese.
- `我[ngo5|I / me]` `識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill]`
  `揸車[zaa1ce1|to drive a car / drive]` — I can (know how to) drive a car.

To negate this, place `唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)]` before
`識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill]`:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)] 識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill] 寫[se2|to write] 英文[jing1man2|English language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I do not know how to write English.
```

### 2. Situational Permission and Possibility: `可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]`

Use `可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]` when "can" means you are **permitted**
to do something or it is **situationally possible** (e.g. schedules align).

- `你[nei5|you]` `可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]`
  `喺[hai2|at / in / on (locative preposition)]` `呢度[ni1dou6|here]`
  `飲[jam2|to drink]` `咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee (loanword)]` — You can (are allowed
  to) drink coffee here.
- `我[ngo5|I / me]` `今日[gam1jat6|today]` `可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]`
  `去[heoi3|to go]` `旅行[leoi5hang4|to travel]` — I can (am able to
  situationally) go travel today.

To negate permission or situational ability, place
`唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)]` before
`可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]`:

```cantonese
呢度[ni1dou6|here] 唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)] 可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible] 揸車[zaa1ce1|to drive a car / drive] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
===
You cannot drive a car here, obviously!
```

### 3. Future Likelihood and Tendencies: `會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to]`

Use `會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to]` to express **future actions**
("will") or an **inherent likelihood/tendency** that something will happen.

- `我[ngo5|I / me]` `聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow]`
  `會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to]` `嚟[lai4|to come]` — I will come
  tomorrow.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)] 會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to] 去[heoi3|to go] 日本[jat6bun2|Japan] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
I will not go to Japan.
```

## 2. Talking About Past Experience: Aspect Particle `過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before']`

To express that you **have done something before** at least once in your life
(the experiential aspect), Cantonese uses the particle
`過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before']`.

### The Experiential Structure

The particle
`過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before']`
is placed **directly after the main verb**:

```
[Subject] + [Verb] + 過 + [Object]
```

Let's look at some examples:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 去[heoi3|to go] 過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before'] 日本[jat6bun2|Japan] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I have been to Japan before.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 食[sik6|to eat] 過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before'] 壽司[sau6si1|sushi] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
===
I have eaten sushi before, obviously!
```

### Negating Experiences: `冇[mou5|to not have / there is not]`

To say that you **have never done** something, place
`冇[mou5|to not have / there is not]` before the verb, but **keep** the
`過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before']`
after the verb!

```
[Subject] + 冇 + [Verb] + 過 + [Object]
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 冇[mou5|to not have / there is not] 飲[jam2|to drink] 過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before'] 凍[dung3|cold / iced] 奶茶[naai5caa4|milk tea (typically Hong Kong-style)] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I have never drunk iced milk tea before.
```

To ask an experiential question ("Have you ever...?"), use the A-not-A style
question or standard question particles:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 去[heoi3|to go] 過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before'] 邊度[bin1dou6|where] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Where have you been before?
```

## 3. Physical Feasibility and Obstacles: Potential Suffixes `得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]` and `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]`

In daily spoken Cantonese, instead of using helper words like
`可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible]`, native speakers prefer to attach suffixes
directly to the end of verbs to show whether an action is physically possible.

### 1. Capability & Physical Feasibility: `得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`

When you attach
`得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`
directly to the end of a verb, it indicates that the action is **physically
doable, allowed, or ready**:

```
[Verb] + 得
```

- `食[sik6|to eat]`
  `得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`
  — edible / ready to eat. (e.g. `呢個[ni1go3|this one]` `食[sik6|to eat]`
  `得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`
  — This is ready to eat!)
- `去[heoi3|to go]`
  `得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`
  — ready to go / able to go.
- `行[hang4|to walk / go / run (a machine)]`
  `得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree]`
  — able to walk / ready to walk.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 依家[ji1gaa1|now / currently] 去[heoi3|to go] 得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
I can go now! / I am ready to go now!
```

### 2. Inability to Succeed or Manage: `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]`

If you want to say that you **cannot manage to** do something (due to physical
obstacles, lack of opportunity, or failure to achieve the result), attach
`唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]` directly to the verb:

```
[Verb] + 唔到
```

- `食[sik6|to eat]` `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]` —
  unable to eat (e.g., because you are full, allergic, or the restaurant is
  closed).
- `去[heoi3|to go]` `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]` —
  unable to go (e.g., because you are too busy or have no ticket).
- `睇[tai2|to look / see / watch / read]`
  `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]` — unable to see (e.g.,
  because someone is blocking you).
- `聽[teng1|to listen / hear / obey]`
  `唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to]` — unable to hear.

Let's look at how this sounds in context:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 去[heoi3|to go] 唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to] 日本[jat6bun2|Japan] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I cannot go to Japan tomorrow.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 聽[teng1|to listen / hear / obey] 唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to] 你[nei5|you] 講[gong2|to speak / say / talk] 咩嘢[me1je5|what / what kind of thing] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I cannot hear what you are saying.
```

## 4. Situational Dialogue: A Trip to Japan

In this conversation, Wing-kit (A) and Sau-han (B) are talking in a café about
their travel plans and cooking abilities. Pay close attention to how they use
modal verbs, past experience markers, and potential suffixes.

```dialog
A: 喂[wai2|hey / hello (typically answering phone or calling for attention)]，Sau-han！你[nei5|you] 去[heoi3|to go] 過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before'] 日本[jat6bun2|Japan] 未[mei6|not yet / have not] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Hey, Sau-han! Have you been to Japan before?
B: 我[ngo5|I / me] 去[heoi3|to go] 過[gwo3|verbal aspect suffix indicating past experience / 'have done before'] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 次[ci3|time / occurrence (classifier)] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。我[ngo5|I / me] 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 鍾意[zung1ji3|to like / prefer] 嗰度[go2dou6|there] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
   === I have been there twice. I really like it there, obviously!
A: 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 你[nei5|you] 識唔識[sik1 m4 sik1|do you know how to (A-not-A question form)] 講[gong2|to speak / say / talk] 日文[jat6man2|Japanese language] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === In that case, do you know how to speak Japanese?
B: 我[ngo5|I / me] 唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)] 識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill] 講[gong2|to speak / say / talk] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，我[ngo5|I / me] 只係[zi2hai6|only / just] 識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill] 講[gong2|to speak / say / talk] 廣東話[gwong2dung1waa2|Cantonese language] 同埋[tung4maai4|and / with / together with] 英文[jing1man2|English language] 啫[ze1|sentence-final particle meaning 'only', 'just', or downplaying something]。
   === I don't know how to speak it; I only know how to speak Cantonese and English.
A: 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 你[nei5|you] 識唔識[sik1 m4 sik1|do you know how to (A-not-A question form)] 煮[zyu2|to cook / boil] 日本[jat6bun2|Japan] 嘢食[je5sik6|snacks / food / things to eat] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Then do you know how to cook Japanese food?
B: 我[ngo5|I / me] 識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill] 煮[zyu2|to cook / boil] 壽司[sau6si1|sushi] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 你[nei5|you] 想唔想[soeng2 m4 soeng2|do you want to (A-not-A question form)] 食[sik6|to eat] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === I know how to make sushi! Do you want to eat some?
A: 想[soeng2|to want] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！我[ngo5|I / me] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 唔[m4|not / not / no (negative prefix)] 食[sik6|to eat] 得[dak1|particle expressing permission, possibility, capability, or adverbial degree] 嘢[je5|thing / stuff / matter] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']。
   === Yes, I do! But I cannot eat things today, obviously.
B: 點解[dim2gaai2|why / how come] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Why?
A: 因為[jan1wai6|because] 我[ngo5|I / me] 唔舒服[m4syu1fuk6|uncomfortable / unwell / sick]，食[sik6|to eat] 唔到[m4dou2|cannot / unable to manage to / fail to] 嘢食[je5sik6|snacks / food / things to eat] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === Because I am feeling unwell; I cannot manage to eat food.
B: 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 你[nei5|you] 可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible] 飲[jam2|to drink] 暖水[nyun5seoi2|warm water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 先[sin1|first / beforehand / only then] 食[sik6|to eat] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === In that case you can drink warm water. Eat tomorrow instead!
A: 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！唔該[m4goi1|excuse me / please / thank you (for service or help)] 你[nei5|you] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
   === Sure! Thank you.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following sentences correctly translates "I can drive a car" (expressing a learned skill) into Cantonese?

answer: B
explanation: 識[sik1|to know / speak (a language) / have a skill] is the modal verb used specifically for learned skills or abilities like driving. 可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible] refers to situational permission or possibility, and 會[wui5|will / would / can / be able to] expresses future likelihood or tendency.
```
