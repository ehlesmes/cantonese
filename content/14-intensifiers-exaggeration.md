Welcome to Chapter 14! In this lesson, we will cover the vibrant world of
Cantonese intensifiers and exaggeration. Spoken Cantonese is a deeply expressive
and emotional language; native speakers rarely stick to mild descriptors. To
sound authentic and colloquial, you must master the art of intensifying
adjectives, expressing excess, and making spoken superlative statements.

We will focus on four main areas:

1. **Post-Adjective Intensifiers**: Using `死[sei2|extremely / to death]`,
   `極[gik6|extremely / to the limit]`, and `爆[baau3|explosively / extremely]`
   directly after adjectives to express high intensity.
2. **Excessive Intensity**: Expressing that something is "too" much using
   `得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]`.
3. **Pre-Adjective Intensifiers & Superlatives**: Using
   `鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely / awfully]` and
   `最[zeoi3|most / superlative prefix]`.
4. **Quantifiers**: Structuring statements of absolute quantity using
   `所有[so2jau5|all / every]` and `全部[cyun4bou6|all / entirely]`.

Mastering these patterns will breathe life into your Cantonese, allowing you to
react with authentic excitement, complaint, or delight.

## 1. Post-Adjective Intensifiers: `死[sei2|extremely / to death]`, `極[gik6|extremely / to the limit]`, and `爆[baau3|explosively / extremely]`

In Cantonese, instead of placing an adverb before the adjective (like "very" in
English), it is highly common and natural to attach an intensifying suffix
directly after the adjective.

### 1. `死[sei2|extremely / to death]` (Tired to Death, Expensive to Death)

Similar to the English "to death" (e.g., "starving to death"),
`死[sei2|extremely / to death]` is appended to adjectives to express an extreme,
often negative, state.

- **Structure**: `[Adjective]` + `死[sei2|extremely / to death]` + `laa1` /
  `aa3`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 攰[gui6|tired] 死[sei2|extremely] 啦[laa1|particle]！
===
I am tired to death from going to work today!
```

```cantonese
呢度[ni1dou6|here] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 嘢食[je5sik6|food] 貴[gwai3|expensive] 死[sei2|extremely] 呀[aa3|particle]！
===
The food here is extremely expensive!
```

### 2. `爆[baau3|explosively / extremely]` (Explosively Busy, Explosively Spicy)

Originally slang but now completely integrated into everyday spoken Cantonese,
`爆[baau3|explosively / extremely]` adds a highly vibrant, modern flavor to your
statements. It can be used for both positive and negative situations.

- **Structure**: `[Adjective]` + `爆[baau3|explosively / extremely]` + `aa3`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 忙[mong4|busy] 爆[baau3|explosively] 呀[aa3|particle]！
===
I am extremely busy today!
```

```cantonese
呢[ni1|this] 碟[dip6|plate] 雞[gai1|chicken] 辣[laat6|spicy] 爆[baau3|explosively] 呀[aa3|particle]！
===
This plate of chicken is explosively spicy!
```

### 3. `極[gik6|extremely / to the limit]` (Extremely Good)

`極[gik6|extremely / to the limit]` is used to express that something has
reached its absolute limit. It is slightly more formal than
`爆[baau3|explosively]` or `死[sei2|extremely]`, but still highly common in
spoken Cantonese, particularly in the expression `好[hou2|good]`
`極[gik6|extremely]`.

- **Structure**: `[Adjective]` + `極[gik6|extremely / to the limit]`

```cantonese
呢個[ni1go3|this one] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 好[hou2|good] 極[gik6|extremely] 呀[aa3|particle]！
===
This egg tart is extremely good!
```

## 2. Excessive Intensity: `得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]`

When a state is not just extreme but has crossed the line into being "too much"
or "excessive", Cantonese speakers use `得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]` after
the adjective.

- **Structure**: `[Adjective]` + `得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]` + `aa3` /
  `laa1`

```cantonese
呢[ni1|this] 杯[bui1|cup] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee] 熱[jit6|hot] 得滯[dak1zai6|too] 呀[aa3|particle]。
===
This cup of coffee is too hot.
```

```cantonese
呢[ni1|this] 件[gin6|measure word for clothes, matters, and items] 衫[saam1|clothes] 大[daai6|big] 得滯[dak1zai6|too] 啦[laa1|particle]！
===
This piece of clothing is too big!
```

> [!NOTE] Do not confuse `得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]` with `咁[gam3|so]`.
> `咁[gam3|so]` is used to describe a state (e.g. `咁[gam3|so]`
> `貴[gwai3|expensive]`), while `得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]` explicitly
> marks a negative excess (e.g. `貴[gwai3|expensive]` `得滯[dak1zai6|too]`).

## 3. Pre-Adjective Intensifiers & Superlatives: `鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely / awfully]` and `最[zeoi3|most / superlative prefix]`

While post-adjective markers are popular, Cantonese also possesses powerful
pre-adjective intensifiers that completely change the rhythm of the sentence.

### 1. `鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely / awfully]` (Awfully Cold, Awfully Cheap)

`鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely / awfully]` is a highly colloquial, expressive
pre-adjective intensifier. It translates literally to something like
"ghost-dead-so", and is used to emphasize that a condition is striking or
overwhelming. It is frequently shortened to `鬼咁[gwai2gam3|extremely]` or
`死咁[sei2gam3|extremely]`.

- **Structure**: `鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely / awfully]` + `[Adjective]`

```cantonese
呢度[ni1dou6|here] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely] 貴[gwai3|expensive]！
===
The egg tarts here are extremely expensive!
```

```cantonese
今日[gam1jat6|today] 鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely] 凍[dung3|cold] 呀[aa3|particle]！
===
Today is awfully cold!
```

### 2. `最[zeoi3|most / superlative prefix]` (The Best, The Prettiest)

To make superlative statements (e.g., "the most expensive", "the best"),
Cantonese uses `最[zeoi3|most]` directly before the adjective.

- **Structure**: `最[zeoi3|most / superlative prefix]` + `[Adjective]` +
  `嘅[ge3|possessive particle]`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|want to] 食[sik6|to eat] 最[zeoi3|most] 好[hou2|good] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 嘢食[je5sik6|food]。
===
I want to eat the best food.
```

```cantonese
呢個[ni1go3|this one] 係[hai6|to be] 最[zeoi3|most] 靚[leng3|pretty] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle]！
===
This one is the prettiest!
```

## 4. Quantifiers: `所有[so2jau5|all / every]` and `全部[cyun4bou6|all / entirely]`

To express "all" in Cantonese, you must choose between two distinct words
depending on their grammatical role:

### 1. `所有[so2jau5|all / every]` (Adjective)

`所有[so2jau5|all / every]` is an adjective and must be placed **before the
noun** it modifies. It does not require a classifier between itself and the
noun.

- **Structure**: `所有[so2jau5|all / every]` + `[Noun]` +
  `都[dou1|also / both / all]` + `[Verb/Adjective]`

```cantonese
所有[so2jau5|all] 嘢[je5|thing / stuff] 都[dou1|also] 搞掂[gaau2dim6|done] 咗[zo2|completed aspect]！
===
All things have been finished!
```

### 2. `全部[cyun4bou6|all / entirely]` (Adverb/Pronoun or Pre-noun)

`全部[cyun4bou6|all / entirely]` can act as an adverb/pronoun placed **after the
subject and before the verb** to state that the entire set of things is acted
upon. It can also be used directly before a noun (e.g., `全部[cyun4bou6|all]`
`蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart]`) acting similarly to `所有[so2jau5|all]`.

- **Structure**: `[Subject]` + `全部[cyun4bou6|all / entirely]` +
  `都[dou1|also / both / all]` + `[Verb]`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 全部[cyun4bou6|all] 食[sik6|to eat] 咗[zo2|completed aspect] 啦[laa1|particle]！
===
I ate all of them!
```

## 5. Situational Dialogue: A Spicy Afternoon Tea

Wing-sum (A) and Wing-kit (B) have just finished a grueling work shift and meet
up at a Hong Kong café (`茶餐廳[caa4caan1teng1|Hong Kong style café]`). They use
colloquial intensifiers to complain about work, praise the café's signature
dishes, and discuss their plans.

```dialog
A: 你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 忙[mong4|busy] 唔[m4|not] 忙[mong4|busy] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Were you busy at work today?
B: 忙[mong4|busy] 爆[baau3|explosively] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 我[ngo5|I] 攰[gui6|tired] 死[sei2|extremely] 啦[laa1|particle]！ 所有[so2jau5|all] 工作[gung1zok3|work] 都[dou1|also] 要[jiu3|need] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 搞掂[gaau2dim6|done]！
   === Extremely busy! I'm tired to death! All the work had to be completed today!
A: 真係[zan1hai6|really] 辛苦[san1fu2|exhausting] 你[nei5|you] 啦[laa1|particle]！ 不如[bat1jyu4|how about] 一齊[jat1cai4|together] 食[sik6|to eat] 嘢食[je5sik6|food] 啦[laa1|particle]？
   === Really exhausting for you! How about eating food together?
B: 好[hou2|good] 呀[aa3|particle]！ 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 最[zeoi3|most] 正[zeng3|excellent]， 頂呱呱[ding2gwaa1gwaa1|top-notch] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === Sure! The egg tarts here are the best, they are top-notch!
A: 係[hai6|to be] 呀[aa3|particle]， 但係[daan6hai6|but] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee] 鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely] 貴[gwai3|expensive] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === Yes indeed, but the coffee here is extremely expensive!
B: 唔緊要[m4gan2jiu3|never mind] 啦[laa1|particle]！ 我[ngo5|I] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 想[soeng2|want to] 飲[jam2|to drink] 凍[dung3|cold] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 嘢飲[je5jam2|drinks]。 呢[ni1|this] 杯[bui1|cup] 茶[caa4|tea] 凍[dung3|cold] 得滯[dak1zai6|too] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === Never mind! Today I want to drink cold drinks. This cup of tea is too cold!
A: 哈[haa1|ha] 哈[haa1|ha]， 我[ngo5|I] 全部[cyun4bou6|all] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 都[dou1|also] 想[soeng2|want to] 食[sik6|to eat] 咗[zo2|completed aspect] 佢[keoi5|it]！
   === Haha, I want to eat all of these egg tarts!
```

```exercise

  "This egg tart is too hot!"

answer: 呢個[ni1go3|this one] 蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart] 熱[jit6|hot] 得滯[dak1zai6|too] 呀[aa3|particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
explanation: |
  The excessive construct in Cantonese is `[Adjective] + 得滯[dak1zai6|too / excessively]`. The classifier `個[go3|measure word for general items]` is used for general items like `蛋撻[daan6taat1|egg tart]`.
```

```exercise
answer: 爆[baau3|explosively / extremely (post-adjective intensifier)]

  `爆[baau3|explosively / extremely]` is a very high-frequency post-adjective intensifier in spoken Cantonese used to express extreme states.
```

```exercise

  B) 所有[so2jau5|all] is only used for food, while 全部[cyun4bou6|all / entirely] is used for people.
```
