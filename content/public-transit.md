## 1. Public Transit Modes

Cantonese speakers use a mixture of traditional characters and colloquial
English loanwords to describe transport options. The standard spoken verb for
taking any transit mode is `搭車[daap3ce1|to take transit / ride a vehicle]`. If
you need to transfer between lines or change vehicles, the term is
`轉車[zyun2ce1|to transfer (vehicles)]`.

Here are the primary modes of public transit:

- `地鐵[dei6tit3|MTR / subway]`: The mass transit railway system in Hong Kong.
- `的士[dik1si2|taxi]`: The phonetic loanword for taxi.
- `巴士[baa1si2|bus]`: The phonetic loanword for bus.
- `紅[hung4|red]` `VAN[man1|minibus (loanword)]`: The iconic red minibuses,
  which have flexible routes and schedules.
- `綠[luk6|green]` `VAN[man1|minibus (loanword)]`: The green minibuses, which
  run on fixed schedules and routes.
- `船[syun4|ferry / boat]` / `天星小輪[tin1sing1siu2leon4|Star Ferry]`: Ferries,
  including the iconic Star Ferry.
- `電車[din6ce1|tram]` / `叮叮[ding1ding1|tram (colloquial)]`: The historic
  double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island.

### Transit Locations & Payments

To locate stops or pay for your fare, use these terms:

- `站[zaam6|station / stop]`: The generic term for station or stop.
- `地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station / subway station]`: MTR subway station.
- `巴士站[baa1si2zaam6|bus stop]`: Bus stop.
- `的士站[dik1si2zaam6|taxi stand]`: Taxi stand.
- `八達通[baat3daat6tung1|Octopus card]`: The ubiquitous local contactless smart
  card. To use it, you `嘟[dut1|to tap a card / scan / beep]`.

## 2. Conversational Dialogue: Navigating Public Transit

A passenger (A) is asking a passerby (B) for help on how to get to the subway
station.

```dialog
A: 你好[nei5hou2|hello / hi]呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 請問[cing2man6|may I ask] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 點樣[dim2joeng6|how] 去[heoi3|to go] 地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station / subway station] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Hello! May I ask how to get to the MTR station from here?
B: 你[nei5|you] 可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible] 搭車[daap3ce1|to take transit / ride a vehicle] 去[heoi3|to go] 呀[aa3|particle]，喺[hai2|at / in / on] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 轉車[zyun2ce1|to transfer (vehicles)] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
   === You can take transit to go, and transfer here.
A: 搭[daap3|to take / ride] 巴士[baa1si2|bus (loanword)] 定[ding6|or] 叮叮[ding1ding1|tram (colloquial)] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Do I take the bus or the tram?
B: 搭[daap3|to take / ride] 巴士[baa1si2|bus (loanword)] 啦[laa1|particle]，好[hou2|very] 快[faai3|fast] 㗎[gaa3|particle]。 巴士站[baa1si2zaam6|bus stop] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 前邊[cin4bin1|ahead]。
   === Take the bus; it's very fast. The bus stop is up ahead.
A: 好[hou2|good] 呀[aa3|particle]，呢度[ni1dou6|here] 可唔可以[ho2m4ho2ji5|can or cannot] 嘟[dut1|to tap a card] 八達通[baat3daat6tung1|Octopus card] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Great, can I tap my Octopus card here?
B: 可以[ho2ji5|can] 呀[aa3|particle]！
   === Yes you can!
A: 唔該晒[m4goi1saai3|thank you very much]！ 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Thank you very much! Bye-bye!
B: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
```

## 3. Exercises

Review the transit modes and vocabulary introduced in this chapter:

```exercise
question: |
  Which transit mode is colloquially named after the sound of its bell ("ding-ding")?
  A) 地鐵[dei6tit3|MTR]
  B) 巴士[baa1si2|bus]
  C) 叮叮[ding1ding1|tram]
  D) 的士[dik1si2|taxi]
answer: C
explanation: |
  The Hong Kong double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island are colloquially known as `叮叮[ding1ding1|tram (colloquial)]` due to the double-ringing bell sound they make to warn pedestrians.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct transit verb meaning "to take/ride transit":
  我[ngo5|I / me] 想[soeng2|to want to / would like to] ________ 巴士[baa1si2|bus] 去[heoi3|to go to] 地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station]。
answer: 搭[daap3|to take / ride]
explanation: |
  `搭[daap3|to take / ride]` is the verb used to describe boarding or riding public transportation.
```
