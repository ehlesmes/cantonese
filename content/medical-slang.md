In this chapter, we will learn about **Doctors, Hospitals & Medical Slang**.

In Hong Kong workplace culture, calling in sick involves specific administrative
terms and highly popular local slang. Let's study how to navigate these
situations colloquially.

Let's dive in!

## 1. Workplace Illness & Medical Leave Slang

Here are the key terms used for requesting sick leave and describing office
attendance:

- `請假[cing2gaa3|to request leave]`: To request time off work.
- `病假[beng6gaa3|sick leave]`: Official sick leave.
- `醫生紙[ji1sang1zi2|medical note]`: Literally "doctor paper," meaning the
  official sick leave certificate.
- `射波[se6bo1|to fake sick]`: Literally "to shoot a ball," a very famous Hong
  Kong slang term meaning to fake sick or call in sick falsely to skip work.
- `唔使[m4sai2|no need to]`: No need.
- `使唔使[sai2m4sai2|do you need?]`: The A-not-A question form for "do you need
  to".
- `可唔可以[ho2m4ho2ji5|can you?]`: The A-not-A question form for "can / may".
- `休息[jau1sik1|to rest]`: To rest.
- `早日康復[zou2jat6hong1fuk6|speedy recovery]`: Wish you a speedy recovery.
- `向[hoeng3|towards]`: Towards, to (e.g. `向[hoeng3|towards]`
  `公司[gung1si1|company]` `請假[cing2gaa3|to request leave]`).

## 2. Situational Dialogue: Calling in Sick & Faking It

Two colleagues, Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B), chat about work attendance and B's
health problems.

```dialog
A: 喂[wai3|hey! / hello]，你[nei5|you] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 返唔返[faan1m4faan1|to return or not (A-not-A question form)] 工[gung1|job] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Hey, are you going to work tomorrow?
B: 唔[m4|not] 返[faan1|to return] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！我[ngo5|I] 諗住[nam2zyu6|to intend] 射波[se6bo1|to fake sick] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === No! I plan to call in sick.
A: 吓[haa2|interjection expressing surprise]？你[nei5|you] 點解[dim2gaai2|why] 想[soeng2|to want to] 射波[se6bo1|to fake sick] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？係唔係[hai6m4hai6|is it?] 琴晚[kam4maan5|last night] 去[heoi3|go] 玩[waan2|to play] 得[dak1|okay / can / ready] 太[taai3|too] 晏[aan3|late] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === What?! Why do you want to call in sick? Is it because you stayed out too late playing last night?
B: 唔係[m4hai6|no / is not] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！我[ngo5|I] 真係[zan1hai6|really] 有[jau5|to have / there is] 啲[di1|a bit / some] 唔舒服[m4syu1fuk6|unwell]，好似[hou2ci5|seems like] 中[zung3|to win / catch an illness] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 感冒[gam2mou6|common cold]。我[ngo5|I] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 開始[hoi1ci2|to start] 發燒[faat3siu1|to have a fever] 同埋[tung4maai4|and] 喉嚨痛[hau4lung4tung3|sore throat]，仲[zung6|still / yet] 係咁[hai6gam2|continuously] 咳[kat1|to cough] 添[tim1|particle indicating addition]。
   === No! I really feel a bit unwell, it seems I've caught a cold. I started running a fever and having a sore throat today, and I'm even coughing constantly.
A: 哇[waa1|wow]！聽落[ting1lok6|sounds like] 幾[gei2|quite] 嚴重[jim4zung6|severe] 喎[wo3|sentence-final particle for surprise or warning]。噉[gam2|like this / then] 你[nei5|you] 有冇[jau5mou5|do you have?] 睇醫生[tai2ji1sang1|to see a doctor] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Wow! That sounds quite serious. Then did you go see a doctor?
B: 諗住[nam2zyu6|to intend] 下晝[haa6zau3|in the afternoon] 去[heoi3|go] 睇[tai2|to look / watch]。唔[m4|not] 睇醫生[tai2ji1sang1|to see a doctor] 攞[lo2|to fetch] 張[zoeng1|measure word for flat objects, sheets, papers] 醫生紙[ji1sang1zi2|medical note]，點樣[dim2joeng6|how] 向[hoeng3|towards] 公司[gung1si1|company] 請[cing2|to invite / request / treat] 病假[beng6gaa3|sick leave] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === I plan to go see one in the afternoon. If I don't see a doctor to get a medical note, how can I apply for sick leave from the company?
A: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]，頂住[ding2zyu6|to endure] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！睇[tai2|to look / watch] 完[jyun4|finished] 醫生[ji1sang1|doctor] 就[zau6|then] 返[faan1|to return] 屋企[uk1kei2|home] 好好哋[hou2hou2dei2|properly] 抖吓[dau2haa5|to rest a bit] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
   === Yes, hang in there! After you see the doctor, go straight home to get some proper rest.
```

## 3. Exercises

Test your understanding of Cantonese workplace sick leave and slang.

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following Cantonese terms is a popular slang meaning "to fake sick" or "call in sick falsely"?
  A) 睇醫生[tai2ji1sang1|to see a doctor]
  B) 射波[se6bo1|to fake sick]
  C) 醫生紙[ji1sang1zi2|medical note]
  D) 早日康復[zou2jat6hong1fuk6|speedy recovery]
answer: B
explanation: |
  `射波[se6bo1|to fake sick]` literally means "to shoot a ball" but is the most popular slang in Hong Kong for calling in sick falsely.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct noun for "doctor's note":
  我[ngo5|I] 要[jiu3|to want / need] 攞[lo2|to fetch] ______ 先[sin1|first] 可以[ho2ji5|can] 請[cing2|to invite / request / treat] 病假[beng6gaa3|sick leave]。
answer: 醫生紙[ji1sang1zi2|medical note]
explanation: |
  `醫生紙[ji1sang1zi2|medical note]` is the standard colloquial term for the official medical certificate required to apply for sick leave.
```
