## 1. Navigating Hong Kong's Transport Network

Getting around Hong Kong requires understanding the core vocabulary of daily
transit. The standard spoken verb for taking any transit mode is
`搭車[daap3ce1|to take transit / ride a vehicle]`. If you need to transfer
between lines or change vehicles, the term is
`轉車[zyun2ce1|to transfer (vehicles)]`.

### Public Transit Modes

Cantonese speakers use a mixture of traditional characters and colloquial
English loanwords to describe transport options:

- `地鐵[dei6tit3|MTR / subway]`: The mass transit railway system in Hong Kong.
- `的士[dik1si2|taxi]`: The phonetic loanword for taxi.
- `巴士[baa1si2|bus]`: The phonetic loanword for bus.
- `紅[hung4|red]` `VAN[man1|minibus (loanword)]`: The iconic red minibuses,
  which have flexible routes and schedules.
- `綠[luk6|green]` `VAN[man1|minibus (loanword)]`: The green minibuses, which
  run on fixed schedules and routes.
- `船[syun4|ferry / boat]` / `天星小輪[tin1sing1siu2leon4|Star Ferry]`: Ferries,
  including the iconic Star Ferry.
- `電車[din6ce1|tram]` / `叮叮[ding1ding1|tram (colloquial)]`: The historic
  double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island.

### Transit Locations & Payments

To locate stops or pay for your fare, use these terms:

- `站[zaam6|station / stop]`: The generic term for station or stop.
- `地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station]`: MTR subway station.
- `巴士站[baa1si2zaam6|bus stop]`: Bus stop.
- `的士站[dik1si2zaam6|taxi stand]`: Taxi stand.
- `八達通[baat3daat6tung1|Octopus card]`: The ubiquitous local contactless smart
  card. To use it, you `嘟[dut1|to tap a card / scan]`.

## 2. Minibus Shouting Etiquette

Riding a minibus (`紅[hung4|red]` `VAN[man1|minibus (loanword)]` or
`綠[luk6|green]` `VAN[man1|minibus (loanword)]`) is a quintessential Hong Kong
experience. Unlike double-decker buses, minibuses do not have stop buttons or
automated announcements. Instead, passengers must shout out to the
`司機[si1gei1|driver]` when they want to request a stop.

### The Stop Request Template

To request a stop, use this formula:

- `唔該[m4goi1|please]` + Landmark/Location + `有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]`

### Common Stop Request Expressions

Here are the most common phrases passengers shout on a minibus:

- `唔該[m4goi1|please]` `前邊[cin4bin1|ahead]`
  `有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]`: Stop up ahead, please.
- `唔該[m4goi1|please]` `路口[lou6hau2|intersection]`
  `有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]`: Stop at the intersection, please.
- `唔該[m4goi1|please]` `燈位[dang1wai2|traffic light]`
  `有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]`: Stop at the traffic light, please.
- `唔該[m4goi1|please]` `地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station]`
  `有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]`: Stop at the MTR station, please.

To get on a vehicle, use `上車[soeng5ce1|to get on a vehicle]`, and to get off,
use `落車[lok6ce1|to get off a vehicle]`.

## 3. Asking for & Giving Directions

When exploring the city or directing a driver, spoken Cantonese relies on simple
directional commands and road features.

### Directional Commands

- `直行[zik6haang4|to go straight]`: Go straight ahead.
- `轉左[zyun2zo2|to turn left]`: Turn left.
- `轉右[zyun2jau6|to turn right]`: Turn right.
- `過馬路[gwo3maa5lou6|to cross the road]`: Cross the street.

### Street Landmarks & Infrastructure

- `前邊[cin4bin1|ahead / in front / up ahead]`: Ahead or in front.
- `路口[lou6hau2|intersection / junction]`: An intersection or street corner.
- `燈位[dang1wai2|traffic light / intersection with lights]`: Traffic lights.
- `天橋[tin1kiu4|pedestrian flyover / footbridge]`: A pedestrian bridge over the
  street.
- `隧道[seoi6dou6|tunnel]`: An underpass or tunnel.

### Time & Delay States

If you are rushing or caught in traffic, these terms are essential:

- `行路[haang4lou6|to walk]`: Traveling on foot.
- `趕時間[gon2si4gaan3|in a hurry / rushing for time]`: Being in a hurry.
- `遲到[ci4dou3|to arrive late]`: Arriving late.
- `等[dang2|to wait]`: Waiting.
- `幾耐[gei2noi6|how long (time)]`: Asking about the duration of time.
- `塞車[sak1ce1|traffic jam / heavy traffic]`: Being stuck in a traffic jam.

## 4. Situational Dialogues

### Dialogue 1: Riding a Red Minibus

A passenger (`A`) is trying to get to the subway station quickly and coordinates
with the minibus driver (`B`).

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|please]，我[ngo5|I] 趕時間[gon2si4gaan3|in a hurry]，請問[cing2man6|may I ask] 架[gaa3|classifier for vehicles] 車[ce1|car / vehicle] 去[heoi3|to go] 地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station] 要[jiu3|need] 幾耐[gei2noi6|how long] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Excuse me, I am in a hurry. May I ask how long it takes for this vehicle to get to the MTR station?
B: 前邊[cin4bin1|ahead] 塞車[sak1ce1|traffic jam] 呀[aa3|particle]，大約[daai6joek3|approximately] 十[sap6|ten] 分鐘[fan1zung1|minutes] 啦[laa1|particle]。
   === There is a traffic jam up ahead, about ten minutes.
A: 噉[gam2|then] 我[ngo5|I] 喺[hai2|at] 前邊[cin4bin1|ahead] 天橋[tin1kiu4|pedestrian flyover] 落車[lok6ce1|to get off] 行路[haang4lou6|walk] 過去[gwo3heoi3|go over] 啦[laa1|particle]。唔該[m4goi1|please] 司機[si1gei1|driver]，天橋[tin1kiu4|pedestrian flyover] 有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]！
   === Then I will get off at the pedestrian flyover ahead and walk over. Thanks, driver. Please stop at the pedestrian flyover!
B: 收到[sau1dou2|got it]！呢度[ni1dou6|here] 唔[m4|not] 可以[ho2ji5|can] 落車[lok6ce1|get off]，過[gwo3|to pass / cross] 咗[zo2|completed action] 燈位[dang1wai2|traffic light] 先[sin1|first] 俾[bei2|to give / for / let / allow] 你[nei5|you] 落[lok6|get off] 啦[laa1|particle]。
   === Got it! I cannot let you off here. I will let you off after we pass the traffic light.
```

### Dialogue 2: Asking a Pedestrian for Directions

A pedestrian (`A`) is trying to locate the MTR station and asks a passerby (`B`)
for help.

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|please]，請問[cing2man6|may I ask] 地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station] 點樣[dim2joeng6|how] 去[heoi3|to go] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Excuse me, may I ask how to get to the MTR station?
B: 你[nei5|you] 喺[hai2|at] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 直行[zik6haang4|go straight]，行到[haang4dou3|walk to] 前邊[cin4bin1|ahead] 路口[lou6hau2|intersection] 轉右[zyun2jau6|turn right] 啦[laa1|particle]。
   === You go straight from here, and when you walk to the intersection ahead, turn right.
A: 轉右[zyun2jau6|turn right] 之後[zi1hau6|after]，仲[zung6|still] 要[jiu3|need] 行[hang4|to walk / go / run (a machine)] 幾耐[gei2noi6|how long] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === After turning right, how much longer do I still need to walk?
B: 轉右[zyun2jau6|turn right] 之後[zi1hau6|after] 過馬路[gwo3maa5lou6|cross the road]，喺[hai2|at] 巴士站[baa1si2zaam6|bus stop] 隔離[gaak3lei4|beside] 就[zau6|then] 係[hai6|is] 地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station] 喇[laa3|particle]。
   === After turning right, cross the road, and right next to the bus stop is the MTR station.
A: 唔該晒[m4goi1saai3|thank you very much]！
   === Thank you very much!
```

## 5. Exercises

Test your understanding of transportation terms, directional commands, and
minibus slang in Cantonese.

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following is the iconic Cantonese phrase shouted to minibus drivers to request a stop?
  A) 唔該[m4goi1|please] 轉車[zyun2ce1|to transfer]
  B) 唔該[m4goi1|please] 有落[jau5lok6|stop here please]
  C) 唔該[m4goi1|please] 塞車[sak1ce1|traffic jam]
  D) 唔該[m4goi1|please] 遲到[ci4dou3|to arrive late]
answer: B
explanation: 唔該[m4goi1|please] 有落[jau5lok6|stop here please] is the standard colloquial phrase used to request a stop on Hong Kong minibuses.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct term for "to get off a vehicle":
  我[ngo5|I] 喺[hai2|at] 前邊[cin4bin1|ahead] ______。
answer: 落車[lok6ce1|to get off]
explanation: 落車[lok6ce1|to get off] is the correct verb meaning "to get off a vehicle" (literally "descend car").
```
