In this chapter, we will expand our grammatical toolkit to express logical
relationships, hypothetical situations, plans, and aspirations.

First, we will learn how to construct standard "if... then..." conditional
sentences using `如果[jyu4gwo2|if]` and `就[zau6|then]`.

Second, we will explore hypothetical conditions using `假使[gaa2si2|if]`, which
allows you to paint "what if" scenarios.

Third, we will master how to talk about our future intentions and dreams using
`打算[daa2syun3|to plan]` and `希望[hei1mong6|to hope]`.

Finally, we will study the authentic local expression
`發夢[faat3mung6|to daydream]` and see how to use it to inject humor and native
flavor into your conversations.

---

## 1. Standard Conditionals: If... Then...

In Cantonese, the most common way to form a conditional sentence ("If A, then
B") is by placing `如果[jyu4gwo2|if]` at the start of the first clause, and
placing `就[zau6|then]` right before the verb in the second clause:

> **Formula:** `如果[jyu4gwo2|if]` [Condition] ， [Subject] `就[zau6|then]`
> [Result]

Let's look at some daily examples:

### 1. Weather and Plans:

```cantonese
如果[jyu4gwo2|if] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]， 我[ngo5|I] 就[zau6|then] 唔[m4|not] 去[heoi3|go] 游水[jau4seoi2|to swim] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
===
If it rains tomorrow, then I won't go swimming.
```

### 2. Conversational Offers:

```cantonese
如果[jyu4gwo2|if] 你[nei5|you] 想[soeng2|to want to] 飲[jam2|drink] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]， 我[ngo5|I] 就[zau6|then] 買[maai5|buy] 俾[bei2|to give / by (passive) / let] 你[nei5|you] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
If you want to drink coffee, then I'll buy it for you.
```

> [!NOTE] In colloquial Cantonese, the word `如果[jyu4gwo2|if]` is frequently
> dropped entirely from the beginning of the sentence. The conditional meaning
> is implied simply by having the condition clause followed by `就[zau6|then]`
> in the second clause (e.g., `聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow]`
> `落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]`，`我[ngo5|I]` `就[zau6|then]` `唔[m4|not]`
> `去[heoi3|go]` `喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]`).

## 2. Hypothetical Possibilities: Supposing...

When you want to describe a purely hypothetical or highly speculative scenario
("supposing that" or "what if"), Cantonese speakers use `假使[gaa2si2|if]`.
Structurally, it functions exactly like `如果[jyu4gwo2|if]`, but it carries a
more imaginative or dramatic nuance.

### 1. Imagining Wealth:

```cantonese
假使[gaa2si2|if] 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 有錢[jau5cin2|rich]， 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 就[zau6|then] 去[heoi3|go] 香港[hoeng1gong2|Hong Kong] 玩[waan2|to play] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！
===
Supposing we were rich, we would go to Hong Kong to have fun, obviously!
```

### 2. Imagining an Alien Invasion:

```cantonese
假使[gaa2si2|if] 有[jau5|to have / there is] 外星人[ngoi6sing1jan4|alien]， 我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 就[zau6|then] 走[zau2|to leave] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！
===
Supposing there were aliens, we would run away!
```

## 3. Planning & Hopes: Concrete vs. Aspirational

When discussing the future, Cantonese distinguishes between concrete plans
(`打算[daa2syun3|to plan]`) and emotional aspirations or wishes
(`希望[hei1mong6|to hope]`).

To describe when these actions will happen, you can use time-related terms like
`第日[dai6jat6|another day]` (some day / in the future) or
`遲啲[ci4di1|later on]` (later on).

### 1. Making Concrete Plans: `打算[daa2syun3|to plan]`

Use `打算[daa2syun3|to plan]` for plans you have already decided on or are
actively arranging.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 打算[daa2syun3|to plan] 遲啲[ci4di1|later on] 去[heoi3|go] 香港[hoeng1gong2|Hong Kong] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work]。
===
I plan to go to Hong Kong to work later on.
```

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we] 打算[daa2syun3|to plan] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 去[heoi3|go] 睇戲[tai2hei3|to watch a movie]。
===
We plan to go watch a movie tomorrow.
```

### 2. Expressing Hopes & Wishes: `希望[hei1mong6|to hope]`

Use `希望[hei1mong6|to hope]` for future scenarios that you want to happen but
do not have full control over.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 希望[hei1mong6|to hope] 第日[dai6jat6|another day] 可以[ho2ji5|can] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 香港[hoeng1gong2|Hong Kong] 買[maai5|buy] 書[syu1|book] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I hope that in the future I can buy books in Hong Kong.
```

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 希望[hei1mong6|to hope] 搵[wan2|to search / find] 到[dou2|successfully do (verb complement)] 新[san1|new] 工[gung1|job] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He hopes to find a new job.
```

## 4. Spoken Cantonese Slang: Daydreaming

The verb `發夢[faat3mung6|to daydream]` is structurally a split-verb:

- Verb Part: `發[faat3|to emit / dream]`
- Object Part: `夢[mung6|dream]`

While it literally means "to dream" while sleeping, in daily colloquial
Cantonese, it is heavily used to tease someone who has unrealistic expectations
or is making wild assumptions. Telling someone `你[nei5|you]`
`發[faat3|to emit / dream]` `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]`
`夢[mung6|dream]`
`呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]`
is a friendly, humorous way of saying "You are daydreaming!" or "In your
dreams!"

### 1. Teasing a Dreamer:

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 發[faat3|to emit / dream] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 夢[mung6|dream] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Are you standing here daydreaming? (Or "Are you dreaming?")
```

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 估[gu2|to guess] 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 會[wui5|will / can] 幫[bong1|to help] 你[nei5|you] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？ 發夢[faat3mung6|to daydream] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
===
You think he will help you? In your dreams!
```

## 5. Situational Dialogue: Daydreaming of the Lottery

Wing-kit (A) and Sau-han (B) are having coffee at a café, gossiping and
imagining what they would do if they won the Mark Six lottery
(`六合彩[luk6hap6coi2|Mark Six (Hong Kong lottery)]`).

```dialog
A: 如果[jyu4gwo2|if] 你[nei5|you] 中[zung3|to win / catch an illness] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 六合彩[luk6hap6coi2|Mark Six (Hong Kong lottery)]， 你[nei5|you] 有[jau5|to have / there is] 咩[me1|sentence-final particle for doubt / what] 打算[daa2syun3|to plan] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === If you won the Mark Six, what plans do you have?
B: 假使[gaa2si2|if] 我[ngo5|I] 中[zung3|to win / catch an illness] 咗[zo2|completed action marker]， 我[ngo5|I] 就[zau6|then] 唔[m4|not] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！ 我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 遲啲[ci4di1|later on] 去[heoi3|go] 香港[hoeng1gong2|Hong Kong] 玩[waan2|to play] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 你[nei5|you] 呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]？
   === Supposing I won, I wouldn't go to work, obviously! I want to go to Hong Kong to have fun later on! How about you?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 打算[daa2syun3|to plan] 買[maai5|buy] 好[hou2|good / very] 多[do1|many] 書[syu1|book]。 我[ngo5|I] 希望[hei1mong6|to hope] 第日[dai6jat6|another day] 可以[ho2ji5|can] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 香港[hoeng1gong2|Hong Kong] 睇書[tai2syu1|to read books] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === I plan to buy a lot of books. I hope that in the future I can read books in Hong Kong.
B: 哇[waa1|wow]！ 你[nei5|you] 真係[zan1hai6|really] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 發[faat3|to emit / dream] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 夢[mung6|dream] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle for obviousness]！ 中[zung3|to win / catch an illness] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 六合彩[luk6hap6coi2|Mark Six (Hong Kong lottery)] 先[sin1|first] 講[gong2|to speak] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Whoa! You are really here daydreaming, obviously! Talk about it only after you win the Mark Six!
```

## 6. Exercises

Test your understanding of conditionals, hypotheticals, plans, and daydreaming.

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into Cantonese using correct semantic annotations:
  "If it rains tomorrow, then I won't go."
  (Hint: Use jyu4gwo2 for "if", zau6 for "then", and m4 heoi3 for "not go")
answer: 如果[jyu4gwo2|if] 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]， 我[ngo5|I] 就[zau6|then] 唔[m4|not] 去[heoi3|go] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
explanation: |
  The conditional pattern in Cantonese is `如果[jyu4gwo2|if] ... 就[zau6|then]`. `聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow]` is "tomorrow", `落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain]` is "to rain", `我[ngo5|I]` is "I", `唔[m4|not]` is "not", `去[heoi3|go]` is "go", and `喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]` indicates a change of state.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank with the correct aspect marker to say "Are you daydreaming (right now)?" in Cantonese:
  你[nei5|you] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 呢度[ni1dou6|here] 發[faat3|to emit / dream] ________ 夢[mung6|dream] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
answer: 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]
explanation: |
  The verb `發夢[faat3mung6|to daydream]` is a split-verb. To express continuous action, the progressive aspect suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` must be inserted between `發[faat3|to emit / dream]` and `夢[mung6|dream]`, yielding `發[faat3|to emit / dream]` `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` `夢[mung6|dream]`.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Which word is the most appropriate to describe a purely hypothetical or speculative situation (equivalent to "supposing that")?
  A) 如果[jyu4gwo2|if]
  B) 就[zau6|then]
  C) 假使[gaa2si2|if]
answer: C
explanation: |
  While both `如果[jyu4gwo2|if]` and `假使[gaa2si2|if]` are conditional conjunctions, `假使[gaa2si2|if]` is specifically used to introduce highly hypothetical, speculative, or dramatic "supposing that" scenarios. Option A is a general conditional "if", and Option B is the adverb "then".
```
