## 1. Currency & Spoken Prices in Cantonese

In daily Cantonese life, handling money and pricing is highly colloquial and
differs significantly from formal written Chinese. When asking for prices,
instead of the formal `幾多[gei2do1|how much]` `錢[cin2|money]`, you will
frequently hear speakers use the shorter, snappier
`幾錢[gei2 cin2|how much money]` in casual street environments.

### The Units of Currency

To talk about currency, spoken Cantonese employs unique units:

- `蚊[man1|dollar]`: The standard spoken unit of currency (equivalent to the
  written Hong Kong dollar symbol).
- `毫[hou4|ten cents / dime]`: Used for ten-cent intervals.
- `毫子[hou4 zi2|ten-cent coin / dime]`: The physical coin of ten cents.
- `分[fan1|cent / minute]`: Used for single cents (though rarely seen in modern
  transactions).

### Spoken Pricing Shortcuts

Cantonese speakers love efficiency, which shows in pricing shortcuts:

- `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` represents a dollar when
  followed by decimal values. The most common is `半[bun3|half]` ($1.5 is
  `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` `半[bun3|half]`), but it
  applies to others like `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `二[ji6|two]` ($1.2) or `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `六[luk6|six]` ($1.6). Note that for values of $2 and above, the number must
  precede `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` (e.g., $2.5 is
  `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]`
  `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` `半[bun3|half]`, $3.5 is
  `三[saam1|three]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `半[bun3|half]`).
- When counting two of a currency unit, always use
  `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]` instead of `二[ji6|two]`.
  E.g., `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]`
  `蚊[man1|dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)]` ($2).
- When prices are in the hundreds or thousands, the final unit is often dropped
  if there are no smaller trailing digits. E.g., `$380` is commonly said as
  `三[saam1|three]` `百[baak3|hundred]` `八[baat3|eight]` instead of the full
  `三[saam1|three]` `百[baak3|hundred]` `八[baat3|eight]` `十[sap6|ten]`
  `蚊[man1|dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)]`.

## 2. The Art of Bargaining (`講價[gong2 gaa3|to bargain]`)

If you are shopping in Hong Kong’s vibrant street markets (like the Ladies'
Market in Mong Kok or Temple Street in Yau Ma Tei), bargaining
(`講價[gong2 gaa3|to bargain]`) is part of the fun and local culture.

Here are the key verbs and expressions you need to bargain effectively with the
`老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor]`:

- `平[peng4|cheap]` `啲[di1|a bit]` `啦[laa1|particle]`: The ultimate
  gold-standard bargaining phrase. It uses `平[peng4|cheap]` with the
  comparative suffix `啲[di1|a bit]` and the softening particle
  `啦[laa1|particle]`.
- `減價[gaam2 gaa3|to reduce price / go on sale]`: To ask the boss to mark down
  the price.
- `特價[dat6 gaa3|special price / bargain price]`: Refer to items on a special
  bargain sale.
- `有折[jau5 zit3|to have a discount]` / `打折[daa2 zit3|to give a discount]`:
  Referring to discounts. E.g., asking if there is a discount
  (`折[zit3|discount]`).
- `送[sung3|to give as a gift / include for free]`: Ask for free additions or
  gifts (e.g., buy this and get that for free).
- `算啦[syun3 laa1|forget it / let it be]`: The ultimate walk-away strategy! If
  they won't budge on price, say `算啦[syun3 laa1|forget it / let it be]` and
  walk. They will often call you back with a lower price!

## 3. Situational Dialogues

### Dialogue 1: At a Clothing Market Stall in Mong Kok

A customer (`A`) set eyes on an item and tries to bargain with the stall owner
(`B`).

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me]，老闆[lou5baan2|boss / proprietor]，呢個[ni1go3|this one]幾多[gei2do1|how much] 錢[cin2|money] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Excuse me, boss, how much is this one?
B: 呢個[ni1go3|this one]好[hou2|very]靚[leng3|high quality]嘅[ge3|final assertive particle]，三[saam1|three]百[baak3|hundred]八[baat3|eight]。
   === This one is of very high quality, three hundred and eighty.
A: 三[saam1|three]百[baak3|hundred]八[baat3|eight]？鬼死咁[gwai2sei2gam3|extremely]貴[gwai3|expensive]！兩[loeng5|two]百[baak3|hundred]蚊[man1|dollar]得[dak1|can] 唔[m4|not] 得[dak1|can] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Three hundred and eighty? Extremely expensive! Is two hundred dollars okay?
B: 兩[loeng5|two]百[baak3|hundred]蚊[man1|dollar]太[taai3|too]平[peng4|cheap]啦[laa1|particle]，賣[maai6|sell]唔到[m4dou2|cannot]！不如[bat1jyu4|how about]咁[gam3|so]啦[laa1|particle]，你[nei5|you]買[maai5|buy]埋[maai4|also]呢個[ni1go3|this one]，我[ngo5|I]俾[bei2|give]個[go3|general measure word]折[zit3|discount]你[nei5|you]。兩[loeng5|two]個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]三[saam1|three]百[baak3|hundred]二[ji6|two]啦[laa1|particle]！
   === Two hundred dollars is too cheap, I can't sell it! How about this: if you also buy this one, I'll give you a discount. Two pieces for three hundred and twenty then!
A: 好[hou2|good] 啦[laa1|particle]！你[nei5|you]真係[zan1hai6|really]好[hou2|very]叻[lek1|smart]講價[gong2 gaa3|to bargain]！
   === Okay! You are really smart at bargaining!
```

### Dialogue 2: Checking Out at the Cashier

The customer (`A`) heads to the cash counter to settle the payment with the
cashier (`B`).

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me]，收銀[sau1 ngan2|cashier]喺[hai2|at]邊度[bin1dou6|where]呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Excuse me, where is the cashier?
B: 收銀[sau1 ngan2|cashier]喺[hai2|at]呢度[ni1dou6|here]。你[nei5|you]想[soeng2|want to]點樣[dim2joeng6|how]俾[bei2|give]錢[cin2|money]？
   === The cashier is over here. How would you like to pay?
A: 用[jung6|to use]八達通[baat3daat6tung1|Octopus card]得[dak1|can] 唔[m4|not] 得[dak1|can] 呀[aa3|particle]？
   === Is it okay to use an Octopus card?
B: 可以[ho2ji5|can]呀[aa3|particle]。碌卡[luk1 ka1|swipe credit card]都[dou1|also]得[dak1|capability]嘅[ge3|final assertive particle]。
   === Yes, you can. Swiping a credit card is also fine.
A: 噉[gam2|then]我[ngo5|I]碌卡[luk1 ka1|swipe credit card]啦[laa1|particle]。
   === In that case, I will swipe a credit card.
B: 好[hou2|okay]，呢個[ni1go3|this one]係[hai6|is]你[nei5|your]嘅[ge3|possessive particle]單[daan1|bill / receipt / sheet]。多謝[do1ze6|thank you]晒[saai3|entirely]！
   === Okay, this is your receipt. Thank you very much!
```

## 4. Exercises

Use these exercises to test your understanding of currency and bargaining in
Cantonese.

```exercise
question: |
  Complete the dialogue with the correct bargaining phrase meaning "cheaper, please":
  A: 呢個[ni1go3|this one]好[hou2|very]貴[gwai3|expensive]呀[aa3|particle]！______ 得[dak1|can] 唔[m4|not] 得[dak1|can] 呀[aa3|particle]？
answer: 平[peng4|cheap] 啲[di1|more]
explanation: 平[peng4|cheap] 啲[di1|more] is combined with the potential question 得[dak1|can] 唔[m4|not] 得[dak1|can] to ask "Can it be a bit cheaper?".
```

```exercise
question: |
  Which Cantonese unit of currency is equivalent to the dollar?
  A) 毫[hou4|ten cents]
  B) 蚊[man1|dollar]
  C) 分[fan1|cent]
answer: B
explanation: 蚊[man1|dollar] is the standard spoken unit of currency in Cantonese.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into colloquial Cantonese:
  "Excuse me, how much is this one?"
answer: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me]，呢個[ni1go3|this one]幾錢[gei2 cin2|how much money]呀[aa3|particle]？
explanation: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me] means "excuse me", 呢個[ni1go3|this one] is "this one", 幾錢[gei2 cin2|how much money] is the short spoken form of "how much money", and 呀[aa3|particle] is the SFP.
```
