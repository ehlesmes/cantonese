In this chapter, we will explore one of the most structurally unique areas of
Colloquial Cantonese: **Split-Verbs** (linguistically known as _separable verbs_
or _lei-hap-ci_ in Cantonese).

These words translate as simple single verbs in English (e.g. "to swim" or "to
sing"), but structurally they are compound verb-object pairs. To express ongoing
actions, past completions, or duration, Cantonese speakers literally split these
compounds in half and insert grammar markers directly between them.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Structuring Split-Verbs: Insert in the Middle

In Cantonese, split-verbs are composed of two parts:

1. A **Verb Part** (the action)
2. An **Object Part** (the target of the action)

For example, `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]` is made of `游[jau4|to swim]` and
`水[seoi2|water]`. Similarly, `唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing]` is made of
`唱[coeng3|to sing]` and `歌[go1|song]`.

When you want to attach **aspect markers**—such as completed
`咗[zo2|completed action marker]`, continuous
`緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]`, or experiential
`過[gwo3|past experience marker / to cross / than]`—you must **split the
compound** and insert the marker between the two parts:

> **Formula:** [Verb Part] + [Aspect Marker] + [Object Part]

Let's see how standard split-verbs behave when modified:

### 1. Swimming: `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]`

- Verb Part: `游[jau4|to swim]`
- Object Part: `水[seoi2|water]`

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 水[seoi2|water] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
===
I swam yesterday already.
```

```cantonese
佢哋[keoi5dei6|they] 游[jau4|to swim] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 水[seoi2|water] 喎[wo3|sentence-final particle for surprise or warning]！
===
Apparently, they are swimming right now!
```

### 2. Singing: `唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing]`

- Verb Part: `唱[coeng3|to sing]`
- Object Part: `歌[go1|song]`

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 聽[teng1|to listen] 吓[haa5|brief action marker]！ 佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 唱[coeng3|to sing] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 歌[go1|song] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
Take a listen! She is singing a song!
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 識[sik1|to know how to] 唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！
===
Obviously, I don't know how to sing! (No split is needed for base dictionary infinitive forms).
```

### 3. Dancing & Jogging: `跳舞[tiu3mou5|to dance]` and `跑步[paau2bou6|to run]`

- `跳舞[tiu3mou5|to dance]` splits into `跳[tiu3|to jump]` and `舞[mou5|dance]`.
- `跑步[paau2bou6|to run]` splits into `跑[paau2|to run]` and `步[bou6|step]`.

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 去[heoi3|go] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 跑步[paau2bou6|to run] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I went jogging. (Note: Here, "jogging" is a noun phrase following "went," so no split is needed inside the compound).
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 跑[paau2|to run] 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker] 步[bou6|step] 喇喎[laa3wo3|sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state]！ 聽日[ting1jat6|tomorrow] 先[sin1|first] 傾[king1|to chat] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！
===
I am jogging right now! Let's chat tomorrow instead!
```

### 4. Asking A-not-A Questions with Split-Verbs

When forming **A-not-A questions** (Verb-not-Verb) with split-verbs, you must
only duplicate the **verb part**, not the entire compound. It is a very common
learner mistake to say `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]` `唔[m4|not]`
`游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]` (swim-water-not-swim-water)—this is incorrect!

The correct structure is to repeat the verb part, and leave the object part at
the end:

> **Formula:** [Verb Part] + `唔[m4|not]` + [Verb Part] + [Object Part]

However, if you are asking about past completion or past experience involving an
aspect marker (like `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` or
`過[gwo3|past experience marker / to cross / than]`), you generally cannot use
the A-not-A verb duplication. Instead, use `有冇[jau5mou5|do you have?]`. For
example, `游[jau4|to swim]` `過[gwo3|past experience marker / to cross / than]`
`水[seoi2|water]` becomes `有冇[jau5mou5|do you have?]` `游[jau4|to swim]`
`過[gwo3|past experience marker / to cross / than]` `水[seoi2|water]`, not
`游[jau4|to swim]` `唔[m4|not]` `游[jau4|to swim]`
`過[gwo3|past experience marker / to cross / than]` `水[seoi2|water]`.

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 游唔游[jau4 m4 jau4|do you swim (A-not-A question form)] 水[seoi2|water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Are you swimming today?
```

```cantonese
你[nei5|you] 今晚[gam1maan5|tonight] 唱唔唱[coeng3 m4 coeng3|do you sing (A-not-A question form)] 歌[go1|song] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
Are you singing tonight?
```

## 2. Modifying Split-Verbs: Time, Duration & Frequency

When you want to describe **how long** (duration) or **how many times**
(frequency) you performed a split-verb action, the description of time or
frequency must also split the verb and be placed between the two halves:

> **Formula:** [Verb Part] + [Aspect Marker] + [Duration / Frequency] + [Object
>
> > Part]

### 1. Specifying Duration

To say you performed an action for "one hour" (`一[jat1|one]`
`個[go3|general classifier]` `鐘頭[zung1tau4|hour]`), place the duration
immediately after the verb part:

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 兩[loeng5|two] 個[go3|general classifier] 鐘頭[zung1tau4|hour] 水[seoi2|water] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He swam for two hours.
```

### 2. Specifying Frequency

To say you performed an action "three times" (`三[saam1|three]`
`次[ci3|time (occurrence)]`), insert the frequency after the verb part:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I] 唱[coeng3|to sing] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 三[saam1|three] 次[ci3|time (occurrence)] 歌[go1|song] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！
===
I have sung three times already!
```

## 3. Situational Dialogue: A Weekend Plan

Sau-han (A) and Wing-kit (B) are talking on Saturday about their weekend plans.

```dialog
A: Wing-kit，你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 唔[m4|not] 係[hai6|to be / yes] 去[heoi3|go] 游水[jau4seoi2|to swim] 咩[me1|sentence-final particle for doubt / what]？
   === Wing-kit, aren't you going swimming today?
B: 我[ngo5|I] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 已經[ji5ging1|already] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 水[seoi2|water] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]！ 今日[gam1jat6|today] 我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 去[heoi3|go] 跑步[paau2bou6|to run]。 你[nei5|you] 呢[ne1|sentence-final particle for queries]？
   === I already swam yesterday! Today I want to go jogging. How about you?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 去[heoi3|go] 唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。 你[nei5|you] 唱唔唱[coeng3 m4 coeng3|do you sing (A-not-A question form)] 歌[go1|song] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === I want to go sing. Are you singing?
B: 我[ngo5|I] 唔[m4|not] 識[sik1|to know how to] 唱歌[coeng3go1|to sing] 嘛[maa3|sentence-final particle indicating a statement is an obvious truth or reminder]！ 我[ngo5|I] 今晚[gam1maan5|tonight] 去[heoi3|go] 跳舞[tiu3mou5|to dance] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === I don't know how to sing, obviously! I'm going dancing tonight.
A: 哇[waa1|wow]，你[nei5|you] 又[jau6|also / again] 跑步[paau2bou6|to run] 又[jau6|also / again] 跳舞[tiu3mou5|to dance]，好[hou2|good / very] 忙[mong4|busy] 喎[wo3|sentence-final particle for surprise or warning]！
   === Wow, you both jog and dance, that's very busy!
B: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]，我[ngo5|I] 跑[paau2|to run] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 兩[loeng5|two] 個[go3|general classifier] 鐘頭[zung1tau4|hour] 步[bou6|step] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]，而家[ji4gaa1|now] 好[hou2|good / very] 攰[gui6|tired] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
   === Yes, I jogged for two hours, and now I'm very tired already.
```

## 4. Exercises

Test your understanding of Cantonese split-verbs, durations, and sentence
structures.

```exercise
question: |
  Translate the following sentence into Cantonese using correct semantic annotations:
  "I swam yesterday already."
  (Hint: Split the verb jau4seoi2 and use the completed aspect marker zo2 and particle laa3)
answer: 我[ngo5|I] 尋日[cam4jat6|yesterday] 游[jau4|to swim] 咗[zo2|completed action marker] 水[seoi2|water] 喇[laa3|sentence-final particle for change of state]。
explanation: |
  The verb `游水[jau4seoi2|to swim]` is a split-verb. The completed aspect marker `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` must be inserted between `游[jau4|to swim]` and `水[seoi2|water]`, resulting in `游[jau4|to swim]` `咗[zo2|completed action marker]` `水[seoi2|water]`.
```

```exercise
question: |
  Fill in the blank to say "He is jogging right now":
  佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 跑[paau2|to run] ________ 步[bou6|step] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
answer: 緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]
explanation: |
  The verb `跑步[paau2bou6|to run]` is a split-verb. To indicate an action in progress, we insert the continuous aspect suffix `緊[gan2|continuous aspect marker]` between the verb part `跑[paau2|to run]` and the object part `步[bou6|step]`.
```
