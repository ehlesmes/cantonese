In this chapter, we will master the art of telling time in Cantonese, including
how to ask and tell hours and minutes, and use the unique five-minute interval
system.

Let's dive in!

---

## 1. Asking and Telling the Hour: `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` and `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]`

In Cantonese, the word for "hour" or "o'clock" is `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`
(which functions as a classifier) or `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]` (which functions
as a noun).

### Telling the Hour

To state the hour, simply place the number before `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`:

- `一[jat1|one]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 1:00
- `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` —
  2:00 (Note: Always use `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]`, never
  `二[ji6|two]`, when telling hours!)
- `三[saam1|three]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 3:00
- `四[sei3|four]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 4:00
- `五[ng5|five]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 5:00
- `六[luk6|six]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 6:00
- `七[cat1|seven]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 7:00
- `八[baat3|eight]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 8:00
- `九[gau2|nine]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 9:00
- `十[sap6|ten]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 10:00
- `十一[sap6jat1|eleven]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 11:00
- `十二[sap6ji6|twelve]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 12:00

To emphasize "o'clock" or reference an exact hour, you can append
`鐘[zung1|clock / bell / hour]` to make `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]`. For example:
`三[saam1|three]` `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]` (3 o'clock sharp).

### Asking the Time

To ask "What time is it?", Cantonese uses the question pronoun
`幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when]`, which translates literally to "how many
hours":

```cantonese
依家[ji1gaa1|now] 幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
What time is it now?
```

You can also use the variant `而家[ji4gaa1|now / currently]` interchangeably
with `依家[ji1gaa1|now]`.

## 2. Adding Minutes and Half-Hours: `分[fan1|minute]`, `半[bun3|half]`, and `字[zi6|five-minute interval]`

When you need to be more precise than just the hour, Cantonese provides several
ways to express minutes and half-hours.

### The Formal/Standard Method: `分[fan1|minute]` and `分鐘[fan1zung1|minute]`

To express minutes directly, Cantonese uses the classifier `分[fan1|minute]`
(for points in time) or `分鐘[fan1zung1|minute]` (for duration or specific
points in time):

- `三[saam1|three]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` `十[sap6|ten]` `分[fan1|minute]` —
  3:10
- `十[sap6|ten]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` `二十[ji6sap6|twenty]` `五[ng5|five]`
  `分[fan1|minute]` — 10:25

When the minutes are in the single digits, it is customary to insert
`零[ling4|zero]` before the minutes to sound natural, just like we say "three oh
five" in English:

```cantonese
依家[ji1gaa1|now] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 零[ling4|zero] 五[ng5|five] 分[fan1|minute] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
It is 2:05 now.
```

### Expressing Half-Hours: `半[bun3|half]`

For half-past the hour, Cantonese uses the numeral `半[bun3|half]`, which is
placed directly after `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`:

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 四[sei3|four] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 半[bun3|half] 去[heoi3|to go] 超市[ciu1si5|supermarket] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
Let's go to the supermarket at 4:30.
```

### The Native Street Method: The Five-Minute `字[zi6|five-minute interval]`

In daily life, native Cantonese speakers rarely use the word `分[fan1|minute]`
for multiples of five. Instead, they use a highly colloquial system based on the
word `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` (literally "character" or "word").

This system comes from traditional analog clock faces, which feature twelve
numbers (or "characters") spaced at **5-minute intervals**. Therefore, each
number on the clock face represents one `字[zi6|five-minute interval]`:

- `一[jat1|one]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 5 minutes (the "1" on the clock)
- `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]`
  `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 10 minutes (the "2" on the clock)
- `三[saam1|three]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 15 minutes (the "3" on the clock)
- `四[sei3|four]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 20 minutes (the "4" on the clock)
- `五[ng5|five]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 25 minutes (the "5" on the clock)
- `六[luk6|six]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 30 minutes (the "6" on the clock, though
  `半[bun3|half]` is much more common!)
- `七[cat1|seven]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 35 minutes (the "7" on the clock)
- `八[baat3|eight]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 40 minutes (the "8" on the clock)
- `九[gau2|nine]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 45 minutes (the "9" on the clock)
- `十[sap6|ten]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 50 minutes (the "10" on the clock)
- `十一[sap6jat1|eleven]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 55 minutes (the "11" on the clock)

> [!NOTE] **No "Quarter" Word**: Unlike English or Mandarin, Cantonese does not
> have a specific word for "a quarter past" or "a quarter to." Instead, you must
> use `三個字[saam1go3zi6|15 minutes / a quarter]` and
> `九個字[gau2go3zi6|45 minutes / three quarters]`.

To use this in a sentence, use the pattern: **[Hour] + [Number of intervals] +
`個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` +
`字[zi6|five-minute interval]`**

```cantonese
依家[ji1gaa1|now] 三[saam1|three] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 三[saam1|three] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
It is 3:15 now.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 八[baat3|eight] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 九[gau2|nine] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 去[heoi3|to go] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I go at 8:45.
```

## 3. Conversational Dialogue

Below is a simple dialogue illustrating telling the time in daily life.

```dialog
A: 唔該[m4goi1|excuse me]，請問[cing2man6|may I ask] 依家[ji1gaa1|now] 幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Excuse me, may I ask what time it is now?
B: 依家[ji1gaa1|now] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 半[bun3|half] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === It is 2:30 now.
A: 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 半[bun3|half] 呀[aa4|sentence-final particle for questions]？ 唔該[m4goi1|thank you]。
   === 2:30? Thank you.
B: 你[nei5|you] 想[soeng2|to want to] 幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when] 去[heoi3|to go] 飲[jam2|to drink] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === What time do you want to go drink coffee?
A: 我[ngo5|I] 想[soeng2|to want to] 三[saam1|three] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 三[saam1|three] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 去[heoi3|to go] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
   === I want to go at 3:15.
B: 好[hou2|good] 呀[aa3|particle]，噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 三[saam1|three] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 三[saam1|three] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 見[gin3|to see / meet] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！
   === Sure, then let's meet at 3:15!
A: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
B: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye]！
   === Bye-bye!
```

## 4. Exercises

Review the time-telling terms and grammar introduced in this chapter:

```exercise
question: |
  Which of the following times represents "two o'clock and ten minutes" (2:10) colloquially in Cantonese?
  A) 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 十[sap6|ten] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval]
  B) 二[ji6|two] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval]
  C) 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval]
  D) 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 零[ling4|zero] 十[sap6|ten]
answer: C
explanation: For 2:10, Cantonese telling time uses `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` (never `二[ji6|two]`) and represents ten minutes as `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` (two five-minute intervals). Option C is the correct colloquial translation.
```
