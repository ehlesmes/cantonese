Now that you have learned how to locate objects and find your way around town,
it is time to talk about _when_ things happen and _how_ the weather is outside.
In this chapter, we will master the art of telling time in Cantonese, express
daily schedules using times of day, and pick up essential weather vocabulary.

Cantonese time-telling has a couple of highly logical rules, including a unique
and colorful system of five-minute intervals that you will hear on the streets
of Hong Kong every day.

---

## 1. Asking and Telling the Hour: `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` and `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]`

In Cantonese, the word for "hour" or "o'clock" is `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`
(which functions as a classifier) or `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]` (which functions
as a noun).

### Telling the Hour

To state the hour, simply place the number before `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`:

- `一[jat1|one]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 1:00
- `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` —
  2:00 (Note: Always use `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]`, never
  `二[ji6|two]`, when telling hours!)
- `三[saam1|three]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 3:00
- `四[sei3|four]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 4:00
- `五[ng5|five]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 5:00
- `六[luk6|six]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 6:00
- `七[cat1|seven]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 7:00
- `八[baat3|eight]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 8:00
- `九[gau2|nine]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 9:00
- `十[sap6|ten]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 10:00
- `十一[sap6jat1|eleven]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 11:00
- `十二[sap6ji6|twelve]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` — 12:00

To emphasize "o'clock" or reference an exact hour, you can append
`鐘[zung1|clock / bell / hour]` to make `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]`. For example:
`三[saam1|three]` `點鐘[dim2zung1|o'clock]` (3 o'clock sharp).

### Asking the Time

To ask "What time is it?", Cantonese uses the question pronoun
`幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when]`, which translates literally to "how many
hours":

```cantonese
依家[ji1gaa1|now] 幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
What time is it now?
```

You can also use the variant `而家[ji4gaa1|now / currently]` interchangeably
with `依家[ji1gaa1|now]`:

```cantonese
朝早[ziu1zou2|morning / in the morning] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work / be at work] 嘅[ge3|possessive particle] 時間[si4gaan3|time / period] 係[hai6|is] 幾點[gei2dim2|what time / when] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
===
What time is the morning commute?
```

## 2. Adding Minutes and Half-Hours: `分[fan1|minute]`, `半[bun3|half]`, and `字[zi6|five-minute interval]`

When you need to be more precise than just the hour, Cantonese provides several
ways to express minutes and half-hours.

### The Formal/Standard Method: `分[fan1|minute]` and `分鐘[fan1zung1|minute]`

To express minutes directly, Cantonese uses the classifier `分[fan1|minute]`
(for points in time) or `分鐘[fan1zung1|minute]` (for duration or specific
points in time):

- `三[saam1|three]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` `十[sap6|ten]` `分[fan1|minute]` —
  3:10
- `十[sap6|ten]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]` `二十[ji6sap6|twenty]` `五[ng5|five]`
  `分[fan1|minute]` — 10:25

When the minutes are in the single digits, it is customary to insert
`零[ling4|zero]` before the minutes to sound natural, just like we say "three oh
five" in English:

```cantonese
依家[ji1gaa1|now] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 零[ling4|zero] 五[ng5|five] 分[fan1|minute] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
It is 2:05 now.
```

### Expressing Half-Hours: `半[bun3|half]`

For half-past the hour, Cantonese uses the numeral `半[bun3|half]`, which is
placed directly after `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`:

```cantonese
我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 四[sei3|four] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 半[bun3|half] 去[heoi3|to go] 超市[ciu1si5|supermarket] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
Let's go to the supermarket at 4:30.
```

### The Native Street Method: The Five-Minute `字[zi6|five-minute interval]`

In daily life, native Cantonese speakers rarely use the word `分[fan1|minute]`
for multiples of five. Instead, they use a highly colloquial system based on the
word `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` (literally "character" or "word").

This system comes from traditional analog clock faces, which feature twelve
numbers (or "characters") spaced at **5-minute intervals**. Therefore, each
number on the clock face represents one `字[zi6|five-minute interval]`:

- `一[jat1|one]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 5 minutes (the "1" on the clock)
- `兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)]`
  `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 10 minutes (the "2" on the clock)
- `三[saam1|three]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 15 minutes (the "3" on the clock)
- `四[sei3|four]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 20 minutes (the "4" on the clock)
- `五[ng5|five]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 25 minutes (the "5" on the clock)
- `六[luk6|six]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 30 minutes (the "6" on the clock, though
  `半[bun3|half]` is much more common!)
- `七[cat1|seven]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 35 minutes (the "7" on the clock)
- `八[baat3|eight]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 40 minutes (the "8" on the clock)
- `九[gau2|nine]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 45 minutes (the "9" on the clock)
- `十[sap6|ten]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 50 minutes (the "10" on the clock)
- `十一[sap6jat1|eleven]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
  `字[zi6|five-minute interval]` — 55 minutes (the "11" on the clock)

> [!NOTE] **No "Quarter" Word**: Unlike English or Mandarin, Cantonese does not
> have a specific word for "a quarter past" or "a quarter to." Instead, you must
> use `三個字[saam1 go3 zi6|15 minutes / a quarter]` and
> `九個字[gau2 go3 zi6|45 minutes / three quarters]`.

To use this in a sentence, use the pattern: **[Hour] + [Number of intervals] +
`個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]` +
`字[zi6|five-minute interval]`**

```cantonese
依家[ji1gaa1|now] 三[saam1|three] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 三[saam1|three] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
===
It is 3:15 now.
```

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 八[baat3|eight] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 九[gau2|nine] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 放工[fong3gung1|to finish work / get off work] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
I get off work at 8:45.
```

This system is extremely high-frequency. When a local tells you to meet them at
"seven points four characters" (`七[cat1|seven]` `點[dim2|o'clock / hour]`
`四[sei3|four]` `個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit]`
`字[zi6|five-minute interval]`), they mean **7:20**!

## 3. Time of Day & Daily Schedules

In Cantonese, time words (such as hours, days, or times of day) function as
**adverbs of time**.

> [!IMPORTANT] **Word Order Rule**: Time words must be placed **before the
> verb** (usually directly after the subject, or at the very beginning of the
> sentence). Placing time words at the end of the sentence (like in English "I
> sleep at 10") is grammatically incorrect in Cantonese.

### Times of Day

To specify the time of day, Cantonese uses these common terms placed _before_
the hour:

- `朝早[ziu1zou2|morning / in the morning]` — Morning (typically 6:00 AM – 12:00
  PM)
- `晏晝[ngaan3zau3|noon / afternoon]` — Noon / Afternoon (typically 12:00 PM –
  6:00 PM) (Interchangeable with `下晝[haa6zau3|afternoon / in the afternoon]`)
- `下晝[haa6zau3|afternoon / in the afternoon]` — Afternoon (typically 12:00 PM
  – 6:00 PM)
- `夜晚[je6maan5|night / evening / at night]` — Evening / Night (typically 6:00
  PM – 12:00 AM)
- `半夜[bun3je6|midnight / middle of the night]` /
  `凌晨[ling4san4|early morning / before dawn]` — Midnight / Early morning
  (typically 12:00 AM – 6:00 AM)

Let's look at how these are integrated into schedules:

```cantonese
我[ngo5|I / me] 朝早[ziu1zou2|morning / in the morning] 九[gau2|nine] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 返工[faan1gung1|to go to work / be at work]。
===
I go to work at 9:00 AM.
```

```cantonese
佢[keoi5|he / she / it] 下晝[haa6zau3|afternoon / in the afternoon] 三[saam1|three] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 半[bun3|half] 食[sik6|to eat] 菠蘿包[bo1lo1baau1|pineapple bun] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]。
===
He eats a pineapple bun at 3:30 PM.
```

## 4. Weather & Seasons: Discussing the Elements

Being able to talk about the weather is crucial in Hong Kong, where the climate
ranges from hot and humid to typhoon-level storms.

Here is some essential weather and seasonal vocabulary:

- `落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain / raining]` — To rain / raining
- `打風[daa2fung1|typhoon / to have a typhoon]` — Typhoon
- `好[hou2|very / quite / extremely]` `熱[jit6|hot]` — Very hot
- `好[hou2|very / quite / extremely]` `凍[dung3|cold / freezing]` — Very cold
- `潮濕[ciu4sap1|humid / damp / moist]` — Humid
- `出太陽[ceot1taai3joeng4|the sun comes out / sunny]` — Sunny / The sun comes
  out
- `天晴[tin1cing4|sunny / clear sky]` — Clear sky / Sunny

When describing how it feels outside, you can use these adjectives directly with
`好[hou2|very / quite / extremely]`:

```cantonese
今日[gam1jat6|today] 天氣[tin1hei3|weather] 好[hou2|very / quite / extremely] 潮濕[ciu4sap1|humid / damp / moist] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
===
The weather is very humid today!
```

## 5. Situational Dialogue: A Weekend Catch-up

Here is a typical weekend phone conversation between two friends, Ka-ho (A) and
Wing-yan (B). Wing-yan is relaxing inside her room and Ka-ho wants to meet up to
drink coffee, but Wing-yan doesn't want to go out because of the humid weather
and rain. Pay close attention to how they tell the time.

```dialog
A: 喂[wai2|hey!]，Wing-yan！ 你[nei5|you] 而家[ji4gaa1|now / currently] 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 房[fong2|room / bedroom] 入面[jap6min6|inside] 休息[jau1sik1|to rest / take a break] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']！
   === Hey, Wing-yan! You are resting inside your room, obviously!
B: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]，今日[gam1jat6|today] 天氣[tin1hei3|weather] 好[hou2|very / quite / extremely] 潮濕[ciu4sap1|humid / damp / moist] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！
   === Yes, the weather is very humid today!
A: 依家[ji1gaa1|now] 下晝[haa6zau3|afternoon / in the afternoon] 三[saam1|three] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 半[bun3|half] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]！ 你[nei5|you] 今日[gam1jat6|today] 放假[fong3gaa3|to have a holiday / day off] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === It's 3:30 in the afternoon already! Do you have a day off today?
B: 係呀[hai6aa3|yes]！ 加上[gaa1soeng5|moreover / plus] 出面[ceot1min6|outside] 落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain / raining]，我[ngo5|I / me] 唔[m4|not / no (negative prefix)] 想[soeng2|to want to / would like to] 出街[ceot1gaai1|to go out]。
   === Yes! Plus it is raining outside, I don't want to go out.
A: 哎呀[aai1jaa3|interjection of surprise or annoyance]！ 落雨[lok6jyu5|to rain / raining] 都[dou1|also / even / already] 可以[ho2ji5|can / may / possible] 出街[ceot1gaai1|to go out] 㗎[gaa3|sentence-final particle asserting a point, combination of ge3 and aa3]！ 我哋[ngo5dei6|we / us] 下晝[haa6zau3|afternoon / in the afternoon] 四[sei3|four] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 三[saam1|three] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 去[heoi3|to go] 飲[jam2|to drink] 咖啡[gaa3fe1|coffee]，好唔好[hou2 m4 hou2|is that good] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Oh come on! Even if it is raining you can still go out! Let's go drink coffee at 4:15 PM, is that good?
B: 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。喺[hai2|at / in / on] 邊度[bin1dou6|where] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]？
   === Fine. Where at?
A: 喺[hai2|at / in / on] 地鐵站[dei6tit3zaam6|MTR station / subway station] 隔籬[gaak3lei4|next to / beside / neighbor] 嗰個[go2go3|that one] 茶餐廳[caa4caan1teng1|Hong Kong style café / diner] 囉[lo1|sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course']，好[hou2|very / quite / extremely] 近[gan6|near / close] 㗎[gaa3|sentence-final particle asserting a point, combination of ge3 and aa3]。
   === At that café next to the MTR station, obviously, it's very close.
B: 好[hou2|good / fine / very / quite] 呀[aa3|sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly]！ 噉[gam2|then / in that case / so] 我[ngo5|I / me] 四[sei3|four] 點[dim2|o'clock / hour] 兩[loeng5|two / pair (used with classifiers)] 個[go3|general measure word / individual / unit] 字[zi6|five-minute interval] 去[heoi3|to go] 嗰度[go2dou6|there] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]。
   === Sure! Then I will go there at 4:10 PM.
A: 搞掂[gaau2dim6|done / finished / settled / resolved]！ 嗰度[go2dou6|there] 見[gin3|to see / meet] 啦[laa1|sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action]，拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye / goodbye (loanword)]！
   === Done! See you there then, bye-bye!
B: 拜拜[baai1baai3|bye-bye / goodbye (loanword)]！
   === Bye-bye!
```
