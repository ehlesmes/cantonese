Chapter 18

Shopping, Bargaining & Spoken Quantifiers

Master spoken currency, bargaining, and clothing classifiers.

1. Currency & Spoken Prices in Cantonese

In daily Cantonese life, handling money and pricing is highly colloquial and differs significantly from formal written Chinese. When asking for prices, instead of the formal 幾多錢gei2 do1 cin2
how much money, you will frequently hear speakers use the shorter, snappier 幾錢gei2 cin2
how much money in casual street environments.

The Units of Currency

To talk about currency, spoken Cantonese employs unique units:

  • 蚊man1
    dollar    : The standard spoken unit of currency (equivalent to the written Hong Kong dollar symbol).
  • 毫hou4
    ten cents / dime    : Used for ten-cent intervals.
  • 毫子hou4 zi2
    ten-cent coin / dime    : The physical coin of ten cents.
  • 分fan1
    cent / minute    : Used for single cents (though rarely seen in modern transactions).

Spoken Pricing Shortcuts

Cantonese speakers love efficiency, which shows in pricing shortcuts:

  • 個go3
    general measure word     represents a dollar when followed by decimal values. The most common is 半bun3
    half     ($1.5 is 個半go3bun3
    one and a half    ), but it applies to others like 個二go3ji6
    one point two     ($1.2) or 個六go3luk6
    one point six     ($1.6). Note that for values of $2 and above, the number must precede 個go3
    measure word     (e.g., $2.5 is 兩個半loeng5 go3 bun3
    two and a half    , $3.5 is 三個半saam1 go3 bun3
    three and a half    ).
  • When counting two of a currency unit, always use 兩loeng5
    two     instead of 二ji6
    two    . E.g., <span class="vocab-term">兩<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>loeng5</strong><br/>two</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">蚊<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>man1</strong><br/>dollar</span></span> ($2).
  • When prices are in the hundreds or thousands, the final unit is often dropped if there are no smaller trailing digits. E.g., $380 is commonly said as 三百八saam1 baak3 baat3
    three hundred and eight (tens)     instead of the full 三百八十蚊saam1baak3 baat3sap6 man1
    380 dollars    .

2. Essential Clothing & Shopping Classifiers

Using the correct classifier (measure word) is essential to sounding natural when referring to different items in a shop or market. Avoid using 個go3
general measure word for everything! Here are the core classifiers for shopping:

  • 件gin6
    measure word for clothes, matters, and items    : Used for upper-body clothing (shirts, jackets, coats).

  • 條tiu4
    measure word for long, thin objects    : Used for long, thin items, including pants, skirts, ties, and necklaces.
    • Example: <span class="vocab-term">呢<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>ni1</strong><br/>this</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">條<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>tiu4</strong><br/>measure word for long, thin objects</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">褲<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>fu3</strong><br/>pants / trousers</span></span> (this pants)
  • 頂deng2
    measure word for hats    : Exclusively used for hats and caps.
    • Example: <span class="vocab-term">呢<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>ni1</strong><br/>this</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">頂<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>deng2</strong><br/>measure word for hats</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">帽<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>mou2</strong><br/>hat / cap</span></span> (this hat)
  • 對deoi3
    pair / couple    : Used for paired items like shoes, socks, or earrings.
    • Example: <span class="vocab-term">呢<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>ni1</strong><br/>this</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">對<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>deoi3</strong><br/>pair / couple</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">鞋<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>haai4</strong><br/>shoes</span></span> (these shoes)
  • 隻zek3
    measure word for animals or objects in pairs    : Used to refer to just one item of a pair.
    • Example: <span class="vocab-term">呢<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>ni1</strong><br/>this</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">隻<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>zek3</strong><br/>measure word for animals or objects in pairs</span></span> <span class="vocab-term">鞋<span class="tooltip-popover"><strong>haai4</strong><br/>shoes</span></span> (this single shoe)

3. The Art of Bargaining (講價gong2 gaa3
to bargain)

If you are shopping in Hong Kong’s vibrant street markets (like the Ladies' Market in Mong Kok or Temple Street in Yau Ma Tei), bargaining (講價gong2 gaa3
to bargain) is part of the fun and local culture.

Here are the key verbs and expressions you need to bargain effectively with the 老闆lou5baan2
boss / proprietor:

  • 平啲啦peng4 di1 laa1
    cheaper please    : The ultimate gold-standard bargaining phrase. It uses 平peng4
    cheap     with the comparative suffix 啲di1
    a bit     and the softening particle 啦laa1
    particle    .
  • 減價gaam2 gaa3
    to reduce price / go on sale    : To ask the boss to mark down the price.
  • 特價dat6 gaa3
    special price / bargain price    : Refer to items on a special bargain sale.
  • 有折jau5 zit3
    to have a discount     / 打折daa2 zit3
    to give a discount    : Referring to discounts. E.g., asking if there is a discount (折zit3
    discount    ).
  • 送sung3
    to give as a gift / include for free    : Ask for free additions or gifts (e.g., buy this and get that for free).
  • 算啦syun3 laa1
    forget it / let it be    : The ultimate walk-away strategy! If they won't budge on price, say 算啦syun3 laa1
    forget it / let it be     and walk. They will often call you back with a lower price!

5. Exercises

Use these exercises to test your understanding of currency, classifiers, and bargaining in Cantonese.

Practice Exercise

Which classifier should you use to ask for the price of a pair of shoes?
A) 件gin6
measure word for clothes, matters, and items
B) 條tiu4
measure word for long, thin objects
C) 對deoi3
pair / couple
D) 頂deng2
measure word for hats

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: C

對deoi3
pair / couple is the standard classifier for paired items like shoes or socks. 件gin6
measure word for clothes, matters, and items is for shirts, 條tiu4
measure word for long, thin objects is for pants/long thin objects, and 頂deng2
measure word for hats is for hats.
