Chapter 6

Café Dining & Ordering

Step inside a local Hong Kong café (茶餐廳) to order authentic food and drinks, learn essential classifiers like cup and plate, express choices, and master request softeners.

Now that you can handle convenience store transactions and count currency, it is time to experience the culinary heart of Hong Kong: the iconic 茶餐廳caa4caan1ting1
Hong Kong style café / diner.

A 茶餐廳caa4caan1ting1
Hong Kong style café / diner is a fast-paced, high-efficiency neighborhood eatery serving local comfort foods, sweet pastries, and unique fusion drinks. In this lesson, you will learn to order food like a local, specify options like hot or cold using 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions), count dishes using classifiers like 杯bui1
cup / glass / mug (classifier for drinks) and 碟dip6
plate / dish (classifier for food dishes), and use polite request softeners.

Let's dive in!

1. The Diner Lexicon: Core Drinks & Snacks

To get started at a 茶餐廳caa4caan1ting1
Hong Kong style café / diner, you must know the core staples. These items are loved by locals and represent the fusion food culture of Hong Kong:

  • 奶茶naai5caa4
    milk tea (typically Hong Kong-style)    : Creamy, strong black tea filtered through a sack and sweetened with evaporated milk.
  • 咖啡gaa3fe1
    coffee (loanword)    : Strong local-style coffee.
  • 鴛鴦jyun1joeng1
    yuanyang (drink mixing coffee and milk tea)    : An iconic blend of seven parts milk tea and three parts coffee.
  • 檸茶ning4caa4
    lemon tea    : Black tea served with fresh slices of lemon.
  • 菠蘿包bo1lo1baau1
    pineapple bun (sweet crusted bun, usually containing no pineapple)    : A sweet, fluffy bun topped with a crunchy, sugary crust resembling a pineapple skin.
  • 西多士sai1do1si2
    French toast (loanword)    : Thick-sliced bread filled with peanut butter, dipped in egg, deep-fried, and served with butter and syrup.
  • 蛋撻daan6taat1
    egg tart    : A sweet custard pastry encased in a flaky puff pastry or crumbly shortcrust shell.

to want / need / must / demand:

2. Cold or Hot? Expressing Choices with 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions)

When you order drinks in a Cantonese café, the server will almost always ask if you want it hot or cold.

Core Adjectives

  • 熱jit6
    hot    : Hot temperature (for drinks or food).
  • 凍dung3
    cold / iced    : Cold or iced temperature.

Alternative Questions with 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions)

To ask a question offering two alternatives, Cantonese uses the conjunction 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions). Unlike the Mandarin word for "or" (huòzhě), 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions) is reserved exclusively for questions where the listener must make a choice between option A and option B:

熱jit6
hot 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions) 凍dung3
cold / iced 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Hot or cold?

You can use 定ding6
or (used exclusively in alternative questions) to connect complete noun phrases or full actions:

3. Counting Foods & Drinks: Classifiers

In Cantonese, you cannot simply place a number directly before a noun. You must insert a classifier (also known as a measure word) between the number and the noun. Choosing the right classifier depends on the shape or nature of the item:

Key Classifiers

  • 個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit    : The general, round-item classifier. Used for buns, tarts, and general round foods (e.g., 菠蘿包bo1lo1baau1
    pineapple bun (sweet crusted bun, usually containing no pineapple)    , 蛋撻daan6taat1
    egg tart    ).
  • 杯bui1
    cup / glass / mug (classifier for drinks)    : The container classifier for liquids. Used for any glass, cup, or mug of drink (e.g., 奶茶naai5caa4
    milk tea (typically Hong Kong-style)    , 咖啡gaa3fe1
    coffee (loanword)    ).
  • 碟dip6
    plate / dish (classifier for food dishes)    : The flat-dish classifier. Used for plates of food, such as stir-fries or barbecued meats (e.g., 叉燒caa1siu1
    barbecued pork / char siu    ).

The Two Rules: 二ji6
two vs 兩loeng5
two / pair (used with classifiers)

Recall that when counting items using a classifier, the number "two" must be spoken as 兩loeng5
two / pair (used with classifiers), never as 二ji6
two:

  • Correct: 兩loeng5
    two / pair (used with classifiers)     杯bui1
    cup / glass / mug (classifier for drinks)     (two cups)
  • Incorrect: 二ji6
    two     杯bui1
    cup / glass / mug (classifier for drinks)

4. Polite Expressions & Request Softeners: 唔該m4goi1
excuse me / please / thank you (for service or help) vs 多謝do1ze6
thank you (for a gift, compliment, or hospitality) and the particle 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action

Being polite is essential when dining out. Cantonese has two distinct ways to say "thank you," and their usage depends entirely on the situation.

1. The Gratitude Divide: 唔該m4goi1
excuse me / please / thank you (for service or help) vs 多謝do1ze6
thank you (for a gift, compliment, or hospitality)

  • 唔該m4goi1
    excuse me / please / thank you (for service or help)    : Used to thank someone for a service, assistance, or favor (e.g., when a waiter brings you water, clears your table, or gives you the bill). It also means "please" or "excuse me" when starting a request.
  • 多謝do1ze6
    thank you (for a gift, compliment, or hospitality)    : Used to thank someone for a gift, compliment, money, or hospitality (e.g., when a friend buys you lunch, gives you a present, or compliments your Cantonese). In retail, clerks will say 多謝do1ze6
    thank you (for a gift, compliment, or hospitality)     to thank you for your payment or business.

Observe how they are used at the table:

I / me 想soeng2
to want to / would like to / think 埋單maai4daan1
to pay the bill 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
Excuse me, I would like to pay the bill.
多謝do1ze6
thank you (for a gift, compliment, or hospitality) 你nei5
you 嘅ge3
possessive particle 蛋撻daan6taat1

Thank you for your egg tart!

2. Softening Suggestions and Requests: Particle 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action

The sentence-final particle 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action is used to soften commands, make suggestions, or encourage action. Without it, a request can sound blunt or demanding. Adding 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action turns it into a polite request:

唔該m4goi1
excuse me / please / thank you (for service or help) 俾bei2
to give 一jat1
one 杯bui1
cup / glass / mug (classifier for drinks) 凍dung3
cold / iced 奶茶naai5caa4
milk tea 我ngo5
I / me 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action。
Please give me a glass of iced milk tea.
