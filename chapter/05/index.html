Chapter 5

Time-Telling & Continuous Action

Master the structures for telling time, expressing schedules, and describing ongoing activities in Cantonese. Learn to ask and tell hours and minutes, use the unique five-minute interval system, apply the continuous aspect particle gan2, and integrate the popular slang hea.

Welcome to Chapter 5! Now that you have learned how to locate objects and find your way around town, it is time to talk about when things happen and what is happening right now. In this chapter, we will master the art of telling time in Cantonese, express daily schedules using times of day, and learn how to describe ongoing actions.

Cantonese time-telling has a couple of highly logical rules, including a unique and colorful system of five-minute intervals that you will hear on the streets of Hong Kong every day. We will also introduce the highly versatile verbal aspect suffix 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) (similar to the English "-ing") and the iconic Cantonese slang verb Heahea1
to chill / hang out / slack off / procrastinate to describe the ultimate state of chilling out.

1. Asking and Telling the Hour: 點dim2
o'clock / hour and 點鐘dim2zung1
o'clock

In Cantonese, the word for "hour" or "o'clock" is 點dim2
o'clock / hour (which functions as a classifier) or 點鐘dim2zung1
o'clock (which functions as a noun).

Telling the Hour

To state the hour, simply place the number before 點dim2
o'clock / hour:

  • 一jat1
    one     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 1:00
  • 兩loeng5
    two / pair (used with classifiers)     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 2:00 (Note: Always use 兩loeng5
    two / pair (used with classifiers)    , never 二ji6
    two    , when telling hours!)
  • 三saam1
    three     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 3:00
  • 四sei3
    four     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 4:00
  • 五ng5
    five     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 5:00
  • 六luk6
    six     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 6:00
  • 七cat1
    seven     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 7:00
  • 八baat3
    eight     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 8:00
  • 九gau2
    nine     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 9:00
  • 十sap6
    ten     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 10:00
  • 十一sap6jat1
    eleven     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 11:00
  • 十二sap6ji6
    twelve     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     — 12:00

To emphasize "o'clock" or reference an exact hour, you can append 鐘zung1
clock / bell / hour to make 點鐘dim2zung1
o'clock. For example: 三saam1
three 點鐘dim2zung1
o'clock (3 o'clock sharp).

Asking the Time

To ask "What time is it?", Cantonese uses the question pronoun 幾點gei2dim2
what time / when, which translates literally to "how many hours":

依家ji1gaa1
now 幾點gei2dim2
what time / when 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
What time is it now?

You can also use the variant 而家ji4gaa1
now / currently interchangeably with 依家ji1gaa1
now:

而家ji4gaa1
now / currently 三saam1
three 點dim2
o'clock / hour 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action！
It is 3 o'clock now!

2. Adding Minutes and Half-Hours: 分fan1
minute, 半bun3
half, and 字zi6
five-minute interval

When you need to be more precise than just the hour, Cantonese provides several ways to express minutes and half-hours.

The Formal/Standard Method: 分fan1
minute and 分鐘fan1zung1
minute

To express minutes directly, Cantonese uses the classifier 分fan1
minute (for points in time) or 分鐘fan1zung1
minute (for duration or specific points in time):

  • 三saam1
    three     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     十sap6
    ten     分fan1
    minute     — 3:10
  • 十sap6
    ten     點dim2
    o'clock / hour     二十ji6sap6
    twenty     五ng5
    five     分fan1
    minute     — 10:25

When the minutes are in the single digits, it is customary to insert 零ling4
zero before the minutes to sound natural, just like we say "three oh five" in English:

依家ji1gaa1
now 兩loeng5
two / pair (used with classifiers) 點dim2
o'clock / hour 零ling4
zero 五ng5
five 分fan1
minute 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
It is 2:05 now.

Expressing Half-Hours: 半bun3
half

For half-past the hour, Cantonese uses the numeral 半bun3
half, which is placed directly after 點dim2
o'clock / hour:

我哋ngo5dei6
we / us 四sei3
four 點dim2
o'clock / hour 半bun3
half 去heoi3
to go 超市ciu1si5
supermarket 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action。
Let's go to the supermarket at 4:30.

The Native Street Method: The Five-Minute 字zi6
five-minute interval

In daily life, native Cantonese speakers rarely use the word 分fan1
minute for multiples of five. Instead, they use a highly colloquial system based on the word 字zi6
five-minute interval (literally "character" or "word").

This system comes from traditional analog clock faces, which feature twelve numbers (or "characters") spaced at 5-minute intervals. Therefore, each number on the clock face represents one 字zi6
five-minute interval:

  • 一jat1
    one     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 5 minutes (the "1" on the clock)
  • 兩loeng5
    two / pair (used with classifiers)     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 10 minutes (the "2" on the clock)
  • 三saam1
    three     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 15 minutes (the "3" on the clock)
  • 四sei3
    four     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 20 minutes (the "4" on the clock)
  • 五ng5
    five     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 25 minutes (the "5" on the clock)
  • 六luk6
    six     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 30 minutes (the "6" on the clock, though 半bun3
    half     is much more common!)
  • 七cat1
    seven     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 35 minutes (the "7" on the clock)
  • 八baat3
    eight     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 40 minutes (the "8" on the clock)
  • 九gau2
    nine     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 45 minutes (the "9" on the clock)
  • 十sap6
    ten     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 50 minutes (the "10" on the clock)
  • 十一sap6jat1
    eleven     個go3
    general measure word / individual / unit     字zi6
    five-minute interval     — 55 minutes (the "11" on the clock)

To use this in a sentence, use the pattern: [Hour] + [Number of intervals] + 個go3
general measure word / individual / unit + 字zi6

3. Time of Day & Daily Schedules

In Cantonese, time words (such as hours, days, or times of day) function as adverbs of time.

IMPORTANT
Word Order Rule: Time words must be placed before the verb (usually directly after the subject, or at the very beginning of the sentence). Placing time words at the end of the sentence (like in English "I sleep at 10") is grammatically incorrect in Cantonese.

Times of Day

To specify the time of day, Cantonese uses these common terms placed before the hour:

  • 朝早ziu1zou2
    morning / in the morning     — Morning (typically 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM)
  • 下晝haa6zau3
    afternoon / in the afternoon     — Afternoon (typically 12:00 PM – 6:00 PM)
  • 夜晚je6maan5
    night / evening / at night     — Evening / Night (typically 6:00 PM – 12:00 AM)

Let's look at how these are integrated into schedules:

我ngo5
I / me 朝早ziu1zou2
morning / in the morning 九gau2
nine 點dim2
o'clock / hour 返工faan1gung1
to go to work / be at work。
I go to work at 9:00 AM.
佢keoi5
he / she / it 下晝haa6zau3
afternoon / in the afternoon 三saam1
three 點dim2
o'clock / hour 半bun3
half 食sik6
to eat 菠蘿包bo1lo1baau1
pineapple bun 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
He eats a pineapple bun at 3:30 PM.
我哋ngo5dei6
we / us 夜晚je6maan5
night / evening / at night 十sap6
ten 點dim2
o'clock / hour 兩loeng5
two / pair (used with classifiers) 個go3
general measure word / individual / unit 字zi6
five-minute interval 食飯sik6faan6
to eat a meal / have dinner 囉lo1
sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course'！

4. Ongoing Action: Particle 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) & Slang Heahea1
to chill / hang out / slack off / procrastinate

When you want to describe an action that is happening right now, Cantonese uses the verbal aspect suffix 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect).

The Continuous Aspect Pattern

The particle 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) attaches directly to the verb to indicate that the action is currently in progress:

This is equivalent to the English "-ing" or the Mandarin zhèngzài (in progress):

我ngo5
I / me 食sik6
to eat 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 飯faan6
cooked rice / meal 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
I am eating a meal.
佢哋keoi5dei6
they / them 飲jam2
to drink 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 凍dung3
cold / iced 奶茶naai5caa4
milk tea 囉lo1
sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course'！
They are drinking iced milk tea, obviously!

The Ultimate Slang: Heahea1
to chill / hang out / slack off / procrastinate

One of the most famous and beloved colloquial words in modern Cantonese is the verb Heahea1
to chill / hang out / slack off / procrastinate. It has no standard Chinese character (though the English letters "Hea" are universally written by locals).

Heahea1
to chill / hang out / slack off / procrastinate can mean:

  1. To chill or hang out without a specific purpose.
  2. To slack off, be lazy, or procrastinate at work or school.
  3. Easy, relaxed, or low-effort (when used as an adjective).

When combined with our continuous aspect marker, it becomes the ultimate description of modern relaxation:

你nei5
you 做zou6
to do / make / perform 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 咩嘢me1je5
what / what kind of thing 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
What are you doing right now?
我ngo5
I / me Heahea1
to chill / hang out / slack off / procrastinate 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 囉lo1
sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course'！
I am chilling out, obviously!
