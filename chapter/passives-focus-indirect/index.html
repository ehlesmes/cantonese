Chapter 17

Passives, Focus & Indirect Speech

Master spoken passive structures using bei2, emphasize and clarify points using zik1hai6, and report statements or news using the verb waa6.

In this lesson, we will cover three advanced, high-frequency grammatical pillars that will elevate your conversational flow to native speed and structure:

  1. The Passive Voice: Structuring passive statements using 俾bei2
    by / to give    .
  2. Focus & Clarification: Defining or emphasizing statements using 即係zik1hai6
    that is / namely / in other words / meaning    .
  3. Indirect Speech & Mistaken Beliefs: Reporting spoken messages using 話waa6
    to say / tell     and expressing mistaken assumptions using 以為ji5wai4
    to mistakenly think / assume / believe    .

Mastering these tools will allow you to recount stories, clarify misunderstandings, and describe unexpected workplace or daily life occurrences with ease.

1. The Passive Voice: 俾bei2
by / to give

Unlike formal written Chinese which relies on the passive marker (Mandarin: bèi), colloquial spoken Cantonese uses 俾bei2
by / to give as its primary passive voice marker.

In Cantonese, the passive voice is highly active and structural: you must explicitly state the "agent" (the person or thing performing the action). If the agent is unknown, native speakers use 人jan4
person / people / human or 人哋jan4dei6
others / other people as a placeholder.

Passive Voice Structure

  • Formula: [Receiver/Object] + 俾bei2
    by / to give     + [Agent/Placeholder] + [Verb] + [Aspect/Suffixes]

Let's look at how this works in practice:

1. Known Agent (e.g. The Boss)

我ngo5
I / me 俾bei2
by / to give 老細lou5sai3
boss / employer (colloquial) 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)。
I was scolded by the boss.
NOTE

In Cantonese, the verb 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech carries a double meaning. While it literally means "to say", when used in passive contexts or when directed at someone (e.g., 俾bei2
by 人jan4
people / others 話waa6
to scold), it colloquially means "to scold" or "criticize".

2. Unknown Agent / General People (using 人jan4
person / people / human or 人哋jan4dei6
others / other people)

If your wallet or phone is stolen, or something is broken, and you do not know exactly who did it, you cannot omit the agent in Cantonese. You must use 人jan4
person / people / human or 人哋jan4dei6
others / other people as the agent:

我ngo5
I / me 個go3
measure word for general items 銀包ngan4baau1
wallet / purse 俾bei2
by / to give 人jan4
person / people / human 偷tau1
to steal 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)！
My wallet was stolen by someone! (Literally: "My wallet by someone stolen!")
我ngo5
I / me 部bou6
measure word for machines/phones 電話din6waa2
telephone / phone 俾bei2
by / to give 人哋jan4dei6
others / other people 打爛da2laan6
to break / smash / shatter 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)。
My phone was smashed by others.
NOTE

If the receiver (the object being acted upon) is already obvious from the context of the conversation, it can be omitted. For example, you can simply exclaim 俾bei2
by / to give 人jan4
person / people / human 偷tau1
to steal 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect)！(It got stolen by someone!) without explicitly stating "my wallet".

2. Focus, Defining & Emphasizing: 即係zik1hai6
that is / namely / in other words / meaning

When speaking Cantonese, you will hear 即係zik1hai6
that is / namely / in other words / meaning in almost every conversation. It is a highly versatile focus marker used to:

  • Define or clarify a statement ("namely", "that is to say", "which means").
  • Summarize a point or draw a conclusion ("basically", "in other words").

Clarifying Actions and Statuses

你nei5
you 唔m4
not 返工faan1gung1
to go to work / be at work？ 即係zik1hai6
that is / namely / in other words / meaning 你nei5
you 放假fong3gaa3
to have a holiday / day off 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action！
You're not going to work? Basically, you have a day off!

We can also pair 即係zik1hai6
that is / namely / in other words / meaning with 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech to form 即係zik1hai6
that is / namely 話waa6
to say / tell, which translates precisely to "which is to say that..." or "which means that...":

即係zik1hai6
that is / namely / in other words / meaning 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech 佢keoi5
he / she / it 唔m4
not 嚟lei4
to come 啦laa1
sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action。
Which is to say he is not coming.

3. Indirect Speech & Mistaken Assumptions: 話waa6
to say / tell & 以為ji5wai4
to mistakenly think / assume / believe

Recounting conversations and describing assumptions that turned out to be false are crucial elements of native communication.

1. Indirect Speech: 話waa6
to say / tell ... 知zi1
to know

In Cantonese, the verb 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech acts both as "to say" and as a conjunction "that" to introduce reported clauses.

To express "tell someone", spoken Cantonese uses the structure 話waa6
to say + [Person] + 知zi1
to know (literally: "say to person to let them know"):

佢keoi5
he / she / it 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech 我ngo5
I / me 知zi1
to know 佢keoi5
he / she / it 好hou2
good / fine / very / quite 攰gui6
tired / exhausted 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
He told me that he is very tired.

You can also use this structure to report more complex situations or news:

同事tung4si6
colleague 話waa6
to say / tell / words / speech 我ngo5
I / me 知zi1
to know 老細lou5sai3
boss 聽日ting1jat6
tomorrow 唔m4
not 返工faan1gung1
go to work。
My colleague told me that the boss is not going to work tomorrow.

Alternatively, you can use 話俾waa6bei2
to tell / say to + [Person] + 知zi1
to know or 話俾waa6bei2
to tell / say to + [Person] + 聽teng1
to listen / hear, which are extremely common variations with the exact same meaning:

佢keoi5
he / she / it 話俾waa6bei2
to tell / say to 我ngo5
I / me 聽teng1
to listen / hear 佢keoi5
he / she / it 好hou2
good / fine / very / quite 攰gui6
tired / exhausted 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
He told me that he is very tired.

And for a complex sentence:

阿媽aa3maa1
mother 話俾waa6bei2
to tell / say to 我哋ngo5dei6
us 聽teng1
to listen / hear 今晚gam1maan5
tonight 出去ceot1heoi3
go out 食飯sik6faan6
eat a meal。
Mom told us that tonight we are going out to eat a meal.

2. Mistaken Assumptions: 以為ji5wai4
to mistakenly think / assume / believe

When you assumed something to be true but it turned out to be false (e.g., "I thought you were at work, but you are not"), Cantonese uses the verb 以為ji5wai4
to mistakenly think / assume / believe.

我ngo5
I / me 以為ji5wai4
to mistakenly think / assume / believe 你nei5
you 返工faan1gung1
to go to work / be at work 緊gan2
continuous aspect particle indicating action in progress。
I mistakenly thought you were working.
