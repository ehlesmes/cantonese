Chapter 1

Greetings & Who You Are

Learn how to say hello, introduce yourself and others, make polite conversation, and ask basic identity questions using pronouns, equative verbs, and A-not-A question structures.

Welcome to your first active speaking lesson! In this chapter, we will learn how to introduce ourselves, greet others, and ask simple questions about identity and language skills.

Rather than speaking in a stiff, robotic manner, you will learn to use essential Cantonese conversational particles from day one to sound natural, polite, and culturally authentic.

1. Natural Greetings

To say hello in Cantonese, the standard phrase is 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi. However, in casual, everyday speech, saying just 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi can sound a bit formal or detached. To make it sound warm and friendly, native speakers almost always append the softening particle 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly to the end:

你好nei5hou2
hello / hi呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly！
Hello there!

Other Everyday Greetings

  • 早晨zou2san4
    good morning    : The standard way to say "good morning."
  • 早頭zou2tau2
    good night    : Used when going to bed.
  • 拜拜baai1baai3
    bye-bye / goodbye (loanword)    : The most common colloquial way to say goodbye.

2. Personal Pronouns & The Equative Verb 係hai6
to be

Pronouns

Cantonese pronouns are extremely logical and consistent. To make any singular pronoun plural, you simply attach the suffix 哋dei6
plural suffix, which acts as a group marker:

  • Singular Pronouns:
    • 我ngo5
      I / me      : "I" or "me"
    • 你nei5
      you      : "you"
    • 佢keoi5
      he / she / it      : "he", "she", or "it"
  • Plural Pronouns:
    • 我哋ngo5dei6
      we / us      : "we" or "us"
    • 你哋nei5dei6
      you (plural)      : "you (plural)"
    • 佢哋keoi5dei6
      they / them      : "they" or "them"
NOTE

Cantonese does not differentiate gender in its spoken pronouns. The word 佢keoi5
he / she / it is used for "he", "she", and "it" alike.

The Equative 係hai6
to be

To express "to be" (am, is, are), use the verb 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes. The standard sentence structure is: Subject + 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes + Identity/Nationality

For example:

我ngo5
I / me 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 香港hoeng1gong2
Hong Kong 人jan4
person / people / human。
I am a Hong Konger.

The Possessive Particle 嘅ge3
possessive particle

To show possession or relationship (like "'s" or "of" in English), attach the possessive particle 嘅ge3
possessive particle / nominalizing particle / final assertive particle after a pronoun or noun:

佢哋keoi5dei6
they / them 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 我哋ngo5dei6
we / us 嘅ge3
possessive particle / nominalizing particle / final assertive particle 朋友pang4jau5
friend。
They are our friends.

3. Negation & A-not-A Questions

Negating with 唔m4
not

To negate 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes, place the negative adverb 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) directly before it, forming 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes ("is not / are not"):

我ngo5
I / me 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 美國mei5gwok3
United States / America 人jan4
person / people / human。
I am not American.
佢keoi5
he / she / it 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 靚仔leng3zai2
handsome boy / handsome man / handsome。
He is not handsome.

sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly is frequently added to negative statements and declarations to soften the tone. For example, stating 我ngo5
I 唔m4
not 識sik1
to know 講gong2
to speak 廣東話gwong2dung1waa2
Cantonese language 呀aa3
particle sounds much friendlier and less abrupt than stating it without the particle.

Asking Questions with 係唔係hai6 m4 hai6
is it or not

In Cantonese, the most natural way to ask yes-no questions is the A-not-A construction, where you present the verb, the negative marker 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix), and the verb again. For the verb 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes, this forms the compound expression 係唔係hai6 m4 hai6
is it or not? / is that right? (A-not-A question form).

你nei5
you 係唔係hai6 m4 hai6
is it or not? / is that right? (A-not-A question form) 英國jing1gwok3
United Kingdom / Britain 人jan4
person / people / human 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Are you a British person?
TIP

Notice that we add the friendly softening particle 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly to the end of the question. This softens the inquiry so it sounds like a warm, polite question rather than a cold interrogation.

4. Asking for Names & Languages

Asking "What is your name?"

To ask someone's name, we use the verb 叫giu3
to be called / to call, the pronoun 咩嘢me1je5
what / what kind of thing, and the noun 名meng2
name:

你nei5
you 叫giu3
to be called / to call 咩嘢me1je5
what / what kind of thing 名meng2
name 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
What is your name?

To answer, simply state: 我ngo5
I / me 叫giu3
to be called / to call [Name]. For example:

我ngo5
I / me 叫giu3
to be called / to call David。
My name is David.

Speaking Languages with 識sik1
to know

To express that you know how to speak a language, use the verb 識sik1
to know / speak (a language) / have a skill (which indicates a learned ability) paired with 講gong2
to speak / say / talk:

我ngo5
I / me 識sik1
to know / speak (a language) / have a skill 講gong2
to speak / say / talk 廣東話gwong2dung1waa2
Cantonese language 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly！
I know how to speak Cantonese!

To ask someone if they speak a language, apply the A-not-A pattern to the verb 識sik1
to know / speak (a language) / have a skill, creating 識唔識sik1 m4 sik1
know how to / know or not (A-not-A question form):

你nei5
you 識唔識sik1 m4 sik1
know how to / know or not (A-not-A question form) 講gong2
to speak / say / talk 英文jing1man2
English language 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Do you know how to speak English?
TIP

Bounce-back Questions with 呢ne1
question particle To casually bounce a question back to someone (like saying "How about you?"), attach the query particle 呢ne1
sentence-final particle for queries (how about / what about) to a pronoun. For example: 你nei5
you 呢ne1
sentence-final particle for queries (how about / what about)？means "How about you?".
