Chapter 26

Narrative Cohesion & Complex Connectives

Master cohesive storytelling in colloquial Cantonese. Form complex connected paragraphs using advanced conjunctions such as because... therefore, although... but, not only... but also, even, since, and instead.

Storytelling and conversation are not just series of isolated sentences. To sound like a natural Cantonese speaker, you need to connect your thoughts smoothly, express logical relationships, highlight contrasts, and build arguments.

In this chapter, we will master Narrative Cohesion using six essential pairs and groups of colloquial Cantonese connectives:

  1. Cause & Effect: 因為jan1wai6
    because     ... 所以so2ji5
    therefore / so
  2. Concession & Contrast: 雖然seoi1jin4
    although / even though     ... 但係daan6hai6
    but / however
  3. Incremental Addition: 唔單止m4daan1zi2
    not only     ... 仲zung6
    still / yet
  4. Extreme Emphasizers & Transitions:
    • 甚至sam6zi3
      even / so much so that
    • 既然gei3jin4
      since / given that
    • 反而faan2ji4
      on the contrary / instead

to stir-fry / speculate / fire 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect) 魷魚jau4jyu2
squid, and how she uses these connectives to tell a cohesive, engaging story.

1. Cause & Effect: 因為jan1wai6
because ... 所以so2ji5
therefore / so

To explain why something happened and what the result was, Cantonese speakers use the classic pair 因為jan1wai6
because ... 所以so2ji5
therefore / so. While English speakers rarely use "because" and "so" in the same sentence, in Cantonese it is extremely common and natural to use both together.

Structure:

  • 因為jan1wai6
    because     [Cause], 所以so2ji5
    therefore / so     [Result]

Let's look at some daily examples:

因為jan1wai6
because 佢keoi5
he / she / it 琴日kam4jat6
yesterday (variant) 唔開心m4hoi1sam1
sad / unhappy， 所以so2ji5
therefore / so 去heoi3
to go 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect) 玩waan2
to play / have fun / enjoy。
Because she was sad yesterday, therefore she went to have fun.

Another example:

2. Concession & Contrast: 雖然seoi1jin4
although / even though ... 但係daan6hai6
but / however

When you want to contrast two ideas ("although A, but B"), you use 雖然seoi1jin4
although / even though ... 但係daan6hai6
but / however. Similar to the cause-and-effect pair, Cantonese speakers frequently use both parts in the same sentence to frame the contrast clearly.

Structure:

  • 雖然seoi1jin4

    but / however     [Contrast/Result]

3. Incremental Addition: 唔單止m4daan1zi2
not only ... 仲zung6
still / yet

To add emphasis by listing multiple facts ("not only A, but also B"), Cantonese uses 唔單止m4daan1zi2
not only paired with 仲zung6
still / yet. This construction builds narrative momentum, making your description sound highly engaging and expressive.

Structure:

  • 唔單止m4daan1zi2
    not only     [Fact A], 仲zung6
    still / yet     [Fact B] 添tim1
    particle indicating in addition / as well / furthermore

Let's look at an example:

to play / have fun / enjoy 添tim1
particle indicating in addition / as well / furthermore！
Not only is he rich, he also really knows how to have fun!

Another example:

我ngo5

particle indicating in addition / as well / furthermore。

4. Extreme Emphasizers & Transitions: 甚至sam6zi3
even / so much so that, 既然gei3jin4
since / given that, 反而faan2ji4
on the contrary / instead, 跟住gan1zyu6
and then / next / follow, 其實kei4sat6
actually / in fact / as a matter of fact

To elevate your narrative fluency to an advanced level, you must master transitional and logical words that highlight extreme circumstances, logical consequences, sequential timelines, and unexpected outcomes:

  • Extreme Emphasizer: 甚至sam6zi3
    even / so much so that     is used to introduce an extreme case or build up to a surprising point.
  • Logical Consequence: 既然gei3jin4
    since / given that     establishes a premise that justifies a subsequent action ("since it is already the case that..."). It is almost always paired with 就zau6
    then / right away / immediately     or 噉gam2
    then / in that case / so     就zau6
    then     in the resulting clause.
  • Unexpected Contrast: 反而faan2ji4
    on the contrary / instead     highlights a result that is the opposite of what was expected.
  • Narrative Sequence: 跟住gan1zyu6
    and then / next / follow     is a high-frequency spoken connective used to sequence chronological events (equivalent to "and then").
  • Clarification / Reality check: 其實kei4sat6
    actually / in fact / as a matter of fact     is used to introduce an underlying reality, a concession, or to clarify a point.

Note on Placement:

  • 甚至sam6zi3
    even / so much so that    , 反而faan2ji4
    on the contrary / instead    , and 其實kei4sat6
    actually / in fact / as a matter of fact     usually come after the subject of the clause (though 其實kei4sat6
    actually / in fact / as a matter of fact     can also start a sentence).
  • 跟住gan1zyu6
    and then / next / follow     is placed at the beginning of the sequenced clause.
  • 既然gei3jin4
    since / given that     is placed at the beginning of the premise clause, either before or immediately after the subject.
