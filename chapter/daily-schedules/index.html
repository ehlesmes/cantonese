Chapter 11

Daily Schedules & Weather

Learn how to express times of day, discuss daily schedules with correct word order, and talk about weather and seasons in Cantonese.

In this chapter, we will learn how to discuss daily schedules and timing, use times of day as adverbs, and talk about weather and seasons.

Let's dive in!

1. Time of Day & Daily Schedules

In Cantonese, time words (such as hours, days, or times of day) function as adverbs of time.

IMPORTANT

Word Order Rule: Time words must be placed before the verb (usually directly after the subject, or at the very beginning of the sentence). Placing time words at the end of the sentence (like in English "I sleep at 10") is grammatically incorrect in Cantonese.

Times of Day

To specify the time of day, Cantonese uses these common terms placed before the hour:

  • 朝早ziu1zou2
    in the morning     — Morning (typically 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM)
  • 晏晝aan3zau3
    noon / afternoon     — Noon / Afternoon (typically 12:00 PM – 6:00 PM) (Interchangeable with 下晝haa6zau3
    in the afternoon    )
  • 下晝haa6zau3
    in the afternoon     — Afternoon (typically 12:00 PM – 6:00 PM)
  • 夜晚je6maan5
    at night / evening     — Evening / Night (typically 6:00 PM – 12:00 AM)
  • 半夜bun3je6
    middle of the night     / 凌晨ling4san4
    early morning / before dawn     — Midnight / Early morning (typically 12:00 AM – 6:00 AM)

Let's look at how these are integrated into schedules:

2. Weather & Seasons: Discussing the Elements

Being able to talk about the weather is crucial in Hong Kong, where the climate ranges from hot and humid to typhoon-level storms.

Here is some essential weather and seasonal vocabulary:

  • 落雨lok6jyu5
    to rain / raining     — To rain / raining
  • 打風daa2fung1
    to have a typhoon     — Typhoon
  • 好hou2
    very / quite / extremely     熱jit6
    hot     — Very hot
  • 好hou2
    very / quite / extremely     凍dung3
    cold / freezing     — Very cold
  • 潮濕ciu4sap1
    humid / damp / moist     — Humid
  • 出太陽ceot1taai3joeng4
    the sun comes out / sunny     — Sunny / The sun comes out
  • 天晴tin1cing4
    sunny / clear sky     — Clear sky / Sunny

When describing how it feels outside, you can use these adjectives directly with 好hou2
very / quite / extremely:

今日gam1jat6
today 天氣tin1hei3
weather 好hou2
very / quite / extremely 潮濕ciu4sap1
humid / damp / moist 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly！
The weather is very humid today!

Practice Exercise

Where must time words (like morning/afternoon) be placed in a Cantonese sentence?
A) At the end of the sentence.
B) Before the verb (either after the subject or at the beginning of the sentence).
C) Between the verb and the object.

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: B

In Cantonese word order, time adverbs must always be placed before the verb, not at the end of the sentence.
