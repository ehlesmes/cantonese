Chapter 2

Greetings & Who You Are

Learn how to say hello, make polite greetings, and introduce yourself and family members using pronouns, possessives, and the equative verb 係.

Welcome to your first active speaking lesson! In this chapter, we will learn how to greet others, introduce ourselves, and discuss family relations.

Rather than speaking in a stiff, robotic manner, you will learn to use essential Cantonese conversational particles from day one to sound natural, polite, and culturally authentic.

1. Natural Greetings

To say hello in Cantonese, the standard phrase is 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi. However, in casual, everyday speech, saying just 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi can sound a bit formal or detached. To make it sound warm and friendly, native speakers almost always append the softening particle 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly to the end:

你好nei5hou2
hello / hi呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly！
Hello there!

Other Everyday Greetings

  • 早晨zou2san4
    good morning    : The standard way to say "good morning."
  • 早唞zou2tau2
    good night    : Used when going to bed.
  • 拜拜baai1baai3
    bye-bye / goodbye (loanword)    : The most common colloquial way to say goodbye.

2. Personal Pronouns & The Equative Verb 係hai6
to be

Pronouns

Cantonese pronouns are extremely logical and consistent. To make any singular pronoun plural, you simply attach the suffix 哋dei6
plural suffix, which acts as a group marker:

  • Singular Pronouns:
    • 我ngo5
      I / me      : "I" or "me"
    • 你nei5
      you      : "you"
    • 佢keoi5
      he / she / it      : "he", "she", or "it"
  • Plural Pronouns:
    • 我哋ngo5dei6
      we / us      : "we" or "us"
    • 你哋nei5dei6
      you (plural)      : "you (plural)"
    • 佢哋keoi5dei6
      they / them      : "they" or "them"
  • General & Reference Pronouns:
    • 大家daai6gaa1
      everyone      : "everyone"
    • 自己zi6gei2
      oneself / own / self      : "oneself / own"
NOTE

Cantonese does not differentiate gender in its spoken pronouns. The word 佢keoi5
he / she / it is used for "he", "she", and "it" alike.

The Equative 係hai6
to be

To express "to be" (am, is, are), use the verb 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes. The standard sentence structure is: Subject + 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes + Identity/Noun

For example:

我ngo5
I / me 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 香港hoeng1gong2
Hong Kong 人jan4
person / people / human。
I am a Hong Konger.

The Possessive Particle 嘅ge3
possessive particle

To show possession or relationship (like "'s" or "of" in English), attach the possessive particle 嘅ge3
possessive particle / nominalizing particle / final assertive particle after a pronoun or noun:

佢哋keoi5dei6
they / them 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 我哋ngo5dei6
we / us 嘅ge3
possessive particle / nominalizing particle / final assertive particle 朋友pang4jau5
friend。
They are our friends.

  • 阿哥aa3go1

4. Exercises

Practice Exercise

Which of the following is the most natural way to say "Hello" in a friendly, casual Cantonese conversation?
A) 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi
B) 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly
C) 早晨zou2san4
good morning
D) 拜拜baai1baai3
bye-bye / goodbye (loanword)

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: B

While 你好nei5hou2
hello / hi is correct, adding the friendly softening particle 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly makes it sound much warmer and more natural to native speakers.
