Chapter 8

Existential Presence & Prepositions

Master existential verbs jau and mou to express presence or absence, and locate specific subjects using the locative preposition hai.

In this lesson, we will learn how to describe where objects and places are in Cantonese, focusing on stating presence and pinpointing locations.

Let's dive in!

1. Stating Presence: Existential 有jau5
to have / there is and 冇mou5
to not have

In Cantonese, the verbs 有jau5
to have / there is and 冇mou5
to not have serve a dual purpose. They express personal possession (e.g., "I have a phone") and existential presence (e.g., "There is a phone here").

Existential Sentence Pattern

To state that something exists in a specific place, Cantonese uses the following structure:

Formula: [Location] + 有jau5
to have / there is / 冇mou5
to not have + [Noun]

This is equivalent to the English "There is/are..." or "There is/are no...". Unlike English, which uses "there is" as a dummy subject, Cantonese anchors the sentence directly to the physical location:

呢度ni1dou6
here 有jau5
to have / there is 一jat1
one 隻zek3
classifier for animals 貓maau1
cat 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
There is a cat here.
嗰度go2dou6
there 冇mou5
to not have 廁所ci3so2
toilet 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
There is no restroom there.

Asking Existential Questions (A-not-A Form)

To ask whether something exists in a place, we combine 有jau5
to have / there is and 冇mou5
to not have into the A-not-A question form. In colloquial Cantonese, we use the spoken expression 有冇jau5mou5
do you have?:

呢度ni1dou6
here 有冇jau5mou5
do you have? 電腦din6nou5
computer 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Is there a computer here?

You can answer simply with 有jau5
to have / there is (yes, there is) or 冇mou5
to not have (no, there isn't).

2. Pinpointing Locations: Preposition 喺hai2
at / in / on

While 有jau5
to have / there is focuses on the presence of an object in a space, the preposition 喺hai2
at / in / on focuses on the location of a specific subject.

In Mandarin, the equivalent word is zài, but in colloquial Cantonese, you must always use 喺hai2
at / in / on.

Location Sentence Pattern

To state where a specific subject is located, use this structure:

Formula: [Subject] + 喺hai2
at / in / on + [Location]

我ngo5
I 喺hai2
at / in / on 公司gung1si1
company 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
I am at the office.
佢keoi5
he / she / it 喺hai2
at / in / on 學校hok6haau6
school 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
She is at school.

Asking "Where?" using 邊度bin1dou6
where

To ask where someone or something is, replace the location with the question pronoun 邊度bin1dou6
where:

你nei5
you 喺hai2
at / in / on 邊度bin1dou6
where 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Where are you?
銀包ngan4baau1
wallet 喺hai2
at / in / on 邊度bin1dou6
where 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Where is the wallet?
