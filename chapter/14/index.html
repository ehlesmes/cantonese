Chapter 14

Intensifiers, Exaggeration & Spoken Superlatives

Learn how to use Cantonese colloquial intensifiers to express extreme states, exaggerate emotions, and construct spoken superlative statements.

Welcome to Chapter 14! In this lesson, we will cover the vibrant world of Cantonese intensifiers and exaggeration. Spoken Cantonese is a deeply expressive and emotional language; native speakers rarely stick to mild descriptors. To sound authentic and colloquial, you must master the art of intensifying adjectives, expressing excess, and making spoken superlative statements.

We will focus on four main areas:

  1. Post-Adjective Intensifiers: Using 死sei2
    extremely / to death    , 極gik6
    extremely / to the limit    , and 爆baau3
    explosively / extremely     directly after adjectives to express high intensity.
  2. Excessive Intensity: Expressing that something is "too" much using 得滯dak1zai6
    too / excessively    .
  3. Pre-Adjective Intensifiers & Superlatives: Using 鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
    extremely / awfully     and 最zeoi3
    most / superlative prefix    .
  4. Quantifiers: Structuring statements of absolute quantity using 所有so2jau5
    all / every     and 全部cyun4bou6
    all / entirely    .

Mastering these patterns will breathe life into your Cantonese, allowing you to react with authentic excitement, complaint, or delight.

1. Post-Adjective Intensifiers: 死sei2
extremely / to death, 極gik6
extremely / to the limit, and 爆baau3
explosively / extremely

In Cantonese, instead of placing an adverb before the adjective (like "very" in English), it is highly common and natural to attach an intensifying suffix directly after the adjective.

1. 死sei2
extremely / to death (Tired to Death, Expensive to Death)

Similar to the English "to death" (e.g., "starving to death"), 死sei2
extremely / to death is appended to adjectives to express an extreme, often negative, state.

  • Structure: [Adjective] + 死sei2
    extremely / to death     + laa1 / aa3
我ngo5
I 今日gam1jat6
today 返工faan1gung1
to go to work 攰gui6
tired 死sei2
extremely 啦laa1
particle！
I am tired to death from going to work today!
呢度ni1dou6
here 嘅ge3
possessive particle 嘢食je5sik6
food 貴gwai3
expensive 死sei2
extremely 呀aa3
particle！
The food here is extremely expensive!

2. 爆baau3
explosively / extremely (Explosively Busy, Explosively Spicy)

Originally slang but now completely integrated into everyday spoken Cantonese, 爆baau3
explosively / extremely adds a highly vibrant, modern flavor to your statements. It can be used for both positive and negative situations.

  • Structure: [Adjective] + 爆baau3
    explosively / extremely     + aa3
我ngo5
I 今日gam1jat6
today 忙mong4
busy 爆baau3
explosively 呀aa3
particle！
I am extremely busy today!
呢ni1
this 碟dip6
plate 雞gai1
chicken 辣laat6
spicy 爆baau3
explosively 呀aa3
particle！
This plate of chicken is explosively spicy!

3. 極gik6
extremely / to the limit and 到極dou3gik6
extremely / to the absolute limit

極gik6
extremely / to the limit is used to express that something has reached its absolute limit. It is slightly more formal than 爆baau3
explosively or 死sei2
extremely, but still highly common in spoken Cantonese. You will frequently hear it used directly after an adjective (e.g. 好hou2
good 極gik6
extremely) or paired with the structural particle 到dou3
to the point of as 到極dou3gik6
to the limit.

  • Structure: [Adjective] + 極gik6
    extremely / to the limit     OR [Adjective] + 到極dou3gik6
    to the limit
呢個ni1go3
this one 蛋撻daan6taat1
egg tart 好hou2
good 極gik6
extremely 呀aa3
particle！
This egg tart is extremely good!
佢keoi5
he / she / it 嬲nau1
angry 到極dou3gik6
extremely / to the absolute limit 呀aa3
particle！
He is extremely angry (to the absolute limit)!

2. Excessive Intensity: 得滯dak1zai6
too / excessively

When a state is not just extreme but has crossed the line into being "too much" or "excessive", Cantonese speakers use 得滯dak1zai6
too / excessively after the adjective.

  • Structure: [Adjective] + 得滯dak1zai6
    too / excessively     + aa3 / laa1
呢ni1
this 杯bui1
cup 咖啡gaa3fe1
coffee 熱jit6
hot 得滯dak1zai6
too 呀aa3
particle。
This cup of coffee is too hot.
呢ni1
this 件gin6
measure word for clothes, matters, and items 衫saam1
clothes 大daai6
big 得滯dak1zai6
too 啦laa1
particle！
This piece of clothing is too big!
NOTE

Do not confuse 得滯dak1zai6
too / excessively with 咁gam3
so. 咁gam3
so is used to describe a state (e.g. 咁gam3
so 貴gwai3
expensive), while 得滯dak1zai6
too / excessively explicitly marks a negative excess (e.g. 貴gwai3
expensive 得滯dak1zai6
too).

3. Pre-Adjective Intensifiers & Superlatives: 鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
extremely / awfully and 最zeoi3
most / superlative prefix

While post-adjective markers are popular, Cantonese also possesses powerful pre-adjective intensifiers that completely change the rhythm of the sentence.

1. 鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
extremely / awfully (Awfully Cold, Awfully Cheap)

鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
extremely / awfully is a highly colloquial, expressive pre-adjective intensifier. It translates literally to something like "ghost-dead-so", and is used to emphasize that a condition is striking or overwhelming. It is frequently shortened to 鬼咁gwai2gam3
extremely or 死咁sei2gam3
extremely.

  • Structure: 鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
    extremely / awfully     + [Adjective]
呢度ni1dou6
here 嘅ge3
possessive particle 蛋撻daan6taat1
egg tart 鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
extremely 貴gwai3
expensive！
The egg tarts here are extremely expensive!
今日gam1jat6
today 鬼死咁gwai2sei2gam3
extremely 凍dung3
cold 呀aa3
particle！
Today is awfully cold!

2. 最zeoi3
most / superlative prefix (The Best, The Prettiest)

To make superlative statements (e.g., "the most expensive", "the best"), Cantonese uses 最zeoi3
most directly before the adjective.

  • Structure: 最zeoi3
    most / superlative prefix     + [Adjective] + 嘅ge3
    possessive particle
我ngo5
I 想soeng2
want to 食sik6
to eat 最zeoi3
most 好hou2
good 嘅ge3
possessive particle 嘢食je5sik6
food。
I want to eat the best food.
呢個ni1go3
this one 係hai6
to be 最zeoi3
most 靚leng3
pretty 嘅ge3
possessive particle！
This one is the prettiest!

4. Quantifiers: 所有so2jau5
all / every and 全部cyun4bou6
all / entirely

To express "all" in Cantonese, you must choose between two distinct words depending on their grammatical role:

1. 所有so2jau5
all / every (Adjective)

所有so2jau5
all / every is an adjective and must be placed before the noun it modifies. It does not require a classifier between itself and the noun.

  • Structure: 所有so2jau5
    all / every     + [Noun] + 都dou1
    also / both / all     + [Verb/Adjective]
所有so2jau5
all 嘢je5
thing / stuff 都dou1
also 搞掂gaau2dim6
done 咗zo2
completed aspect！
All things have been finished!

2. 全部cyun4bou6
all / entirely (Adverb/Pronoun or Pre-noun)

全部cyun4bou6
all / entirely can act as an adverb/pronoun placed after the subject and before the verb to state that the entire set of things is acted upon. It can also be used directly before a noun (e.g., 全部cyun4bou6
all 蛋撻daan6taat1
egg tart) acting similarly to 所有so2jau5
all.

  • Structure: [Subject] + 全部cyun4bou6
    all / entirely     + 都dou1
    also / both / all     + [Verb]
我ngo5
I 全部cyun4bou6
all 食sik6
to eat 咗zo2
completed aspect 啦laa1
particle！
I ate all of them!

Practice Exercise

Which of the following correctly describes the difference between 所有so2jau5
all / every and 全部cyun4bou6
all / entirely?
A) 所有so2jau5
all is an adjective placed before a noun, whereas 全部cyun4bou6

B) 所有so2jau5

C) 所有so2jau5

Answer: A

所有so2jau5
