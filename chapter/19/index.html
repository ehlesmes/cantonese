Chapter 19

Dating, Relationships & Social Media Slang

Colloquial romance, social media slang, and texting habits.

1. Modern Romance & Student Relationship Codes

Navigating the world of romance and dating in Hong Kong involves a unique blend of traditional colloquial Cantonese and highly specific modern student slang.

Spoken Romance Basics

To talk about relationships, spoken Cantonese uses several high-frequency verbs:

  • 拍拖paak3to1
    to date / go steady    : The standard spoken verb for dating someone.
  • 單身daan1san1
    single    : To be single.
  • 分手fan1sau2
    to break up    : To break up with a partner.
  • 表白biu2baak6
    to confess one's love    : To make a romantic confession to someone.

Split-Verb Relationship Structures

Many romance verbs like 拍拖paak3to1
to date and 分手fan1sau2
to break up are separable verbs (Verb-Object pairs). When adding an aspect marker like 咗zo2
completed aspect, it must go between the two characters, not at the end:

  • 拍拖paak3to1
    to date     -> 拍咗拖paak3 zo2 to1
    dated / have been dating
  • 分手fan1sau2
    to break up     -> 分咗手fan1 zo2 sau2
    broke up / have broken up
我哋ngo5dei6
we 已經ji5ging1
already 分咗手fan1 zo2 sau2
broke up 啦laa1
particle。
We have already broken up.

The University Relationship Status Codes

In Hong Kong university culture, students have developed a famous alphanumeric coding system to describe their relationship statuses. The status is represented by a letter indicating their status, followed by a number indicating the number of romantic relationships they have experienced:

  • A (Available): Single.
  • O (Occupied): Currently in a relationship.
  • C (Complicated): In a complicated relationship status.

Combined with a number, you get unique slang terms:

  • A0ei1ling4
    single and never dated    : Single and has never been in a relationship (a very common student self-descriptor).
  • O1ou1jat1
    in a relationship for the first time    : In a relationship for the first time in their life.

When someone successfully transitions from being single to being in a relationship, they use a highly popular student slang expression:

  • 出ceot1
    to exit / go out / leave     Poolpu1
    singles pool (student slang)    : Literally meaning "leaving the singles pool" (getting into a relationship).

2. The Dynamics of Flirting & "Soldiering"

Cantonese pop culture and student dynamics have created distinct humorous terms for crushes and unrequited admiration.

Crushes & Ideals

  • 暗戀am3 lyun2
    to crush on / love secretly    : To have a secret crush on someone.
  • 女神neoi5san4
    goddess / ideal woman    : An ideal woman or female crush.
  • 男神naam4san4
    male god / ideal man    : An ideal man or male crush.

The Concept of "Soldiering" (收兵sau1bing1
to collect platonic admirers)

One of the most unique local slang metaphors is:

  • 收兵sau1bing1
    to collect platonic admirers (literally 'collect soldiers')    : The act of keeping platonic admirers around to do favors, buy gifts, or drive them around, without ever intending to date them.
  • 兵bing1
    platonic admirer who is led on (literally 'soldier')    : A platonic admirer who is led on and does favors for their crush (the "goddess") in the hope of eventually dating them.

3. Social Media & Messaging Culture

When texting or browsing social media, Cantonese speakers seamlessly integrate English loanwords and spoken contractions.

Social Media Actions

  • Popou1
    to post (social media loanword)     相soeng2
    photo / picture    : To post photos.
  • 呃aak1
    to cheat / trick / fish     likelaaik1
    likes (social media)    : Literally meaning "cheating/fishing for likes" (posting attention-seeking photos or updates to harvest likes).
  • 些牙se1aa4
    to share (loanword)    : To share a post or link (a direct phonetic loanword from English "share").
  • 科勞fo1lou4
    to follow (loanword)    : To follow someone on social media (phonetic loanword from "follow").
  • 船syun4
    boat / ship     Ppi1
    party (loanword)    : A yacht party or boat party, highly popular for social media posting in the summer.

Messaging Etiquette

  • 覆fuk1
    to reply / write back    : To reply to a message.
  • 已讀不回ji5duk6bat1wui4
    read but not reply / ghost    : To read a message and not reply (ghosting someone).
  • 潛水cim4seoi2
    to lurk / go offline / go silent    : Literally "diving underwater" (going completely silent or offline in group chats).

4. Public Displays of Affection & Infidelity

  • 放閃fong3sim2
    to show public displays of affection / show PDA    : Literally "releasing a flash" (showing public displays of affection or bragging about relationships on social media).
  • 閃sim2
    flashy / showing PDA    : Flashy, sweet, or showing PDA.
  • 閃盲sim2maang4
    to be blinded by PDA    : Literally "blinded by the flash" (being blinded by other people's sweet relationship posts).
  • 甜tim4
    sweet / romantic    : Sweet or romantic.
  • 心碎sam1seoi3
    heartbroken    : Heartbroken.
  • 出軌ceot1gwai2
    to cheat / have an affair / infidelity    : To cheat or have an affair.

6. Exercises

Test your understanding of romance terminology, student slang, and social media expressions in Cantonese.

Practice Exercise

Which status code represents a student who has never been in a romantic relationship before?
A) O1ou1jat1
in a relationship for the first time
B) A0ei1ling4
single and never dated
C) C1si1jat1
complicated first relationship
D) A2ei1ji6
single after two relationships

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: B

A0ei1ling4
single and never dated represents a student who is Available (single) and has experienced zero (0) relationships. O1ou1jat1
in a relationship for the first time represents Occupied (in a relationship) for the first time.
