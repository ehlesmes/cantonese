Chapter 3

Identity & Simple Questions

Learn negation, A-not-A question structures, and asking about names and language skills in Cantonese.

Now that you know how to greet people and make basic introductions, we will learn how to negate statements, ask yes-no questions using the classic A-not-A structure, and inquire about people's names and language skills.

1. Negation & A-not-A Questions

Negating with 唔m4
not

To negate 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes, place the negative adverb 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) directly before it, forming 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes ("is not / are not"):

我ngo5
I / me 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 美國mei5gwok3
United States / America 人jan4
person / people / human。
I am not American.
佢keoi5
he / she / it 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix) 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes 靚仔leng3zai2
handsome boy / handsome man / handsome。
He is not handsome.

The particle 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly is frequently added to negative statements and declarations to soften the tone. For example, stating 我ngo5

not 識sik1
to know 講gong2
to speak 廣東話gwong2dung1waa2

Asking Questions with 係唔係hai6 m4 hai6
is it or not

In Cantonese, the most natural way to ask yes-no questions is the A-not-A construction, where you present the verb, the negative marker 唔m4
not / no (negative prefix), and the verb again. For the verb 係hai6
to be (am/is/are) / yes, this forms the compound expression 係唔係hai6 m4 hai6
is it or not? / is that right? (A-not-A question form).

你nei5
you 係唔係hai6 m4 hai6
is it or not? / is that right? (A-not-A question form) 英國jing1gwok3
United Kingdom / Britain 人jan4
person / people / human 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Are you a British person?
TIP

Notice that we add the friendly softening particle 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly to the end of the question. This softens the inquiry so it sounds like a warm, polite question rather than a cold interrogation.

2. Asking for Names & Languages

Asking "What is your name?"

To ask someone's name, we use the verb 叫giu3
to be called / to call, the pronoun 咩嘢me1je5
what / what kind of thing, and the noun 名meng2
name:

你nei5
you 叫giu3
to be called / to call 咩嘢me1je5
what / what kind of thing 名meng2
name 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
What is your name?

To answer, simply state: 我ngo5
I / me 叫giu3
to be called / to call [Name]. For example:

我ngo5
I / me 叫giu3
to be called / to call David。
My name is David.

Speaking Languages with 識sik1
to know

To express that you know how to speak a language, use the verb 識sik1
to know / speak (a language) / have a skill (which indicates a learned ability) paired with 講gong2
to speak / say / talk:

我ngo5
I / me 識sik1
to know / speak (a language) / have a skill 講gong2
to speak / say / talk 廣東話gwong2dung1waa2
Cantonese language 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly！
I know how to speak Cantonese!

To ask someone if they speak a language, apply the A-not-A pattern to the verb 識sik1
to know / speak (a language) / have a skill, creating 識唔識sik1 m4 sik1
know how to / know or not (A-not-A question form):

你nei5
you 識唔識sik1 m4 sik1
know how to / know or not (A-not-A question form) 講gong2
to speak / say / talk 英文jing1man4
English language 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
Do you know how to speak English?
TIP

Bounce-back Questions with 呢ne1
question particle To casually bounce a question back to someone (like saying "How about you?"), attach the query particle 呢ne1
sentence-final particle for queries (how about / what about) to a pronoun. For example: 你nei5
you 呢ne1
sentence-final particle for queries (how about / what about)？means "How about you?".
