Chapter 13

Native Idioms & Cultural Humor

Discover colorful Cantonese colloquial idioms and expressions like ceoi1seoi2 and caau2jau4jyu2. Learn how they behave structurally and grammatically as separable verbs.

In this chapter, we will explore some of the most widely used and colorful Cantonese Native Idioms (俗語zuk1jyu5
colloquial expression / slang / idiom).

Many of these idioms are structurally split-verbs, which means they follow the exact same grammatical rules of separation we learned in the previous chapter.

Let's dive in!

1. Spoken Cantonese Native Idioms

Many of the most popular colloquial expressions used by Hong Kongers in daily life are structurally split-verbs. Let's study five of the most expressive idioms:

1. Chatting & Gossiping: 吹水ceoi1seoi2
to chat / shoot the breeze / gossip

  • Literal meaning: "Blowing water"
  • Colloquial usage: Chattering, hanging out, or gossiping casually.
  • Splitting example:
我哋ngo5dei6
we / us 喺hai2
at / in / on 茶餐廳caa4caan1teng1
Hong Kong style café / diner 吹ceoi1
to blow / boast 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 水seoi2
water 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
We are shooting the breeze at the Hong Kong style café.

2. Pretending or Showing Off: 扮嘢baan6je5
to pretend / show off / act

  • Literal meaning: "Dressing up things"
  • Colloquial usage: Pretending, showing off, playing dumb, or acting like someone you're not.
  • Splitting example:
佢keoi5
he / she / it 真係zan1hai6
really / truly 扮baan6
to dress up / act / pretend 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 嘢je5
thing / stuff / matter 囉lo1
sentence-final particle showing obviousness, resignation, or 'of course'！
He is really just pretending / showing off, obviously!

3. Standing Someone Up: 放飛機fong3fei1gei1
to stand someone up / break a promise

  • Literal meaning: "Releasing an airplane"
  • Colloquial usage: Standing someone up, failing to show up to an agreed meeting or appointment.
  • Splitting example:
你nei5
you 今日gam1jat6
today 又jau6
again 放fong3
to release / let go / put 我ngo5
I / me 飛機fei1gei1
airplane 喇喎laa3wo3
sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state！
Hey, you stood me up again today! (Friendly or direct warning!)

4. Sitting Back and Watching Drama: 食花生sik6faa1sang1
to sit back and watch drama / spectate

  • Literal meaning: "Eating peanuts"
  • Colloquial usage: Staying out of a conflict or dramatic situation, just sitting back to enjoy the drama unfold (like eating popcorn at a movie).
  • Splitting example:
我ngo5
I / me 喺hai2
at / in / on 隔籬gaak3lei4
next to / beside / neighbor 食sik6
to eat 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 花生faa1sang1
peanut 啫ze1
sentence-final particle meaning 'only', 'just', or downplaying something。
I am just sitting back eating peanuts (watching the drama unfold next to me).

5. Getting Fired: 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
to get fired / lay off / fire someone

  • Literal meaning: "Stir-frying squid" (historically, when employees were fired from shops in Hong Kong, they had to pack up their sleeping mats, which rolled up like a curled, stir-fried squid).
  • Colloquial usage: Getting fired or firing someone.
  • Splitting example: When you want to say "the boss fired him," you split the verb 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
    to get fired / lay off / fire someone     and insert the pronoun object (佢keoi5
    he / she / it    ) in the middle:
老闆lou5baan2
boss / proprietor 炒caau2
to stir-fry / speculate / fire 咗zo2
verbal aspect suffix indicating completed action (perfective aspect) 佢keoi5
he / she / it 魷魚jau4jyu2
squid 喇喎laa3wo3
sentence-final particle combination indicating warning, sudden realization, or change of state！
Whoa, the boss fired him already!

6. Four-Character Idioms & Color Terms

Cantonese also uses 四sei3

characters 俗語zuk1jyu5
idiomatic expressions and color-based idioms to express vivid imagery:

  • 人山人海jan4saan1jan4hoi2
    crowded / huge crowds of people     (Mountains of people, seas of people): Used to describe a very crowded place.
  • 黑面hak1min6
    to have a dark expression / angry face     (Black face): To look visibly angry or annoyed.

Mong Kok (place in Hong Kong) 真係zan1hai6
really / truly 人山人海jan4saan1jan4hoi2

Mong Kok is truly packed with people!

boss / proprietor 今日gam1jat6
today 黑hak1
black / dark / bad luck 緊gan2
verbal aspect suffix indicating an action in progress (continuous aspect) 面min6
