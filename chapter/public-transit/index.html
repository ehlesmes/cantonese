Chapter 14

Public Transit Modes & Payments

Learn how to navigate Hong Kong's public transit system (MTR, buses, trams), and pay using Octopus cards.

In this chapter, we will learn how to navigate the public transit network of Hong Kong, including the MTR, buses, historical trams, and Octopus card payments.

Let's dive in!

1. Public Transit Modes

Cantonese speakers use a mixture of traditional characters and colloquial English loanwords to describe transport options. The standard spoken verb for taking any transit mode is 搭車daap3ce1
to take transit / ride a vehicle. If you need to transfer between lines or change vehicles, the term is 轉車zyun2ce1
to transfer (vehicles).

Here are the primary modes of public transit:

  • 地鐵dei6tit3
    MTR / subway    : The mass transit railway system in Hong Kong.
  • 的士dik1si2
    taxi    : The phonetic loanword for taxi.
  • 巴士baa1si2
    bus    : The phonetic loanword for bus.
  • 紅hung4
    red     VANman1
    minibus (loanword)    : The iconic red minibuses, which have flexible routes and schedules.
  • 綠luk6
    green     VANman1
    minibus (loanword)    : The green minibuses, which run on fixed schedules and routes.
  • 船syun4
    ferry / boat     / 天星小輪tin1sing1siu2leon4
    Star Ferry    : Ferries, including the iconic Star Ferry.
  • 電車din6ce1
    tram     / 叮叮ding1ding1
    tram (colloquial)    : The historic double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island.

Transit Locations & Payments

To locate stops or pay for your fare, use these terms:

  • 站zaam6
    station / stop    : The generic term for station or stop.
  • 地鐵站dei6tit3zaam6
    MTR station / subway station    : MTR subway station.
  • 巴士站baa1si2zaam6
    bus stop    : Bus stop.
  • 的士站dik1si2zaam6
    taxi stand    : Taxi stand.
  • 八達通baat3daat6tung1
    Octopus card    : The ubiquitous local contactless smart card. To use it, you 嘟dut1
    to tap a card / scan / beep    .

3. Exercises

Review the transit modes and vocabulary introduced in this chapter:

Practice Exercise

Which transit mode is colloquially named after the sound of its bell ("ding-ding")?
A) 地鐵dei6tit3
MTR
B) 巴士baa1si2
bus
C) 叮叮ding1ding1
tram
D) 的士dik1si2
taxi

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: C

The Hong Kong double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island are colloquially known as 叮叮ding1ding1
tram (colloquial) due to the double-ringing bell sound they make to warn pedestrians.
