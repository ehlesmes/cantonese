Chapter 20

Travel, Directions & Transport Slang

Colloquial transport terms, directions, and minibus etiquette.

1. Navigating Hong Kong's Transport Network

Getting around Hong Kong requires understanding the core vocabulary of daily transit. The standard spoken verb for taking any transit mode is 搭車daap3ce1
to take transit / ride a vehicle. If you need to transfer between lines or change vehicles, the term is 轉車zyun2ce1
to transfer (vehicles).

Public Transit Modes

Cantonese speakers use a mixture of traditional characters and colloquial English loanwords to describe transport options:

  • 地鐵dei6tit3
    MTR / subway    : The mass transit railway system in Hong Kong.
  • 的士dik1si2
    taxi    : The phonetic loanword for taxi.
  • 巴士baa1si2
    bus    : The phonetic loanword for bus.
  • 紅hung4
    red     VANman1
    minibus (loanword)    : The iconic red minibuses, which have flexible routes and schedules.
  • 綠luk6
    green     VANman1
    minibus (loanword)    : The green minibuses, which run on fixed schedules and routes.
  • 船syun4
    ferry / boat     / 天星小輪tin1sing1siu2leon4
    Star Ferry    : Ferries, including the iconic Star Ferry.
  • 電車din6ce1
    tram     / 叮叮ding1ding1
    tram (colloquial)    : The historic double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island.

Transit Locations & Payments

To locate stops or pay for your fare, use these terms:

  • 站zaam6
    station / stop    : The generic term for station or stop.
  • 地鐵站dei6tit3zaam6
    MTR station    : MTR subway station.
  • 巴士站baa1si2zaam6
    bus stop    : Bus stop.
  • 的士站dik1si2zaam6
    taxi stand    : Taxi stand.
  • 八達通baat3daat6tung1
    Octopus card    : The ubiquitous local contactless smart card. To use it, you 嘟dut1
    to tap a card / scan    .

2. Minibus Shouting Etiquette

Riding a minibus (紅hung4
red VANman1
minibus (loanword) or 綠luk6
green VANman1
minibus (loanword)) is a quintessential Hong Kong experience. Unlike double-decker buses, minibuses do not have stop buttons or automated announcements. Instead, passengers must shout out to the 司機si1gei1
driver when they want to request a stop.

The Stop Request Template

To request a stop, use this formula:

  • 唔該m4goi1
    please     + Landmark/Location + 有落jau5lok6
    stop here please

Common Stop Request Expressions

Here are the most common phrases passengers shout on a minibus:

  • 唔該m4goi1
    please     前邊cin4bin1
    ahead     有落jau5lok6
    stop here please    : Stop up ahead, please.
  • 唔該m4goi1
    please     路口lou6hau2
    intersection     有落jau5lok6
    stop here please    : Stop at the intersection, please.
  • 唔該m4goi1
    please     燈位dang1wai2
    traffic light     有落jau5lok6
    stop here please    : Stop at the traffic light, please.
  • 唔該m4goi1
    please     地鐵站dei6tit3zaam6
    MTR station     有落jau5lok6
    stop here please    : Stop at the MTR station, please.

To get on a vehicle, use 上車soeng5ce1
to get on a vehicle, and to get off, use 落車lok6ce1
to get off a vehicle.

3. Asking for & Giving Directions

When exploring the city or directing a driver, spoken Cantonese relies on simple directional commands and road features.

Directional Commands

  • 直行zik6haang4
    to go straight    : Go straight ahead.
  • 轉左zyun2zo2
    to turn left    : Turn left.
  • 轉右zyun2jau6
    to turn right    : Turn right.
  • 過馬路gwo3maa5lou6
    to cross the road    : Cross the street.

Street Landmarks & Infrastructure

  • 前邊cin4bin1
    ahead / in front / up ahead    : Ahead or in front.
  • 路口lou6hau2
    intersection / junction    : An intersection or street corner.
  • 燈位dang1wai2
    traffic light / intersection with lights    : Traffic lights.
  • 天橋tin1kiu4
    pedestrian flyover / footbridge    : A pedestrian bridge over the street.
  • 隧道seoi6dou6
    tunnel    : An underpass or tunnel.

Time & Delay States

If you are rushing or caught in traffic, these terms are essential:

  • 行路haang4lou6
    to walk    : Traveling on foot.
  • 趕時間gon2si4gaan3
    in a hurry / rushing for time    : Being in a hurry.
  • 遲到ci4dou3
    to arrive late    : Arriving late.
  • 等dang2
    to wait    : Waiting.
  • 幾耐gei2noi6
    how long (time)    : Asking about the duration of time.
  • 塞車sak1ce1
    traffic jam / heavy traffic    : Being stuck in a traffic jam.

Practice Exercise

Which of the following is the iconic Cantonese phrase shouted to minibus drivers to request a stop?
A) 唔該m4goi1
please 轉車zyun2ce1
to transfer
B) 唔該m4goi1

please 塞車sak1ce1

D) 唔該m4goi1

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: B

唔該m4goi1
please 有落jau5lok6
stop here please is the standard colloquial phrase used to request a stop on Hong Kong minibuses.
