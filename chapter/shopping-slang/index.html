Chapter 5

Authentic Transactions & Daily Slang

Master payment methods, plastic bags, change, and essential everyday slang and loanwords in Cantonese.

In this lesson, we will focus on authentic transactions—specifically paying for your goods using contactless cards or cash, getting change, and utilizing daily slang and loanwords.

Let's dive in!

1. Authentic Hong Kong Transactions

When buying something at a local store, you will encounter terms for local payment methods, change, and convenience items.

Payment Methods: Octopus & Cash

Hong Kong is famous for its contactless smart card, the 八達通baat3daat6tung1
Octopus card (Hong Kong contact-less smart card). Alternatively, you can use 現金jin6gam1
cash.

To ask if a store accepts a payment method, use the verb 收sau1

Plastic Bags & Change

At the register, the clerk will often ask if you need a 膠袋gaau1doi2

2. Daily Slang & Loanwords

The Ultimate Slang: 搞掂gaau2dim6
done / finished / settled / resolved

One of the most essential Cantonese colloquial words is 搞掂gaau2dim6
done / finished / settled / resolved. It is an incredibly versatile adjective/expression used to declare that a task is finished, a problem is solved, or a transaction is successfully completed.

  • When you finish paying: 搞掂gaau2dim6
    done / finished / settled / resolved    ！ (Done! / All settled!)
  • When a task is complete: 搞掂gaau2dim6
    done / finished / settled / resolved     啦laa1
    sentence-final particle suggesting, requesting, or encouraging action    ！ (Sorted!)

English Loanwords

Spoken Cantonese naturally absorbs English words, adapting their pronunciation to fit Cantonese phonetics. Here are three crucial loanwords:

  • 的士dik1si2

NOTE

唔該m4goi1

excuse me / please / thank you (for service) to thank someone for a service or help (like a clerk handing you an item). Use 多謝do1ze6
thank you (for a gift, compliment, or hospitality) when receiving a gift, paying, or in a retail context where the cashier thanks you for your business.

The A-not-A question form 收唔收sau1 m4 sau1
do you accept / accept or not (A-not-A question form) (from the verb 收sau1
to receive / accept / collect, meaning 'to accept/receive') is the most natural way to ask if a shop accepts a particular payment method.
