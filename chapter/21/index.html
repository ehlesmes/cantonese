Chapter 21

Housing, Flat-Hunting & Neighborhood Slang

Spoken terms for flat-hunting, layouts, and neighborhood life.

1. Types of Housing in Hong Kong

Hong Kong’s high-density urban environment has led to unique residential structures. When navigating the housing market, it is essential to distinguish between traditional buildings and modern developments:

  • 唐樓tong4lau2
    traditional low-rise walk-up building    : These are older low-rise buildings (typically 5 to 8 stories) constructed without a 升降機sing1gong3gei1
    elevator / lift    . They are famous for their high ceilings and historical value, though climbing them can be physically demanding.
  • 洋樓joeng4lau2
    modern high-rise building with elevator    : These are modern mid-to-high-rise residential buildings equipped with a 升降機sing1gong3gei1
    elevator / lift     and usually have a security guard at the lobby entrance.
  • 套房tou3fong2
    studio flat / ensuite bedroom    : A studio apartment or a single self-contained bedroom containing its own private bathroom, and sometimes a small kitchenette.
  • 劏房tong1fong2
    subdivided flat / partitioned room    : A flat that has been partitioned or split into multiple tiny individual units. These are highly compact and common in older urban areas.
  • 公屋gung1uk1
    public housing    : Public housing, which houses a massive portion of the population.
  • 屋uk1
    house / room    : A general term for a house or room.
  • 樓lau2
    building / flat / apartment / floor    : A generic term that can mean a building, a flat, an apartment, or a floor.

2. Flat-Hunting & Rental Terminology

The process of finding and securing a place to live involves specific terminology:

  • 搵樓wan2lau2
    to look for apartments / flat-hunting    : The general action of flat-hunting or searching for an apartment.
  • 睇樓tai2lau2
    to view apartments / flats    : Going to view apartments or flats in person with an agent or landlord.
  • 租zou1
    to rent / lease    : The verb meaning to rent or to lease a property.
  • 租客zou1haak3
    tenant / renter    : The tenant or renter who leases the property.
  • 業主jip6zyu2
    landlord / property owner    : The property owner or landlord.
  • 租金zou1gam1
    rent payment / rental price    : The monthly rental price or payment.
  • 按金on3gam1
    deposit    : The security deposit held by the landlord.
  • 上期soeng5kei4
    rent paid in advance (first month rent)    : The first month of rent paid in advance.
  • 兩按一上loeng5on3jat1soeng5
    two months deposit and one month rent in advance    : The standard Hong Kong upfront rental payment scheme consisting of two months of security deposit and one month of advance rent.
  • 合約hap6joek3
    contract / agreement    : The formal lease contract or agreement.
  • 佣金jung2gam1
    commission fee    : The agency commission fee, usually split between the tenant and landlord.
  • 屋苑uk1jyun2
    housing estate / complex    : A housing estate or residential complex.
  • 管理費gun2lei5fai3
    management fee    : The building management fee.

3. Apartment Layouts & Neighborhood Life

To describe your living space and neighborhood, use these vocabulary words:

  • 客廳haak3ting1
    living room / parlor    : The main living or sitting room.
  • 睡房seoi6fong2
    bedroom    : The bedroom.
  • 面積min6zik1
    area / size    : The total size or area of a flat.
  • 實用面積sat6jung6 min6zik1
    saleable area / net floor area    : The net saleable area of the apartment (excluding common areas and thick walls).
  • 呎cek3
    square foot / feet    : The local standard unit of area measurement, shorthand for square feet.

Example Layout Description:

  • 間gaan1
    measure word for rooms     屋uk1
    house/flat     有jau5
    has     兩loeng5

    saleable area     五百ng5baak3
    five hundred     呎cek3
    square feet    : The flat has two bedrooms and one living room, with a saleable area of five hundred square feet.
  • 環境waan4ging2
    environment / surroundings    : The local surroundings or environmental quality of a neighborhood.
  • 方便fong1bin6
    convenient    : Describing high ease of access to transit, shops, or facilities.
  • 周圍zau1wai4
    around / surroundings    : The surrounding area around the building.
  • 嘈cou4
    noisy    : Noisy or loud surroundings.
  • 靜zing6
    quiet / peaceful    : Quiet, calm, or peaceful surroundings.
  • 好住hou2zyu6
    nice to live in / comfortable to reside    : Comfortable, cozy, and highly pleasant to reside in. This uses the grammatical structure of 好hou2
    good     + Verb (住zyu6
    to live    ) to create an adjective meaning "good to [verb]" or "easy to [verb]".

      this       屋苑uk1jyun2
      housing estate       真係zan1hai6
      really       好住hou2zyu6
      nice to live in      ！: This housing estate is really nice to live in!
  • 附近fu6gan6
    nearby / neighborhood    : Nearby or adjacent to a location.
  • 最好zeoi3hou2
    best / preferably    : Best or preferably.

Practice Exercise

Which of the following describes a traditional low-rise walk-up building that does not have an elevator?
A) 洋樓joeng4lau2
modern high-rise building with elevator
B) 唐樓tong4lau2
traditional low-rise walk-up building
C) 劏房tong1fong2
subdivided flat / partitioned room
D) 套房tou3fong2

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Answer: B

唐樓tong4lau2
traditional low-rise walk-up building refers specifically to older, low-rise walk-up buildings in Hong Kong without elevator access.
