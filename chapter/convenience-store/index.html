Chapter 4

Convenience Store Purchases

Learn to express wants and needs, point out items, ask for prices, and count dollars in Cantonese at a convenience store.

Now that you know how to introduce yourself and strike up a basic conversation, it is time to venture out into the vibrant streets of Hong Kong.

In this lesson, we will focus on everyday transactions—specifically inside the ubiquitous local convenience stores (like 7-Eleven and Circle K). You will learn how to express wants and needs, ask how much things cost, and count spoken dollars using Cantonese numerals.

Let's dive in!

1. Wanting & Needing: 想soeng2
to want to / would like to / think vs 要jiu3
to want / need / must / demand

To request items or express desires, Cantonese uses two main auxiliary verbs. Understanding the pragmatic difference between them helps you sound polite and natural:

  • 想soeng2
    to want to / would like to / think    : Equivalent to "would like to" or "want to". It is soft, polite, and expresses an internal desire or intention.
  • 要jiu3
    to want / need / must / demand    : Equivalent to "need" or a stronger, more assertive "want". It implies necessity or an immediate, direct request.

Basic Sentence Structure

To ask for or request something, place either auxiliary verb directly before the verb 買maai5
to buy or use 要jiu3
to want / need / must / demand directly with a noun:

我ngo5
I / me 想soeng2
to want to / would like to / think 買maai5
to buy 嘢飲je5jam2
drinks / beverages 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly。
I would like to buy some drinks.
我ngo5
I / me 要jiu3
to want / need / must / demand 呢個ni1go3
this one。
I need this one.
TIP

When you are at a counter ordering or buying something, using a combination like 我ngo5
I 想soeng2
to want to / would like to / think 要jiu3
to want / need / must / demand is an extremely common, polite, and natural way to make requests.

2. Pointing Things Out & Connecting Items: 呢個ni1go3
this one, 嗰個go2go3
that one, 咁樣gam2joeng2
like this / in this way / this way, and 同埋tung4maai4
and / with / together with

If you do not know the name of an object in a shop, you can simply point and use these high-frequency demonstrative pronouns:

  • 呢個ni1go3
    this one    : Used for items close to you.
  • 嗰個go2go3
    that one    : Used for items further away.
  • 咁樣gam2joeng2
    like this / in this way / this way    : Used when describing choices, indicating "like this" or "this way".

To connect two nouns together, like saying "and" in English, use the conjunction 同埋tung4maai4
and / with / together with.

Observe how these are used to make simple requests:

3. Asking for Prices & Counting Dollars

Asking "How much?"

To ask how much an item costs, we use 幾多gei2do1
how many / how much and 錢cin2
money. Together, 幾多gei2do1
how many / how much 錢cin2
money is the standard phrase for "How much is it?":

呢個ni1go3
this one 幾多gei2do1
how many / how much 錢cin2
money 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
How much is this one?
嗰個go2go3
that one 幾多gei2do1
how many / how much 錢cin2
money 呀aa3
sentence-final particle softening statements, making questions friendly？
How much is that one?

The Spoken Dollar: 蚊man1
dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)

In formal written Chinese, currency is represented by words like jyun or faai. However, in spoken Cantonese, you must use the colloquial classifier 蚊man1
dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency) to mean "dollars".

To state a price, use the pattern: Number + 蚊man1
dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)

Cantonese Numerals (1 to 10)

Here are the basic numerals you will use to count dollars and specify quantities:

  • 一jat1
    one    : One
  • 二ji6
    two    : Two (used in compound numbers like twelve or twenty)
  • 三saam1
    three    : Three
  • 四sei3
    four    : Four
  • 五ng5
    five    : Five
  • 六luk6
    six    : Six
  • 七cat1
    seven    : Seven
  • 八baat3
    eight    : Eight
  • 九gau2
    nine    : Nine
  • 十sap6
    ten    : Ten
IMPORTANT

The 二ji6
two vs 兩loeng5
two / pair (used with classifiers) Rule: When counting items or stating prices with a classifier like 蚊man1
dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency), you must use 兩loeng5
two / pair (used with classifiers) instead of 二ji6
two.

  • Correct: 兩loeng5
    two / pair (used with classifiers)     蚊man1
    dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)
  • Incorrect: 二ji6
    two     蚊man1
    dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)

Combined Prices

You can easily combine tens and units just like in English:

  • 十sap6
    ten     五ng5
    five     蚊man1
    dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)     = fifteen dollars
  • 二十ji6sap6
    twenty     三saam1
    three     蚊man1
    dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)     = twenty-three dollars
  • 三十saam1sap6
    thirty     二ji6
    two     蚊man1
    dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)     = thirty-two dollars

Let's look at examples:

this one 四sei3

dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)。
This one is four dollars.
嗰個go2go3
that one 二十ji6sap6
twenty 蚊man1
dollar (spoken Cantonese colloquial unit of currency)。
That one is twenty dollars.
