Chapter 22

Entertainment, Nightlife & Leisure Slang

Spoken terms for socializing, nightlife, and weekend hobbies.

1. Socializing & Night-Out Habits

Hong Kong's nightlife and social scene is incredibly vibrant. To describe activities like going to bars, singing karaoke, or hanging out late at night, Cantonese speakers use these highly colloquial terms:

  • 唱coeng3
    to sing    K: An extremely popular pastime. The letter "K" is a shorthand loanword for karaoke (pronounced coeng3 kei1).
  • 卡拉kaa1laa1
    karaoke    OK: The full standard term for karaoke (pronounced kaa1 laa1 ou1 kei1).
  • 蒲pou4
    to hang out / go clubbing    : A classic slang term meaning to hang out or go clubbing, typically in nightlife hotspots.
  • 夜蒲je6pou4
    night clubbing / nightlife / to club at night    : To go clubbing or participate in late-night entertainment.
  • 酒吧zau2baa1
    bar / pub    : A bar, pub, or lounge.
  • 飲酒jam2zau2
    to drink alcohol / have a drink    : To drink alcohol or have a social drink.
  • 劈酒pek3zau2
    to drink heavily / drink competitively    : To down drinks, engage in heavy drinking, or play drinking games at a bar.
  • 醉zeoi3
    drunk    : Intoxicated or drunk.
  • 斷片dyun6pin2
    to blackout / memory lapse from drinking    : To blackout or suffer a memory lapse from excessive drinking.

2. Gathering Plans & Shorthand

Making plans and managing social gatherings comes with its own local slang, from arranging get-togethers to handling friends who cancel last minute:

  • 局guk6
    gathering / social event / plan    : A gathering, social plan, or get-together (e.g., a drinking or dinner plan).
  • 直落zik6lok6
    to continue onto another venue / next round without stopping    : To continue to another venue or the next round of a night out without stopping.
  • 下場haa6coeng4
    next venue / afterparty / next round    : The next venue, round, or afterparty of the night (literally "next scene").
  • 放飛機fong3fei1gei1
    to stand someone up / break a promise    : To stand someone up, cancel plans last minute, or fail to show up.
  • 飛機王fei1gei1wong4
    chronic flake / person who always stands others up    : A chronic flake; someone who habitually stands people up.
  • 派對paai3deoi3
    party    : A party or festive social gathering.
  • 吹水ceoi1seoi2
    to chat / shoot the breeze / gossip    : To chat, shoot the breeze, or gossip with friends.
  • 潛水cim4seoi2
    to go offline / go silent    : To go offline, disappear from chat groups, or go silent (literally "to dive/snorkel").

3. Weekend & Indoor Leisure

For those who prefer a more relaxed weekend, outdoor activities, or indoor entertainment, these terms cover everyday hobbies:

  • 打機daa2gei1
    to play video or computer games    : To play video, computer, or mobile games.
  • 睇戲tai2hei3
    to watch a movie    : To watch a movie at a cinema or home.
  • 聽歌teng1go1
    to listen to music    : To listen to music.
  • 睇書tai2syu1
    to read books    : To read books.
  • 影相jing2soeng2
    to take photos    : To take photos or snapshots.
  • 打卡daa2kaa1
    to check-in / take photos at trendy spots    : To check-in or take photos at a popular or trendy spot to share on social media.
  • 行山haang4saan1
    to go hiking / climb mountains    : To go hiking or mountain climbing.
  • 野餐je5caan1
    to go on a picnic / picnic    : To go on a picnic or have a picnic.
  • 做運動zou6wan6dung6
    to exercise / do sports    : To exercise, work out, or do sports.
  • 睇波tai2bo1
    to watch ball games (e.g. soccer)    : To watch a sports match, particularly a soccer or basketball game (literally "to watch ball").
  • 好玩hou2waan2
    fun / enjoyable    : Entertaining, fun, or highly enjoyable.
  • 悶mun6
    boring / bored    : Dull, boring, or feeling bored.
  • 介紹gaai3siu6
    to introduce / recommend    : To introduce, present, or recommend something.
