Chapter 16

Workplace Slang & Office Dynamics

Master the essential colloquial terms, slang, and expressions used in Hong Kong professional settings, from slacking off to getting a raise.

Welcome to Chapter 16! In this lesson, we will explore the lively and colorful world of Cantonese workplace slang and office dynamics. The Hong Kong professional environment is famous for its fast pace, high efficiency, and intense work ethic. To cope with this pressure, local workers have developed a highly expressive vocabulary of slang, English loanwords, and vivid metaphors.

Understanding these terms is not only essential for anyone working in or with a Hong Kong company, but it also provides a unique window into local culture, humor, and social dynamics.

We will focus on three main areas:

  1. The Philosophy of "Making a Living": Understanding the cultural significance of 搵食wan2sik6
    to make a living    , and mastering daily office routines using standard terms like 公司gung1si1
    company    , 返工faan1gung1
    to go to work    , and 放工fong3gung1
    to finish work    .
  2. Slacking Off at the Office: Learning local expressions for relaxing, procrastinating, and slacking off under the boss's radar using 摸魚mo2jyu2
    to slack off    , 蛇王se4wong4
    to slack off / avoid work    , and Heahea1
    to chill / slack off    .
  3. Overtime, Salaries, and Firing: Expressing working overtime using OTo1ti1
    to work overtime    , discussing salaries with 人工jan4gung1
    salary    , requesting a raise using 加人工gaa1jan4gung1
    to get a raise    , and describing getting fired using the famous metaphor 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
    to get fired    .

Mastering these terms will make you sound like an authentic Hong Kong professional and help you participate in daily office gossip and lunch chats.

1. Making a Living: 搵食wan2sik6
to make a living

In Cantonese culture, work is closely tied to the concept of food. The most common colloquial way to say "making a living" or "earning a livelihood" is 搵食wan2sik6
to make a living, which literally translates to "seeking food." When someone asks you why you work so hard, a standard self-deprecating reply is that it is all for 搵食wan2sik6
to make a living.

Core Office Routines

To talk about daily routines at the 公司gung1si1
company, you will use several high-frequency verbs:

  • 返工faan1gung1
    to go to work    : Literally "returning to work."
  • 放工fong3gung1
    to finish work    : Literally "releasing work," meaning getting off shift or leaving the office for the day.
  • 同事tung4si6
    colleague    : The standard term for coworkers.

Let's look at how to put these together in natural conversation:

2. Slacking Off at the Office: 摸魚mo2jyu2
to slack off, 蛇王se4wong4
to slack off / avoid work, and Heahea1
to chill / slack off

Every office culture has its ways of slacking off, and Cantonese is incredibly rich in terms describing these activities. If you are taking a break or procrastinating, you are likely doing one of the following:

1. 摸魚mo2jyu2
to slack off (Feeling for Fish)

Derived from the Chinese idiom meaning "to catch fish in muddy waters," 摸魚mo2jyu2
to slack off refers to slacking off on the job, performing personal tasks during work hours, or browsing the internet instead of doing actual work.

佢keoi5
he / she / it 成日seng4jat6
always 喺hai2
at 公司gung1si1
company 摸魚mo2jyu2
to slack off。
He always slacks off at the company.

2. 蛇王se4wong4
to slack off / avoid work (Snake King)

A highly colloquial Cantonese term, 蛇王se4wong4
to slack off / avoid work literally means "Snake King." In the workplace, it is used as a verb meaning to dodge work, sneak out for a long tea break, or avoid assignments. A person who is a serial slacker is often called a "snake."

老細lou5sai3
boss 唔m4
not 喺度hai2dou6
here， 我哋ngo5dei6
we 一齊jat1cai4
together 蛇王se4wong4

The boss is not here, let's slack off together!

3. Heahea1
to chill / slack off (To Procrastinate / Chill)

Heahea1
to chill / slack off is perhaps the most famous modern Cantonese slang term. It has no standard Chinese character and is written in Roman alphabet. It is incredibly versatile and can mean to hang out, chill, do things half-heartedly, or loiter around.

佢keoi5
he / she / it 今日gam1jat6
today 唔m4
not 想soeng2
want to 做嘢zou6je5
to work， 只係zi2hai6
only 想soeng2
want to Heahea1
to chill。
He doesn't want to work today; he only wants to chill.

3. Overtime, Salaries, and Career Moves: OTo1ti1
to work overtime, 人工jan4gung1
salary, 加人工gaa1jan4gung1
to get a raise, and 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
to get fired

To talk about the professional realities of compensation and job transitions, Cantonese uses several colorful and loanword-based terms.

1. Working Overtime: OTo1ti1
to work overtime

In Hong Kong's notoriously high-intensity work environment, working overtime is extremely common. Cantonese speakers simply use the English initials "OT" as a loanword. It can function as both a verb ("to OT") and a noun ("doing OT").

我ngo5
I 今日gam1jat6
today 要jiu3
need OTo1ti1
to work overtime 呀aa3
particle！
I have to work overtime today!

2. Salaries and Raises: 人工jan4gung1
salary and 加人工gaa1jan4gung1
to get a raise

The word for salary is 人工jan4gung1
salary, which literally means "human labor" or "manual work." When you want to request a pay raise, you use the verb 加人工gaa1jan4gung1
to get a raise, which combines 加gaa1
to add / increase with 人工jan4gung1
salary.

老細lou5sai3
boss 幫bong1
for 我ngo5
me 加人工gaa1jan4gung1
to get a raise 啦laa1
particle！
The boss gave me a raise! (Or: The boss got me a raise!)

3. Getting Fired: 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
to get fired

One of the most famous food-related slang terms in Cantonese is 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
to get fired, which literally means "to stir-fry squid."

This term dates back to old Hong Kong when migrant workers lived at their workplaces and slept on rolled-up mats. When they were dismissed, they had to roll up their mats and pack their belongings. Since squid curls up when stir-fried, the rolling up of the sleeping mat was likened to a curled-up fried squid, and the term stuck as a metaphor for getting dismissed.

佢keoi5
he / she / it 因為jan1wai6
because 喺hai2
at 公司gung1si1
company 蛇王se4wong4
to slack off / avoid work， 俾bei2
by / to give 老細lou5sai3
boss 炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
to get fired 咗zo2
completed aspect 啦laa1
particle！
Because he slacked off at the company, he was fired by the boss!

4. Resigning and Job Hopping: 辭職ci4zik1
to resign and 跳槽tiu3cou4
to job hop

When an employee decides to leave on their own terms, they 辭職ci4zik1
to resign. If they leave to join another company (often for better pay), they 跳槽tiu3cou4
to job hop (literally "jumping troughs").

I will resign tomorrow.
佢keoi5

to job hop 去heoi3
to go to 咗zo2
completed aspect 第間dai6gaan1
another 公司gung1si1
company。
He job hopped to another company.

"I have to work overtime today!"

need OTo1ti1

In Cantonese, working overtime is represented by the English loanword initials OTo1ti1
to work overtime. The auxiliary verb 要jiu3
need / have to goes before OT.

Practice Exercise

Answer: 蛇王se4wong4

蛇王se4wong4

Practice Exercise

Which of the following Cantonese terms literally means "to stir-fry squid" but colloquially refers to getting fired?
A) 摸魚mo2jyu2
to slack off
B) 蛇王se4wong4
to slack off / avoid work

Answer: C

炒魷魚caau2jau4jyu2
